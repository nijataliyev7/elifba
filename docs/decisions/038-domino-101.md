## Context

A room has seven games: six races of rounds (ADRs 021–023) and draughts, a board game of two seats (ADR 034). Domino is neither, in three ways no room game has been so far:

1. **It hides things.** Each seat holds tiles nobody else may see, and the boneyard is seen by nobody. Until now "nothing in a match is secret" (ADR 034 decision 17) and a race's one secret, the solution, is the same for everyone and ends with the round.
2. **Two to four seats**, each for itself or two against two, where a match has exactly two.
3. **Many hands.** A game is a row of deals played to 101, with an account kept between them — the «13» rule, «gözləmə», the bank — where a match is one position played to its end.

The room already has the sentence for it, from the roadmap: the phone is the hand, the screen is the table.

The study (v2 §8) says what the app does well — one rule that gives the game its tension («13-lə hesab açılır»), a sheet at a hand's end, playable tiles lit, a bot in an empty or abandoned seat — what a room can do better — the table on a wall and the tiles large in the hand, a sheet that opens by itself and says why, nothing to tap where the rules leave no choice — and what a room never takes (decision 61).

ADR 034 promised one machinery: a game is a `room_games` row, its facts are `room_acts` rows written only by `RecordRoomAct`, everything else is derived by replay, and the kind of a game is read in one place. This record keeps the promise for a game that hides things.

## Decision

### A third kind

1. **A game of domino is a `room_games` row, of a third kind.** `Content\Enums\RoomGameKind` gains `domino`; `RoomFamily` gains `domino` («Domino») and `RoomFamily::kind()` gives it; `RoomMechanic` gains `domino_101` («101»). It is one game of the room in every sense a race or a match is: one runs at a time, by `position`; `StartRoomGame` starts it from the room's choice and `EndRoomGame` ends it; it is the state's `game`; it is listed in «Keçmiş oyunlar» and a closed room's results; its taunts are `room_taunts` rows; it counts in `RoomRanking::ofRoom()`; `PruneOldRooms`, `CloseRoom` and `DeleteRoom` reach it through the tables they know. **Why not a wider `board` kind:** a board game's shape — two colours, a clock a side, one position every device gets whole, `white` and `black` in the choice and the state — is what a match **is** on every device today; widening it to seats, sides and a hidden part would change draughts' state and its pages, which this record must not. **Why not a kind per game:** «Telefon 365» is the same tiles, deal, seats, turns and boneyard with other rules of scoring (decision 60), so the kind is `domino` and the game is its mechanic.
2. **One dispatch point, a third driver.** `Rooms\Support\Drivers\RoomGameDriver::of($mechanic)` returns the `RaceDriver`, the `BoardDriver` or the `DominoDriver`; nothing else in `Rooms` asks for the kind, compares a mechanic with a name or tests a driver's class (ADR 034 decision 3; `RoomsApartTest`). Which rules a domino game plays by — today always «101» — is asked in one place too, inside `Support/Drivers`: `DominoDriver` gives the game's `DominoVariant`.

    | Concern                          | `RaceDriver`                         | `BoardDriver`                                  | `DominoDriver`                                                                                        |
    | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | the choice                       | a level and a number of rounds       | a level — its clock — two seats, a computer    | a level — a turn's time — a table, its seats and the computer's level (decisions 11–14)               |
    | the start                        | builds and stores the rounds         | seats the two, copies the clock                | seats the table, copies the turn's time and the pause, draws the seed; refuses a table nobody sits at |
    | when the game is over            | `RoomTimetable`                      | the outcome of `DraughtsMatch`                 | a side at 101 in `DominoGame`, or the host's end                                                      |
    | its closes and what each earned  | every round's close; answers' points | one close, its end; the outcome's points       | one close, its end; `MATCH_WIN` to each player of the winning side, `DRY_WIN` after a dry win         |
    | a taunt: who, at whom, from what | any present player at any other      | a seat at the other seat; `MATCH_WIN`          | a seat's player at a player of another side; `MATCH_WIN`                                              |
    | the state's part                 | `round`                              | `match`                                        | `domino`, as its viewer may see it                                                                    |
    | an act                           | none                                 | `move`, `offer`, `accept`, `decline`, `resign` | `play`, `yield`, `back`                                                                               |

    The data classes that hold a shape by kind — `RoomGameSetupData`, `RoomSetupData`, `RoomGameData`, `RoomStateData` — gain the third shape in their constructors.

3. **What the shared machinery gains is signatures, never behaviour.** `part()` also returns `domino` (null for the other two kinds). `computerTurn()` reads "the server is to play this turn, and from when"; `computerChoice()` gives the act to store — its kind and its `path` — where it gave a path, and `PlayComputerMove` stores what it is given (decision 28). `RoomActKind` gains `play`, `yield` and `back`; `RoomActData` knows their form (decision 16). `RecordRoomAct`, `StartRoomGame`, `EndRoomGame`, `ChooseRoomGame`, `ThrowRoomTaunt`, `RoomRanking`, `RoomState` and the two controllers ask the driver as they do today. Every test of a race and of a match passes unchanged but for `v` and `domino: null` in the state.
4. **Names.** In code the game is `domino` and «101» is `domino_101`; the interface says «Domino» for the family and «101» for the game. The pure classes live in `Rooms\Support\Domino`:

### Hidden hands

5. **The deal is derived from a seed only the server holds.** A domino game's `room_games.seed` — the column a race builds its rounds from; a match stores 0 — is `RoomSeed::fresh()` at the start. `RoomSeed::deal($seed, $hand, $try)` is the source of the `$try`-th deal of hand `$hand`: keyed, like every source of a room, by the seed, those two numbers and the instance's `app.key`, so a seed read from a database without the key tells nothing. `DominoDeal` shuffles the 28 tiles with it: the first seven to seat 0, the next seven to seat 1 and so on, and the rest is the boneyard **in the order it will be drawn**. Nothing stores a hand, the boneyard or a drawn tile: they follow from the seed and the plays. The seed is never served (as today), and neither it nor a tile off the table is ever in a log, an exception, a URL, a broadcast, a page prop or a row a device reads.
6. **The state is built for its viewer, and a viewer gets its own hand only.** `DominoDriver::part()` is given the viewer's row and whether the read is the screen's (the signature ADR 037 gave it):

    | What                                                                         | A seat's player            | A watcher, a host who does not sit | The screen                                               |
    | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
    | the viewer's own tiles and what it may do with them (`mine`)                 | its own                    | `mine` is null                     | `mine` is null — though it reads with the host's account |
    | another seat's tiles                                                         | a count                    | a count                            | a count                                                  |
    | the line, its ends, how many tiles the boneyard holds, the turn, the account | all                        | all                                | all                                                      |
    | every seat's tiles left when a hand is over (`sheet.left`)                   | from the moment it is over | the same                           | the same                                                 |
    | a tile of the boneyard, the seed, anything of a deal not yet played          | never                      | never                              | never                                                    |

    The screen hangs on a wall with the host's account behind it: a host who sits reads its hand on its phone and never on the screen.

7. **Nothing tells of a hand but what the rules make public.** Public by rule, and so in every viewer's state: that a seat passed; how many tiles it drew; who opens with the lowest double (R6: that seat holds it); that the opponents had no reply (R5). Nothing else: a play the judge does not take — a tile the seat does not hold, a tile that does not fit, an end it does not fit — gets **one** answer, 422 with `rooms.domino.illegal`, so a device cannot ask whether a tile is in play; an act's receipt is `{status, revision}`; the channel carries `room.changed` `{v, revision}` and nothing more; the computer's and a stand-in's play takes the same time whatever the seat holds (decision 28).
8. **After a hand, the hands; the boneyard never.** When a hand is over every seat's tiles left are part of the count and are sent to everyone (`sheet.left`) — the sheet explains every point with them (decision 49). The boneyard is never sent. After a hand it is what is left of the 28, which tells nothing of the next deal: that is another try of the keyed source.
9. **The tests that prove it** (Y4, Y5; decision 59 has the whole list):
    - `tests/Feature/Rooms/RoomDominoSecrecyTest.php` plays scripted games through the room's own routes — four seats, a watcher, a host who does not sit and the screen, then two and three seats with a boneyard — and after **every** act reads every viewer's whole state, the `rooms/show` and `rooms/screen` props, «Keçmiş oyunlar», the act's receipt, a refused act's answer, what `FakeReverb` was sent and the log. In each it collects every tile name anywhere in the JSON and requires: for a seat's player, only tiles on the table and its own; for a watcher, the host who does not sit and the screen, only tiles on the table; in the phases `sheet` and `over`, also the tiles `sheet.left` names — and never a tile that was in the boneyard, never the string of the seed, never a tile at all in the channel or the log. The same refusal for a tile another seat holds, a tile in the boneyard and a tile already played.
    - `tests/Unit/Rooms/Domino/DominoViewTest.php`: two games that differ only in tiles a seat has not seen give that seat an equal `DominoView`, and `DominoEngine` the same choice (decision 54).
    - `RoomContractTest` and `contract.test.ts`: `mine` is null in the watcher's and the screen's fixtures; `sheet.left` in the phase `play` fails the schema.
    - `RoomsApartTest`: `DominoEngine` names neither `DominoDeal`, `DominoGame`, `DominoHand` nor `RoomSeed`.

### The table and its seats

10. **Four tables.** `Rooms\Enums\RoomTable`: `two` («2 nəfər»), `three` («3 nəfər»), `four` («4 nəfər», each for itself) and `pairs` («Para 2×2», two against two), as the app has them. Seats are numbered from 0 in the order of the turns (R3); a **side** is what scores: a seat at the first three tables, and at `pairs` seats 0 and 2 against seats 1 and 3 — partners face each other.
11. **The table and the seats are part of the choice.** `rooms.setup_table` (nullable) and `rooms.setup_seats` (nullable JSON: a list as long as the table, each place a `room_players.id` or null) are facts the host wrote, like the rest of `rooms.setup_*`: only `ChooseRoomGame` writes them, whose whole choice for a domino game is `{mechanic, difficulty, table, seats, computer}` — `seats` either absent, when the room's stand, or the whole list, each place a present player of the room named once, or null for "nobody placed here". A choice of another kind leaves them as they are, and a domino game chosen after another kind names only the game and its level and the room fills the rest (ADR 034 "As built (D5)" a1): a page never fills a choice from its own memory.
12. **Who sits is derived at each read** — `Rooms\Support\RoomTableSeats::of($room)`, from one list of the present players, as `RoomSeats` does it for a match:
    - **the table** is the stored one, else by the number of present players: `two` for fewer than three, `three` for three, `pairs` for four or more;
    - **a seat** is the stored player while present; an empty place takes the present player who joined first and has no seat; a place nobody is left for is **the computer's**;
    - **the computer's level** is `rooms.setup_computer` — the column a match's computer has — else «Orta» (`RoomSeats::ALONE`); it is one level for every computer seat of the table.

    So a room of one starts against the computer with no choosing, a room of two is a duel, three play each for themselves and four sit as two pairs; a player who joins before the start takes a computer's seat by joining; with more present players than seats the rest watch, and the host says who sits. A present player always sits before the computer does: a table with a computer and a watching player is not offered. Nothing writes any of it at a join, a leave or a read.

13. **Partners by default are not the two who came together.** At `pairs` the places fill in the order of joining, so the first two present players sit at seats 0 and 1 — opposite sides, each with the computer or the next comers as a partner. The host puts two people on one side by placing them at seats 0 and 2.
14. **The start.** `StartRoomGame`, still without a body, hands the row to the driver: `DominoDriver::start()` refuses a table with no present player to seat (`rooms.errors.needs_one`, ADR 036), and stores the table (`room_games.table`), the seats (`room_games.seats`, the same list — a null place is the computer's for the whole game), the computer's level when a seat is its (`room_games.computer`), the level's turn time (`room_games.turn_ms`), the pause between hands (`room_games.reveal_ms`, from `elifba.rooms.domino.sheet_ms`, as a race copies its own) and the seed. The seats do not turn round as a match's colours do: «Yenidən oyna» is the same people in the same places, and who opens a hand is the rules' (R6). `setup.played` is: the latest game is over, with this mechanic, level and table, the same players in the same seats and the same computer's level. The seats of a running game never change: a player who joins during it watches.

### Acts, and what happens by itself

15. **A tile and an end have one name everywhere.** A tile is its two numbers, the larger first, with a hyphen — `6-5`, `1-1`, `3-0`, `0-0` — as the app's own record of a game writes them; `DominoTile` alone turns a name into numbers and back, and a tile is that name in a stored act, the state, a fixture and the oracle's output, never an index. An end is `left` or `right` — the line's two ends as every device draws them («sol uc», «sağ uc»).
16. **A seat's acts are `room_acts` rows through `RecordRoomAct`,** on the route a match has, `POST /rooms/{room}/acts` `{event_id, game_id, kind, ply, path}` — no route, action or table is new. Three kinds are a domino game's:

    | Kind    | Body                                                                | What it is                                                                          |
    | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | `play`  | `ply`; `path`: `[tile]` for a hand's first tile, else `[tile, end]` | the seat to move puts one of its tiles at an end                                    |
    | `yield` | `ply`; no `path`                                                    | «Sən aç»: the seat that has the right to open gives the opening to its partner (R9) |
    | `back`  | neither                                                             | «Oyuna qayıt»: a seat's player who was away plays its own turns again (decision 26) |

    `ply` is the number of turn acts — plays and yields — the game holds, over all its hands; an act names the ply its device saw, and another is `late`. `path` is the one field for what an act did, in its game's own notation: a match's squares, a domino play's tile and end; `RoomActData::rules()` checks the form of both and judges neither. The order of `RecordRoomAct` is ADR 034 decision 9's: a stored `event_id` → `duplicate`; no live game, another game than the act names, or an account that is not present → `late`; a player who holds no seat → 403; then the driver — a `play` or a `yield` outside the phase `play`, out of turn, at another ply or at or after the turn's flag → `late`; a play the judge does not take → 422 (decision 7); a `yield` the seat may not make → `late`; a `back` from a seat that is not away → `late`; an act of another kind → `late` — and otherwise the row, with `acted_ms`, the room active, its revision raised and `room.changed` after the commit. A `back` is stored at the ply the game stands at and is no turn.

17. **The actor of a turn act is derived, not stored.** Whose play a row is follows from the game: it is the seat to move at that ply. `room_player_id` says only who sent it — the session's account, which must hold that seat — and is null for a play the server made (decision 28), as it is for the computer in a match. So three computer seats need no column, and nothing reads an act's player to find a side.
18. **Drawing and passing are not acts: they follow by themselves.** Under the standard rules a seat without a fitting tile has no choice — it draws until it holds one or may draw no more (R12–R13), and then plays or passes (R11, R14). So the replay does it: after every turn act `DominoRules` gives the tiles the next seat draws, in the boneyard's order, and passes it on when it still cannot play, and so round the table to a seat that can play — or to the end of a blocked hand. No tap, no row, no moment of its own: it happens at the moment of the act before it. Every device is told what happened (`last.then`: a seat, how many it drew, whether it passed) and draws it in order (decision 47). **Why:** in the app a seat must tap «Bazar» until a tile fits and then «Pas» when none does — taps with one possible outcome, on a 15-second clock — and a pass arrived unexplained with six tiles in the boneyard (study v2 §2, hand B6). A room draws the same tiles the same way and says why.
19. **Playing is never done for a seat that can choose.** A seat with one fitting tile still plays it itself: a play made at once would tell the table it had no other.
20. **Everything of a game is derived by replay, never stored.** `Rooms\Support\Domino\DominoGame::replay()` — pure: the game's start facts (the table, the seats, the seed's deals, `started_ms`, `turn_ms`, `reveal_ms`, `ended_ms`), its acts and a moment in — gives every hand's line and ends, each seat's tiles, the boneyard, the seat to move and since when, what followed each act by itself, each hand's ending and its count, the account, the bank, who opens, the outcome. No column holds a hand, a line, a turn, a score, a result or its reason. An act the server would not have taken is passed over, as `DraughtsMatch` does. A game is a few hundred acts at most; Y5 measures the state of a game of fifteen hands and «Keçmiş oyunlar» with fifty games before it lands, and a replay may be remembered within one request, never between two.
21. **The acts of a hand the app has and a room does not.** «Mən açım» is the entitled seat's own first play, so only «Sən aç» is an act. A draw is not chosen face down («bazardan çəkim: seçimlə»): the server gives the tiles, as the app's standard does. There is no resignation: a player who wants out leaves the room, and the game goes on (decision 27). There is no undo.

### Time

22. **A domino game's level is a turn's time.** `content/rooms/levels.json` (version 7) gains the levels of `domino_101`, each `{mechanic, difficulty, turn_ms}` and no other key — "how much time", as a level is defined (ADR 023 decision 2). The host picks it with the control of a difficulty (`DifficultySegments`), which shows the seconds:

    | Difficulty | A turn | In the app                                    |
    | ---------- | ------ | --------------------------------------------- |
    | 1          | 60 s   | — (for a table that is learning the game)     |
    | 2          | 30 s   | the standard («Sürətli oyun» off)             |
    | 3          | 15 s   | «Sürətli oyun — Gediş üçün 30 yox, 15 saniyə» |

    A new room takes `RoomDefaults`' difficulty, 2. `ContentValidator` requires of a domino mechanic two to four levels numbered from 1 without a gap, `turn_ms` whole seconds from 10 to 120, a shorter turn at each higher number, and no key of a race's or a board game's level. There is no clock a side: a hand is 28 tiles at most, and the game is a row of hands.

23. **A turn, on the server's clock.** The seat to move began its turn at `since_ms` — the moment of the turn act before it, or for a hand's opener the moment the hand opened — and its **flag** is `since_ms + turn_ms`. An act received at or after the flag is `late` (`RoomClock::nowMs()` under the room's lock; never anything a device says). Nothing is lost at the flag: the turn is played for the seat (decision 25).
24. **A hand opens after the pause.** The first hand opens at `started_ms` (the start plus `elifba.rooms.countdown_ms`, as a race's round 0); every later one at the end of the hand before it — the moment of its last act — plus `room_games.reveal_ms`. Until then the phase is `sheet` and every act is `late`. The phases and the hand that is open are a function of the acts and the moment, as a race's timetable is: nothing writes them. A game that reached 101 has no pause: it is over at that act.
25. **At the flag the server plays the turn for the seat — a fact, written after a response.** No job, queue or schedule exists (CLAUDE.md "No queue"), a read writes no fact within its own request, and a derived play would make every reader choose a tile. So the way of the computer's move (ADR 036 decisions 8–9) serves: `RoomActController` after an act and `RoomStateController` after a read whose state says the server is to play hand `PlayComputerMove` to `defer()`; pages of a live game ask at the flag (`phaseEnds()`), so the play follows it by a round trip. Until it is stored the turn is still that seat's and every device shows its time as run out. A room nobody reads stands still and is closed as idle rooms are.
26. **Away.** A seat is **away** while its last `elifba.rooms.domino.away_after` (2) turns were both played for it and no `back` came after them — derived from the acts. For an away seat the server plays at once (decision 28), so the table does not wait a full turn each round for a phone left on a sofa. The seat's player sees it said plainly and one button, «Oyuna qayıt», which posts `back`; a player who had left the room and comes back while its seat is away gets the `back` from `JoinRoom` through the driver's "a presence that changed". Until then the tiles are still its own on its phone.
27. **Who leaves does not end the game.** A seat whose player left the room or was removed is played for by the server from then on, at the pace of a computer's seat; the others play on, the seat keeps its place in its side, and a player who comes back holds its tiles again. Its player earns what its side earns (decision 31) — a room's rule already: who leaves keeps their points, who is removed is out of the ranking. `EndRoomGame` (the host's «Oyunu bitir») and `CloseRoom` set `ended_ms`: the game is over without a result. **Why not "who leaves, loses" (ADR 034 decision 13):** three people at a table of four should not lose their game to a fourth's battery.
28. **`PlayComputerMove` is the one writer of a play nobody's device sent.** It asks the driver whether the server is to play (`computerTurn()`: the ply, and from when) — the seat to move is a computer's, or its player is gone or away: from `since_ms + elifba.rooms.domino.think_ms`; any other seat: from its flag — holds that game and ply (`Cache::lock`), asks for the act (`computerChoice()`: a `play`, chosen by `DominoEngine` from the seat's `DominoView` — at the game's level for a computer's seat, at «Orta» for a player's), waits until it is due, and under the room row's lock stores it only if the same game still stands at that ply with the server still to play: a row without a player or an `event_id`. It only plays: it never yields and never comes back for anyone. A turn due more than 5 s ahead is left to a later read, as today.

### Points and taunts

29. **The account of a game is the game's own** — balances to 101, «gözləmə», the bank (R20–R26) — and never the room's points. It is derived, shown in the strip and on the sheet, and ends with the game.
30. **A game gives its winners 3, or 6 after a dry win.** `RoomPointsRule::MATCH_WIN` (3) to each player of the side that reached 101, and `RoomPointsRule::DRY_WIN` (6) when it is a dry win (R26) — the app counts a dry win double. Everyone else and a game without a result get nothing; there is no draw. They are the game's points at its one close, its end, and enter `RoomRanking::ofGame()` and `ofRoom()` through the driver's "what each earned", where a match's enter.
31. **A seat's player earns its side's points**, present at the end or not; a computer's seat earns nothing and stands in no ranking (ADR 036 decision 12).
32. **A taunt goes to a player of another side, and is paid from what the game gives.** The thrower is a seat's player; the target a player who holds a seat of **another** side — never a partner, never a computer's seat, never a watcher; with no such player nobody throws and the strip's rival half is no button. The cost is `TAUNT_COST` (1) of the thrower's points of this game, counted at its end and never below 0; the balance is `MATCH_WIN − TAUNT_COST ×` the taunts thrown in the game — at most three — and a throw needs 1. The window is the live game, its countdown and its pauses included. Everything else of a taunt is ADR 029's, through `ThrowRoomTaunt` and `room_taunts`; on the phone `throwChoice()` offers the players of the other sides in the order of the seats, the strip's rival first.

35. **No option is offered.** The app lets a table change eleven rules («Oyun qaydalarım»); a room plays the standard set, and the host's choice is the table, the seats, a turn's time and the computer's level. **Why:** every option is a branch of the judge, the oracle and the sheet's words, and friends who sit down together want the game they know. A rule the owner wants offered later — «tək-tək» drawing, a tie that gives nobody anything, no third-try burn — is a value of `DominoVariant` chosen with the game, and an amendment of this record.
36. **Lit tiles are not an option either.** The app's «Kömək rejimi» — playable tiles lit — is always on: the viewer's state names the tiles it may play and where (`mine.legal`), as a match names its legal paths, so what a page offers and what the server takes are one rule. It says what the rules allow, never which play is good.
37. **Not taken from the app's rules:** the win given for an abandoned game («tərk edən heç xal yazmayıbsa və qalib ən azı 76 xal yığıbsa»; decision 27 instead), the streak («seriya»), everything of rating and rank, and the entry stake (decision 61).

### The state and the protocol

38. **The state is `v: 10`, and the game is in it as its viewer may see it.** `RoomStateData::VERSION` 10, `ROOM_STATE_VERSION` 10; `game` and `setup` gain a third shape, and a domino game — live or over — has its `domino` where a race has its `round` and a match its `match`:

41. **A game's moments** are the game, the ply and the phase (`roomMoment()`: `{game, round: ply, phase}`), so `sawBegin()`, `momentKey()` and `sameMoment()` serve as they are: a play is a moment, and so is the sheet that follows the last play of a hand.

### The pages

44. **One state, the parts a game has.** `rooms/show` in `RoomFrame` and `rooms/screen` follow the one `useLiveRoom()`; nothing fetches a domino game by itself, and no hook of it keeps a clock or a sound player. The functions that look at the kind gain a third case each — `roomMoment()`, `phaseEnds()`, `waitsForOthers()`, `roomCues()`, `stripRows()`, `finalRows()`, `GAME_VIEWS`, `choiceParts()` — and no component compares a mechanic with a name. New parts live in `components/rooms/domino/`. A page never computes a legal play, a draw, a pass, a count of pips, a line of the account, a place or a result: it draws `line`, offers `mine.legal`, counts `flag_ms` down for show and posts an act. docs/frontend.md rule 15 "Domino" has the layout, the sizes and the words.
45. **The phone is the hand, the screen is the table — and a phone alone is enough.** A seat's phone shows its own tiles large at the bottom and the table small above them; the screen shows the table large and no hand. A room without a screen plays whole on phones (ADR 028 as amended 2026-11-19), so the phone's table is the same part drawn smaller, never a different one.
46. **A tile is played with one tap where it fits one end.** While `mine.legal` is there, a tile it names is lit and a tap on one that fits one end posts the play; a tile that fits both ends lifts and the phone asks «Sol uca» or «Sağ uca» with two buttons, the two ends marked on the table; «Geri» puts it back. A tile `legal` does not name does nothing. The app asks for two taps every time (study v2 §6); a match posts a move when its path is whole, and so does this.
47. **What happened by itself is shown, in order, and said in words.** A play the page saw arrive is drawn first, then `last.then` a step at a time: a seat drawing («Bazar :count×» by its name, the boneyard's count going down, and on the drawer's own phone the tiles `mine.drawn` arriving), a seat passing («PAS» with `Flag` by its name). The viewer's own are sentences in the line under the table: «Uyğun daşınız yox idi — bazardan :count daş götürdünüz», «Uyğun daşınız yoxdur — pas». Why the boneyard is shut is said where its count stands: «Son 2 daş çəkilmir», «Uclara yalnız qoşa düşə bilər — bazar bağlıdır». A turn the server played for a player says so on every device: «Vaxt bitdi — :name üçün kompüter oynadı».
48. **The strip is the account.** `RivalryStrip` stands above the table from the countdown to the end. `stripRows(state)` gives a domino game's rows from `domino.sides`: at `two` and `pairs` the two sides — a seat's phone shows its own on the left as «Biz» and the other as «Onlar» (at `two`, «Siz» and the other's name), a watcher's phone and the screen the side of seat 0 on the left, each pair by its two names; at `three` and `four` the viewer against the leader, or the runner-up when leading, and on the screen the first two — picked by `rivalPair()` from the server's order, never re-sorted. The totals are the balances, the target «101» stands between the two names, 👑 is by the higher balance, and under a side whose account is closed its state stands in words with an icon: «Hesab bağlıdır» with `Lock`, or «Gözləmədə :count» with `Hourglass` and its tries («2/3»). It moves only at a hand's end the page saw arrive — a balance changes nowhere else — and a change of the lead between two pairs the page drew is an overtake, with its ribbon. The sides are sky and orange (ADR 027). The rival's half is the taunt's button for a seat's player with someone to aim at (decision 32).
49. **The sheet opens by itself and explains every point.** In the phase `sheet` — and under the final in `over` — every phone and the screen show the hand that ended, with no tap: how it ended and by whom («Əli :name bitirdi», «Əl bağlandı», «Əl bərabər bağlandı», «Cavab yoxdur — daşlar yenidən paylanır»); every seat's tiles left, face up, with their count — and «0-0 tək qalıb: 10 xal» where R16 made it so; each side's count and, in a blocked hand, why that side won («Az xal qalan tərəf udur: 24 < 36»); then every line of the account in the order it happened (`sheet.lines`), each a sentence — «+24 rəqibin əlindən → hesaba yazıldı», «+8 → gözləməyə (13-dən az)», «Hesab açıldı: +20 və gözləmədəki 12», «Rəqibin gözləmədəki 8 xalı yandı», «Üçüncü dəfə 13 tutulmadı: gözləmədəki 11 xal yandı», «Bərabərlik: 24 xal banka getdi», «Bank alındı: +24», «Bank yandı: 24» —; the balances after it and «101-ə qədər: :count»; who opens the next hand and why («Növbəti əli :name açır: əli o bitirdi», «… ən az xal onda qaldı», «… əli o bağladı», «Növbəti əli ən kiçik qoşası olan açır»); and a bar that runs out at `until_ms`. A page writes none of the numbers: they are the state's. The app's sheet waits for a tap from a watcher and explains neither why a blocked hand was won nor who opens next (study v2 §4, §8).
50. **The final is the game's.** When a game is over with a winner, `FinalStanding` opens `result.standing` — the winning side's players first, each with the points the game gave it, the others placed by their side's balance — over the game's own score («101 : 43», and «Quru qələbə» as words with an icon after a dry win), the last hand's sheet, and the room's standing as the plain card. The rows come from `finalRows(state)`; the confetti, the hits, the roll and the fanfare follow `finalTimeline()` unchanged. The cheer (ADR 030) names the first place — a pair's two first names, three at most as ever — and then «Əla oynadınız!»; a computer is never named, and a final only computers won has the fanfare and no name. A game the host stopped shows «Oyun dayandırıldı» and no final. «Hazıram» between games is ADR 035's, as after a race or a match — the call rises after the final's show — and nothing is asked between hands.
51. **Whose turn it is** is one pure function for a domino game, as `yourTurn()` is for a match — the phase is `play` and the viewer holds the seat to move — and only that seat's phone acts on it: the band in the left side's colour («Sizin növbənizdir»), its own time ringed, its turn's sound (decision 53), one `navigator.vibrate(50)` under its mute, the tab's title «● Sizin növbənizdir», and a wake lock on every seat's phone while its game is live. ADR 025's "no vibration" has this one exception in a match; in a domino game it is the same one.
52. **Motion follows ADR 025.** A page animates only a moment it saw begin: a play it saw arrive puts its tile at its end; a draw brings tiles from the boneyard; a hand it saw open deals; the sheet rises when the hand it saw end ends. A page opened on a position, a state more than one ply ahead and every state fetched again are drawn still. Nothing moves differently for a seat that is about to win. Everything stands still under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The confetti is the final's alone. How the line turns at the table's edge is drawn by one pure function of the line and the box it is given — the same on a phone and the screen — and which of Y3's turnings it draws is the owner's choice.

### The computer

54. **The computer decides from what its seat has seen, and from nothing else.** `DominoEngine::choose()` takes a `DominoView` and a random source, and returns one of the plays the judge lists for that seat. A `DominoView` is built by `DominoGame` for one seat and holds: that seat's own tiles; the line and its ends; how many tiles each other seat holds and how many the boneyard does; every play, draw and pass of the hand, by seat; the account. It has no field for another seat's tile, for the boneyard's order or for the seed, and the engine is given no game, hand or deal — so "the computer does not peek" is a property of a type, held by `DominoViewTest` and `RoomsApartTest` (decision 9), and not a promise of the search.
55. **Three levels, the computer's own** — `Rooms\Enums\RoomComputerLevel` as ADR 036 has it, «Asan», «Orta», «Çətin»; never the game's difficulty, which is a turn's time. What each does (the numbers are Y5's, in `elifba.rooms.computer.domino`, each a **count**, never a time, so the same view, level and source give the same play on every machine):

    | Level | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
    | ----- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | 1     | one of its legal plays at random                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
    | 2     | sheds the most pips, a double before another tile of the same pips, and keeps the numbers it holds most of open                                                                                                                  |
    | 3     | deals the tiles it has not seen to the other seats and the boneyard in a fixed number of ways that agree with every pass and draw it saw, plays each of its plays out to the hand's end in each, and takes the best for its side |

    They are the app's three in spirit («təsadüfi», «ən xallı gediş», «bir gediş irəli baxır»; study v2 §3). A seat played for its player (decisions 25–27) is played at «Orta».

56. **Its random source is the game's**, `RoomSeed::computer($game, $ply)` (ADR 036 decision 7): a play computed twice is the same play. It only plays (decision 28): it never yields the opening, and nothing is thrown at it.
57. **On a page** a seat without a player is «Kompüter (:level)» with `Bot`, as in a match; with several, each row is keyed by its seat (`seatKey()` gains the seat), and the choice's line says the level once: «101 · 30 san · Para 2×2 · Kompüter (Orta)».

## What is not built

- A replay of a finished game («Oyuna bax»), a list of the plays, an export: the acts and the seed are all kept, so one may be built later; v1 shows a finished game's score.
- A hint, an analysis, a count of tiles played kept for the player, a mark on a weak play.
- A resignation, a pause, an undo, time added by a player, a second boneyard draw by choice («Əlində ola-ola bazar»), tiles picked face down.
- More than four seats, a table of three against one, a tournament of several tables in one room: one game runs at a time and the rest of the room watches.
- An opening draw for the double (decision 34), a deal thrown away shown on the table.
- Arranging one's own tiles by hand: a hand stands in the order it was dealt and drawn.
- «Telefon 365» (Y9–Y11), and any other domino game.

## Consequences

- A room plays races, matches and domino in any order, and its standing adds them up: a race can give 200, a match 3, a domino game 3 or 6 to each winner. ADR 034's remedy stands if that is wrong in play: a standing per kind is a change of the page, not of the facts.
- For the first time a device is not told everything the server knows. Every later change of the state's `domino`, of a page prop or of a log line near a game must pass `RoomDominoSecrecyTest`, which reads whole payloads rather than named fields for that reason.
- People who sit in one room, or talk over the live voice, can tell a partner their tiles. Nothing prevents it, as nothing does at a real table; a room is friends, and the app's rule against it is not ours to enforce.
- A turn the server plays costs a PHP process the search and the wait before it, after a response (ADR 036's consequence); a table of one player and three computers makes three such plays a round.
- A seat that is out of time gets its play a round trip after the flag, when some page asks. A room in which every phone sleeps stands still until one wakes — and a game cannot be lost while nobody looks.
- `room_acts.path` and the act's `path` are no longer a match's squares alone: a reader of acts asks the driver what a row is.
- The readings of decision 34 may be wrong about the local game. They are few, named, and each a single case; a game is never judged twice.

## Alternatives rejected

- **A wider `board` kind** (decision 1): it would change what draughts is on every device to make room for a game that is not like it.
- **A table of its own for hands, deals or scores:** a second truth beside the acts. A stored hand is also the thing most worth stealing; a deal that exists only as a keyed function of a seed is in no row to leak.
- **The deal sent to the device and hidden by the page:** a hidden hand that is in the payload is not hidden.
- **A second state endpoint for "my hand":** two things to fetch and hold together; the state is already built per viewer (ADR 037), so one field of it is the viewer's own.
- **Draw and pass as acts the seat taps** (the app): taps with one possible outcome, each a round trip on the turn's clock, and two more ways for a play to be `late`.
- **Playing a seat's only fitting tile for it:** it would tell the table the seat had no choice.
- **A timeout derived at each read** — every reader choosing the tile a timed-out seat "played": a search inside every replay, and a game nobody reads playing itself to its end.
- **A pass or a random tile at the flag:** a pass where a tile fits breaks R11; a random tile punishes the partner. A stand-in at «Orta» is what the app's bot in an abandoned seat is.
- **"Who leaves, loses" for a table of four,** and a seat given to another player in mid-game: the first ends three people's game for one; the second shows a newcomer a hand it did not hold.
- **A `room_players` row for a computer,** or one per seat: ADR 036 decision 1, three times over.
- **A level per computer seat:** three more controls on a phone for a difference nobody asked for.
- **The app's options, all or some** (decision 35).
- **A rules library, or the app itself as the oracle:** none exists for these rules; and nothing is ever sent to, read from at run time or automated against the app — the study watched it and wrote down its rules.
- **Points for the room's standing by the margin of the game** (a 101 : 0 worth more than a 101 : 99 beyond the dry win): a second scale beside the match's 3.
- **A voice for a pass, a count or a winner's side:** the board's voice enters a room in two places (ADR 030, ADR 029), and this is neither.

## What would make us revisit

A hand of the local game that contradicts a reading; an owner's wish for an option (question 1); games that drag because of the pause or the turn's time (two numbers); a table that wants to watch a finished game again (the acts are there); a third domino game, or «Telefon 365» needing more of the core than decision 60 kept general; a room's standing in which domino games drown.

## Amended 2026-11-26 (Y4) — where the text was silent

The rules R1–R26 and the eleven readings do not change, and no hand played under them would be judged otherwise.

62. **The first tile lies as its name is written:** the larger number at the left end and the smaller at the right (`6-5`: `left` 6, `right` 5), so a later `left` or `right` of the hand names the same end on every device. The line grows from it at either end, and `first` follows it.
63. **R8 (b)'s tie-break counts from the last player itself.** Of several seats with the same fewest pips — one of the winning side first (decision 34) — the first in the order of the turns starting **at** the seat that made the hand's last play, that seat first when it is one of them.
64. **R8 (d): after a void hand, the seat that had the right.** A void hand opened under R8 gives the right to open again to the seat that had it — before any «Sən aç», so a partner who was given the opening does not keep it — with `again`, and that seat may give it again (R9). A void hand opened under R6 or R7 leaves the opener to the next deal, as it was for the void hand (R5).
65. **A void hand's sheet counts nothing and shows everything.** `left` and `counts` are every seat's tiles left and each side's count by R16, as facts of the tiles; `winner` is null, `gain` 0, and the account writes no line.
66. **`sheet.next` is null when the game is over** — on its last hand and on a game the host stopped in a pause: no hand follows, so nobody opens it, and the final shows the last hand's sheet without «Növbəti əli …». While the game goes on it is as decision 38 has it, and `{seat: null, why: lowest_double}` while the next deal decides — at every table, though at `two` and `three` R7 may decide when the deal holds no double, which the next hand's `hand.why` then says (`lowest_tile`): a sheet tells nothing of a deal not yet played.
67. **R12 draws a pair whole.** A seat without a fitting tile takes two tiles — one when three are left — and looks at its tiles again only after the pair; it plays after the pair even when the pair's first tile fits.
68. **`last.then` is each seat that drew or passed after the act, in the order of the turns from the actor.** The seat that moves next is in it only when it drew (`passed` false). After a play that leaves nobody able to play, every seat's pass is there, the actor's own last. A play that finishes the hand or makes it void, and a «Sən aç», have none.
69. **R13 is the boneyard's.** At `two` and `three` the boneyard is closed while the line says so (decision 34's reading), whatever it holds — down to its last two as well. At `four` and `pairs`, which have no boneyard (R2), nothing is closed and `boneyard.closed` is false: a seat passes there because there is nothing to draw (R14 names it beside R13), and «Uclara yalnız qoşa düşə bilər — bazar bağlıdır» would speak of a boneyard that is not there.
70. **The lines of the account are in the order it changed.** A won hand writes the winner's own line — `gained`; or `opened`, then `released` with the waiting points when any waited; or `waited`, then at the third try `burnt_third` with the waiting points, the third gain among them —, then the bank's — `bank_taken` or `bank_burnt` —, then `burnt_rival` for each closed side that had points waiting, in the order of the sides. A tied hand writes `bank_added`; a void hand writes none. A line's `side` is the winner's for its own lines and `bank_taken`, the burnt side's for `burnt_rival`, and null for `bank_added` and `bank_burnt`: the bank is nobody's. An account's `tries` go back to 0 when it opens, and stay there.
71. **Hands and tries are numbered as the seed is keyed:** hands from 1 and tries from 0 (`RoomSeed::deal($seed, $hand, $try)`). A deal R4 throws is the next try of the same hand and never a hand; a void hand is a hand of its own — it has a number and a sheet —, and the deal after it is the next hand's first try.
72. **Away counts the turns of the whole game.** A seat's last two turns (decision 26) may lie in two hands — the pause between them is no `back` —, and a `back` is taken in any phase of a live game, its countdown and its pauses included.

## As built (Y5) — the computer

Decision 55 left the numbers to Y5 and said what each level does, not how in every case. Where it did not say, Y5 took the following under the owner's standing rule of 2026-11-18; none changes a rule of «101», the state or what a page shows.

- **«Orta»** takes the most pips; of the same pips a double; then the play after which most of its other tiles show a number left open (each of the two ends counted); then the tiles' one order (`DominoTile::all()`), the left end first.
