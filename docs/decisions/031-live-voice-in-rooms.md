## Context

Rooms were built so that nobody could reach anybody: a name among people who share a code, and nothing else ([024](024-adults-privacy-in-rooms.md)). The owner asked on 2026-11-18 that players be able to talk while they play — friends in different homes on their phones — "at a fully professional level", and decided the frame: a server of our own (no cloud, no phone to phone), off until the host turns it on, each player's own microphone, the host mutes anyone, never recorded.

What this meets:

- **No age is asked** (024 decision 7): a 15–18 year old may be in a room, and an account that guesses a six-digit code joins it (020). Until now such a stranger was a name in a list. With voice, a stranger could listen to a private conversation and speak into it, so "seen in the lobby and removed" is no longer enough by itself.
- **No third-party request** (024 decision 11, frontend rule 9). The usual way to add voice — a cloud service, or WebRTC with a public STUN server — is one.
- **The race** (021): the server alone keeps the clock, and nothing is queued. A voice server that is slow or down must never move a round.
- **One host** (§10): Caddy holds the only published ports, and the firewall allows 22, 80 and 443.
- **Nothing recorded** means Elifba can never review what was said. The only moderator is the host, in the room, at that moment. That is the price of the promise, and it is stated on `/privacy`.

## Decision

### The voice server and what leaves a phone

1. _(Its moment amended 2026-11-19, decision 40: the library is imported when the page first joins the voice — by itself for an admitted player whose account agreed, or on the tap.)_ **Our own voice server, and only it.** A self-hosted LiveKit server, pinned to `v1.13.11`, is one more service of the instance (`voice`, decision 27). A phone's audio goes to it and from it to the other phones of that room: never to a cloud service, and never from phone to phone, so no player learns another's IP address. The library in the page is `livekit-client`, pinned to an exact version in `package.json`, bundled by Vite as its own chunk and imported only by `resources/js/rooms/voice.ts`, only when a player taps «Səsə qoşul». No CDN, no worker or model fetched from elsewhere, no Krisp.
2. **No public STUN server, on either side.** Three settings, each with its proof:
    - the voice server is told its address — `rtc.node_ip` from `ELIFBA_ROOMS_VOICE_IP`, `rtc.use_external_ip: false` — so it asks nobody (C2: the stack starts healthy with no route out and logs the explicit address);
    - `rtc.stun_servers` names only the instance's own address, since an empty list would mean Twilio's and Google's (C2: the config check of `scripts/docker-smoke.sh`);
    - the page never uses the server's list: it always connects with `rtcConfig: { iceServers: [] }` (C4: Vitest `voice.test.ts` "connects with no ICE server, its own or the server's"). A phone needs none: the voice server has a public address and answers on it.

    C5 proves all three at once: in a browser driven over the DevTools protocol every `RTCPeerConnection` was built with an empty list and every request went to the app's own host, and a packet capture on the host shows no packet to UDP 3478 or 19302 and no lookup of a STUN name while two phones join.

3. **The voice server calls nobody.** No webhook (`webhook.urls` unset), no telemetry, no tracing, no Redis, no egress, ingress, SIP or agent service. It answers the browser's WebSocket and the app's API calls, and sends media to the phones that joined.
4. **Transport: UDP first, TCP as the fallback, no TURN in the first release.** A phone sends media to the instance's address on UDP 7882 and, where a network blocks UDP, over TCP on 7881. TURN over TLS on 443 is not built: it would replace the pinned official Caddy image — the only door to the whole app — with a custom build carrying an experimental layer-4 plugin, and need a second hostname. A network that allows nothing but 80 and 443 therefore gets no voice; the page says so and the game plays on (decision 26). C5 counts how often that happens.
5. **Two connections, two jobs.** The room channel is untouched: `room.changed` `{v: 1, revision}` and nothing else, no client event. A page in the voice holds a second connection, to the voice server, which carries audio and the voice server's own signalling — who is connected, whose microphone is open, who is speaking. It carries no game fact, the page never sends a data message (`canPublishData: false`), and nothing of a round reads it: not the clock, not `RoomWaits`, not a point. What the voice connection says is drawn and forgotten.

### Who may be in the voice

6. **A voice participant is `room_players.id` and nothing else.** That ULID is the ticket's `sub`. A ticket has no `name`, `metadata` or `attributes`; the voice server never learns a name, an e-mail address or an account id, and a page names a speaker from the state's `players` by that id, as it names everyone.
7. **The stored facts** (migration `add_voice_to_rooms`, additive, nullable; the columns exist whether or not the switch is on):

    | Column                           | Is                                                                                                                                                                           |
    | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | `rooms.voice_at`                 | the room's voice is on since this moment — from its opening while the instance's voice is on (decision 37, 2026-11-19), or since the host turned it on again; null: off      |
    | `rooms.voice_epoch`              | a small counter (default 0) in the voice room's name, raised to close a voice room for all                                                                                   |
    | `room_players.voice_admitted_at` | this player was let into the voice — the host at the opening, a player who came in before a game at the join (decisions 37, 39), or by the host; null: waiting (decision 12) |
    | `room_players.voice_muted_at`    | the host muted this player; null: may speak                                                                                                                                  |
    | `users.voice_agreed_at`          | the account accepted the voice's sentence (decision 14)                                                                                                                      |

    Nothing else is stored: not who joined the voice or when, not whose microphone was open, not who spoke or for how long. Those are the voice server's, in memory, while they last. Nor are they kept in a log or a cache (C5, the audit): a reported failure of the voice server names the room and never the account (`RoomVoiceUnreachable`, `RoomVoiceNotCut`); the ticket route's limiter (`room-voice`) counts an account's requests in the cache for its minute, which the scheduler empties of expired rows every minute (`cache:prune-expired`) and the backup leaves out, so the count lives two minutes at most and is in no backup; and the voice server logs at `warn` (decision 27).

8. **A player's standing is derived** (`Rooms\Support\RoomVoice::standingOf()`, `Rooms\Enums\RoomVoiceStanding`): `waiting` (not admitted: no ticket), `muted` (admitted and muted: listens), `open` (admitted: listens and may open the microphone). The host's own row is always `open`, and nobody can mute or hold the host.
9. _(Amended 2026-11-19: a room's voice is on from its opening and the host turns it off and on again, decision 37; «on» is never refused — the limit is the ticket's, decision 38.)_ **The host's three tools, and only the host's** (`RoomPolicy::voice`: the host of an open room, while the instance's voice is on):
    - `PUT /rooms/{room}/voice` `{on}` → `SetRoomVoice`. Turning it **on** sets `voice_at`, admits every player who is present at that moment (they are the list the host is looking at) and the host. Turning it **off** clears `voice_at` and raises the epoch. Allowed at any moment, a round included. Refused with `rooms.voice.errors.busy` while `elifba.rooms.voice.max_rooms` (5) other rooms have their voice on.
    - `PUT /rooms/{room}/members/{member}/voice` `{standing: open | muted}` → `SetRoomPlayerVoice`: admits a waiting player (as `open` or as `muted`), mutes, or lets a muted player speak again. Never the host's own row. A mute stays until the host lifts it, through the voice going off and on.
    - `RemoveRoomPlayer`, as it is, which now also raises the epoch while the voice is on — for a player let into the voice; a newcomer who waits holds no ticket (C5, decision 22).

    Each runs under the room row's lock, raises the revision and signals, like the lock and the choice. They are guarded by the policy and by nothing about the page; the chosen variant draws them on the host's phone only.

10. **Letting a muted player speak never opens a microphone.** It gives the permission back; the player opens the microphone again themselves. Nothing — not the host, not the server, not a reconnect the player did not ask for — turns on a microphone its owner turned off, and `room.enable_remote_unmute` stays false.
11. _(Replaced by decisions 40 and 41, 2026-11-19: an admitted player whose account agreed is in the voice, listening, without a tap; leaving it is still one tap, and the device remembers it for that room.)_ **Joining the voice is each player's own act, every time.** While the voice is on, an admitted player sees «Səsə qoşul» and may never tap it: the game is the same without it. A join starts with the microphone off. Leaving («Səsdən çıx») is one tap and changes nothing in the game.
12. _(Amended 2026-11-19, decision 39: only an account that joins while a game runs waits; one that joins before a game — the start screen, between games — is let in at once.)_ **The newcomer waits.** An account that joins the room while its voice is on gets a player's row without `voice_admitted_at`: it plays like everyone, sees that the voice is on, and **hears nothing and can say nothing until the host taps «Səsə burax»** — no ticket is issued for it (decision 18). The host sees «:name səsə buraxılmağı gözləyir» with that button. A player who had left and returns keeps what their row had. So a stranger who guessed a code is a name in a list for the host to judge, as before voice existed.
13. _(Amended 2026-11-19, decision 39: the voice is no longer off in every room until a host turns it on, and the wait holds only while a game runs; the code, the player role and the host's eyes on the list before a game take their place.)_ **No age, still.** Nothing asks one. What stands between a young account holder and a stranger's voice is the code (a stranger has to guess it), the wait (decision 12), the host's mute and removal, the voice being off in every room until a host turns it on, and that nothing can be played back later. If that proves too little, the remedy is the switch, not a field (see "What would make us revisit").

### The ticket

18. **Who gets one** (`RoomPolicy::listen`): the host of an open room whose voice is on — whether or not the host plays, since whoever can mute must be able to hear — and a present player of that room who is admitted. Nobody else: not a waiting newcomer, a player who left, a removed player, an account outside the room, a closed or idle room, a room whose voice is off, a guest board's bearer. **The screen gets none**: `rooms/screen` has no voice code and asks for nothing (decision 22).
19. **`POST /rooms/{room}/voice/tickets`** `{agree?: true}` → `Rooms\Actions\JoinRoomVoice` (`throttle:room-voice`, 12 a minute per account). It stamps the agreement when asked to, makes sure the voice room exists at the voice server, and answers `{url, token}` (`RoomVoiceTicketData`, JSON, `no-store`) — or 503 `{status: "unavailable"}` when the voice server does not answer within `elifba.rooms.voice.timeout_ms`. A ticket is never logged, never put in a redirect and never broadcast.
20. **What a ticket is** (`Rooms\Support\RoomVoiceTicket`, signed with `firebase/php-jwt`, which C3 names in `composer.json`; no LiveKit SDK): HS256 with the instance's voice secret; `iss` the voice key, `sub` the player's row id, `exp` 60 seconds (`elifba.rooms.voice.ticket_seconds`), and

    ```
    video: { room: "{rooms.id}.{voice_epoch}", roomJoin: true,
             canSubscribe: true, canPublish: <standing is open>, canPublishData: false,
             canPublishSources: ["microphone"], canUpdateOwnMetadata: false }
    ```

    Every permission is written out, since the voice server grants what a token leaves out. It never holds `roomCreate`, `roomAdmin`, `roomList`, `roomRecord`, `hidden`, `recorder`, `agent` or `ingressAdmin`. The secret lives in `.env` (`ELIFBA_ROOMS_VOICE_SECRET`, written by `scripts/docker-env.sh`), is read through `config('elifba.rooms.voice.secret')` and never reaches a page.

21. **The voice room** is named `{rooms.id}.{voice_epoch}`, created only by the app (`room.auto_create: false`; `max_participants` = `elifba.rooms.max_players` + 1, for a host who does not play; emptied rooms close themselves after five minutes and are made again by the next ticket). The app's own calls carry tokens minted per call that live 30 seconds, with only the grant that call needs.

### Enforcement at the voice server

22. **The page is never the enforcement.** `Rooms\Support\RoomVoiceServer` is the one class that talks to the voice server (Laravel's HTTP client, the Twirp JSON calls `CreateRoom`, `DeleteRoom`, `RemoveParticipant`, `UpdateParticipant`, `ListRooms`, `ListParticipants`; connect and answer within `timeout_ms`, 1 500). Each action tells it what changed **after the commit and after the signal**, in the same request, never inside the transaction or under the room's lock, and no queue is involved:

    | What happens                                                     | The voice server is told                                                                                                         | Old tickets                                     |
    | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
    | The host mutes a player                                          | `UpdateParticipant` with the whole permission set and `can_publish: false`: the microphone's track is unpublished at once        | see decision 23                                 |
    | The host lets a muted player speak                               | `UpdateParticipant` with `can_publish: true`; the player opens the microphone themselves                                         | —                                               |
    | The host admits a waiting player                                 | nothing: that player now gets a ticket                                                                                           | —                                               |
    | The host removes a player let into the voice                     | the epoch is raised with the removal; `DeleteRoom` of the old name. Everyone is disconnected and the others' pages come back in  | dead: their room no longer exists               |
    | The host removes a newcomer who waits (C5)                       | nothing: no ticket was ever issued for that row (`voice_admitted_at` is never cleared), so nobody in the voice is cut            | —                                               |
    | The host turns the voice off; the host closes the room           | the epoch is raised; `DeleteRoom`                                                                                                | dead                                            |
    | A player leaves the room                                         | `RemoveParticipant`                                                                                                              | see decision 23                                 |
    | The host steps out of playing («Mən də oynayıram» off)           | nothing: the host keeps the voice                                                                                                | —                                               |
    | The host locks the room                                          | nothing: a lock admits no new player, and whoever is in stays                                                                    | —                                               |
    | A room goes idle, an account is deleted, anything else           | no request at that moment: tickets are refused at once (`Room::isOpen()`), and the sweep closes the voice room within 15 seconds | dead once swept                                 |
    | The instance's switch goes off (`ELIFBA_ROOMS_VOICE`, a restart) | the routes are gone at once; the sweep, which runs while a voice server is configured, deletes every voice room it finds         | dead; or the operator stops the `voice` service |

    A call that fails or times out never fails the write: it is reported as `Rooms\Exceptions\RoomVoiceNotCut` with the room id and the cause's class — never a name, an identity or a ticket — and the sweep finishes the job.

23. **The sweep** (`Rooms\Actions\SweepRoomVoices`, `rooms:voice-sweep`, every 15 seconds by the scheduler that already runs; registered while a voice server is configured). It lists the voice rooms and their participants and compares them with the facts:
    - a voice room whose room is not open, has its voice off or has another epoch — or with the instance's switch off, any voice room — is deleted;
    - a participant who may not be there (not the host, not a present admitted player), or a muted player who holds the permission to publish, means a ticket from before the fact was replayed or a cut did not arrive. The sweep does not patch it: it raises the epoch and deletes the voice room, so every old ticket dies and each page comes back with a new one.

    It reads and writes no round, no clock and nothing of `RoomWaits`. So a mute costs the room nothing in the honest case, a replayed ticket buys at most 15 seconds, and a removal is final at once.

    As built (C3, C5): within `ticket_seconds` of a player's own leave or mute, a participant who joined **before** that fact — or within two seconds after it, with a ticket signed a moment before — gets the fact's call again instead of a new epoch, since the action's own call may still be on its way. One who joined later (the voice server's `joined_at_ms`) came back with a ticket or a refreshed token from before the fact, and gets the new epoch at the first sweep that sees it: 15 seconds, and 30 at most when it came back within those two seconds and a sweep fell in them. C3 repeated the call for anyone of that minute, which let a changed client rejoin after each call for 60–75 seconds (the C5 audit).

24. **A voice server that is down delays nothing.** No request to it is made by `RecordRoomAnswer`, `RecordRoomRead`, `RoomState`, `StartRoomGame`, `EndRoomGame` or `ChooseRoomGame`. `LeaveRoom` and `RemoveRoomPlayer` commit their round's close and signal first; only the answer to that one request can wait, for at most `timeout_ms`. With the voice server down there is no voice, so nothing is left to cut; when it comes back it knows no room, and nobody can enter one until the app makes it again.

### Who hears where, and when

25. **Where a voice is heard.**
    - **A phone** plays the voice through the output the phone chooses for media — its loudspeaker, or headphones when they are connected. A page cannot pick the earpiece and does not try; loudness is the phone's volume keys. There is no volume per person.
    - **The big screen never joins**: it asks for no ticket, plays no voice and captures nothing. It shows the state's facts — that the voice is on, who waits, who is muted — and a line saying the voice is on the phones. Voices from a television beside open microphones would come back to everyone as an echo, and a second connection under the host's identity would push the host's phone out.
    - **Players in one physical room.** A phone's echo canceller removes only what that phone plays; it cannot remove another phone's loudspeaker. So people side by side wear headphones or let one phone join — the notice says it (decision 14), microphones start off, and the host can mute. Nothing detects it and nothing is promised.
    - **Cues beside the voice** (ADR 025). The screen plays the room's cues as before, and in a room without a screen every phone plays them too ([028](028-room-sound-set.md) as amended 2026-11-19). A phone in the voice keeps the cues it plays — its own quiet ones, and the room's where there is no screen — at the same level; a cue is never sent into the voice by the page, and the phone's echo canceller removes what that phone itself plays. The cue mute (`elifba.rooms.phone.muted`) does not mute the voice, and leaving the voice does not mute the cues. On iOS an open microphone changes the audio session and Safari may play other audio lower: accepted. C5 checks on a real iPhone that a cue never interrupts the voice; if it does, a phone's cues are silent while its microphone is open, and this decision says so then. _(As amended 2026-11-19, decision 35: a page keeps its capture, closed, from its first «Mikrofonu aç» until the player leaves the voice, so on iOS the call's audio session — and what it does to other audio — lasts that long, not only while the microphone is open.)_
    - **Two words apart.** The cue toggle is renamed so that «Səs» is not two things: `rooms.sound.label` «Oyun səsləri» / "Game sounds", `rooms.sound.unlock` «Oyun səslərini aç» / "Turn game sounds on" (C4) — and with it the button's name in [030](030-winner-name-at-the-final.md)'s `privacy.rooms.voice` («Ekranın «Səs» düyməsi onu da susdurur»), which C4 changes to the new word in both languages.
26. **In every phase, with nothing switched by the game.** The voice is the room's, not a game's: it runs from the start screen through the countdown, the study, the question, the reveal and the final, and between games, until the host turns it off or the room closes. No phase mutes or unmutes anyone. **Saying an answer aloud is allowed** — it is what people round a table can do; each phone's own answer is still what the server judges, and a host who wants a silent race mutes or turns the voice off.

### Hosting

27. **The service.** `voice`: `livekit/livekit-server:v1.13.11`, config `docker/voice/livekit.yaml`, behind the Compose profile `voice` — an instance that does not name the profile starts exactly today's six services. It publishes the first ports beside the proxy's: **`7882/udp`** and **`7881/tcp`**, which the host's firewall must allow. Caddy routes `/rtc/v1` and `/rtc/v1/validate` — the two paths `livekit-client` 2.24.1 opens, the WebSocket and the check it makes after a failed upgrade, matched on the path the browser sent and without the site's cookies — to `voice:7880`, and answers every other `/rtc` path itself, 404, with the voice off too (C5); nothing else of the voice server is public: its API is for `app` and `scheduler` on the internal network, as Reverb's is. The ticket travels in that WebSocket's query: Caddy keeps no access log, and its default logger — which still writes a request that fails with a 5xx, as every retry does while the voice server restarts — drops the query's ticket and join request, the headers and the second half of the address (C5). The voice server's log is `warn`: a refused or broken connection, with the voice room's name and the player's row id (C5: at `info` every join wrote the phone's system, model and browser, its addresses and its whole SDP). It is, as every service's log is (C5), Docker's `json-file` with `max-size: 5m` and `max-file: 3`: a size, not a time, which `/privacy` says as it is. Only Opus and RED pass the voice server (`room.enabled_codecs`, C5): it checks the source a client names, not the kind of its track, and decision 20's `["microphone"]` alone let a video track named the microphone through.

30. **Capacity.** The voice server adds about a twentieth of a core for a room of 20 with 3 talking, and about 200 MB; `max_rooms` (5) keeps the worst case — every microphone open and noisy — near one core (as amended 2026-11-19, decision 38: five voice rooms in use, with someone connected, not five with the voice on). The instance needs at least 2 vCPU and 4 GB with voice on; the release's plan starts by reading the VPS's size and running `lk load-test` on it.

**As built (C2, 2026-11-18).** C2 ran what C1 had read, on Docker 29.6.1 with Compose v5.3.0 (Docker Desktop, macOS, arm64); the tests are `scripts/docker-smoke.sh` with and without the profile and, outside the repository, two headless Chromes (`~/elifba-shots/voice/c2/`).

- **The image** `livekit/livekit-server:v1.13.11` pulled with the digest of the Context table. It is Alpine with busybox, so the health check is `wget` on `/` (it answers `OK`).
- **The STUN trap, seen.** With no `stun_servers` a join response offers `stun:global.stun.twilio.com:3478`, `stun:stun.l.google.com:19302` and `stun:stun1.l.google.com:19302`; with `use_external_ip: true` on a network with no route out the server does not start at all ("could not resolve external IP"). With our config it logs "using explicit node IP for NAT1To1Ips", starts healthy on such a network, and its join response offers `stun:{address}:7882` and nothing else — the smoke's STUN proof, by curl and openssl alone.
- **How the address reaches the config** (decision 28). The server reads no variable inside its file and `rtc.stun_servers` has no flag, so `docker/voice/start.sh` writes `ELIFBA_ROOMS_VOICE_IP` into a copy of `livekit.yaml` and starts the server with it.
- **Refusing the profile without its values.** Compose interpolates the whole file before it applies profiles, so a required value of `voice` would stop an instance without the profile too. `start.sh` refuses instead (exit 1, the empty lines named in `docker compose logs voice`), and `docker compose up --wait` fails ("container … is unhealthy", exit 1). An `.env` written before C2 starts the six services with no warning.
- **The two addresses exist only while `ELIFBA_ROOMS_VOICE_IP` is set**, and Caddy routes `/rtc` only then: an instance without the profile neither offers voice nor schedules the sweep, and never calls a `voice` host that is not there (the seam with C3, §12 "Live voice"). `ELIFBA_ROOMS_VOICE_URL` is the site's own `{scheme}://{domain}:{port}`, not `wss://…`: Compose cannot map a scheme, and `livekit-client` 2.24.1 turns `https` into `wss` and `http` into `ws` itself (`toWebsocketUrl`).
- **Answers.** `/rtc` gives 401 to a WebSocket without a ticket (a plain GET is 404); a ticket for a room the app did not make gets 404 "requested room does not exist" (`auto_create: false` holds); the API gives 401 JSON without a token and, from outside, the path reaches the app (404). `livekit-client` 2.24.1 signals on `/rtc/v1` (protocol 18) with the ticket as `access_token` and a `join_request` in the query; Caddy's `/rtc/*` covers it. The server sends a joined client a refreshed token in its first frames.
- **Media.** Both Chromes received each other's audio, every `RTCPeerConnection` built and kept with `iceServers: []`, only `host` candidates, the pair on UDP 7882 — through `composer dev:voice` (127.0.0.1) and through the https try-out on the LAN (signalling `wss` through Caddy, media to the LAN address). Neither finish-line condition of §12 C2 arose: the STUN entry that answers nothing is never used by the page, and Docker's bridge network kept UDP; from the page's load to hearing the other took 1.5–2 s on one laptop. Chrome's fake capture device hangs headless on macOS for audio (video works), so the harness publishes a Web Audio tone as the microphone.
- **The log.** At `info` the voice server logs private addresses whole and public ones with their last three characters cut (`140.82.12...`). On Docker Desktop a forwarded browser's packets reach the container from an address Docker's forwarder picks (here 140.82.121.5, a public one), which no socket on the Mac used. No ticket was in Caddy's log or the voice server's. At start the server warns that the UDP receive buffer is small (425 984 bytes, 5 000 000 suggested: the host's `net.core.rmem_max`); C5 judges it with its measurements on the VPS.

## The state

`RoomStateData::VERSION` becomes 5 (`z.literal(5)`; `room.changed` stays `{v: 1, revision}`). C1 wrote 3; taunts took 3 ([029](029-taunts.md), T2) and a room without a screen 4 (`room.screen`, [028](028-room-sound-set.md) as amended) on `main` first, 2026-11-19, so the voice takes the next number, and a page of either step that meets the other's state reloads itself once:

```
RoomState { v: 5, …,                                                   // every other key as in v 4 (taunts' and room.screen too)
  voice:   { on: bool, agreed: bool } | null,                          // null: the instance's voice is off, or a closed room
  players: [{ id, name | null, present, host,
              voice: open | muted | waiting | null }] }                // not null exactly for the host and the present players while voice.on
```

`agreed` is the viewer's own account's. A page shows nothing of voice while `voice` is null, the host's switch while `on` is false, and reads who may what from `players`. Who is connected, whose microphone is open and who speaks are never in the state. Fixtures: every `state.*.json` gains `voice: null` and `voice: null` on each player; `state.voice.json` (the start screen, the voice on, the viewer agreed; one player open, one muted, one waiting) and `state.voice.off.json` (the host's view, `on` false) are new.

## Consequences

- An instance decides in `.env` whether adults can talk in it, and an instance for schools never runs a voice server at all.
- A host can make a room safe to talk in without Elifba knowing anyone's age: nobody new is heard or hears until the host says so. _(As amended 2026-11-19, decision 39: while a game runs; before a game a newcomer is in at once, and the host removes whom they do not know.)_
- Elifba can honestly say that it has nothing of what was said — and for the same reason can investigate nothing. A complaint about what someone said in a room has no record behind it.
- Each removal of a player let into the voice costs everyone else a gap of about a third of a second (C5: 345 ms of silence, heard again 401 ms after the tap, both medians on a laptop); removing a newcomer who waits costs nothing, nor does a mute.
- The host now has two published ports and a server reachable from the internet to keep patched; Dependabot watches the image's tag as it watches the others.
- Players on a network that allows only 80 and 443 have no voice in the first release.
- With voice, a room without a big screen becomes the usual one, and ADR 025's own reason to revisit ("a room played without a screen often enough that phones should carry the room's sounds") is likely met. That was a decision of its own: on 2026-11-19 the owner took it ([028](028-room-sound-set.md) as amended — in a room without a screen every phone plays the room's cues), and decision 25 above reads with it.
- A host's television playing the room's cues beside the host's open microphone is heard by the others as any noise in that home is; the screen's own toggle mutes it.

## Alternatives rejected

- **A cloud voice service** (LiveKit Cloud, Twilio, Agora, Daily): every word would pass through a company outside the instance, against frontend rule 9 and ADR 024 decision 11, and outside what `/privacy` can promise.
- **Phone to phone (a WebRTC mesh)**: 20 players are 380 one-way streams, each phone uploads its voice nineteen times, and every player learns every other's IP address unless a relay is forced — which is then a server anyway.
- **A public STUN server "just for discovery"**: a third-party request from every phone, for something a server with a public address does not need.
- **mediasoup, Janus, Jitsi Videobridge**: a Node library that leaves the signalling, the rooms and the tickets to be written; GPLv3; a part of a larger stack (Prosody, Jicofo). LiveKit is one Apache-2.0 binary with tickets, permissions and a server API.
- **The LiveKit PHP SDK** (community, protobuf and Twirp packages): the app needs one JWT and six JSON calls.
- **TURN over TLS on 443 in the first release**: see decision 4.
- **End-to-end encryption**: the key would be handed out by the same server it is meant to exclude, so it would be a promise without substance; `/privacy` says the truth instead. It returns when keys can be agreed without the operator.
- **Voice always on, or on by default**: the owner decided otherwise, and a room where everyone sits together would start with an echo. _(On by default since 2026-11-19, the owner's request, decision 37; never always on — the host turns it off. Microphones still start off, so a room where everyone sits together starts without an echo.)_
- **An open microphone at a join, or a microphone remembered open**: nobody should be heard without having just chosen it.
- **The host unmuting a player's microphone**: a microphone turned on by someone else.
- **A newcomer who listens until admitted, or who is in at once while a game runs** _(rewritten 2026-11-19, decision 39; it read "or who is in at once", for every newcomer)_: a stranger with a guessed code would hear a private conversation in the middle of a game, when the host's eyes are on the round; hearing is the harm, not only speaking. Before a game the host is looking at the list as it grows, and a newcomer there is let in at once.
- **A mute that also closes the voice room for all** (airtight against a replayed ticket at once): a gap for everyone at each mute; the sweep closes that hole within 15 seconds and removal closes it at once.
- **A webhook from the voice server**: a route into the app that no session guards, for a check the sweep already makes.
- **The screen as the voices' loudspeaker, or as a silent watcher that shows who speaks**: an echo in the first case; in the second a ticket and an identity for a page that is no player, to show what every phone already shows.
- **Push-to-talk**: the fingers are answering.
- **Muting everyone during a question**: the game would be driving the voice server, and a table does not work that way.
- **A nickname, an avatar or a photo for a speaker**: the same refusal as ADR 024 — free text or a picture that others see, with no moderation.
- **Recording "for safety", or keeping who spoke when**: the opposite of what the owner promised.
- **An age question or an "I am 18" tick before the voice**: ADR 024 rejected it, and an untrue tick still costs nothing.
- **The consent folded into the player role's sentence**: a stamped sentence would change under those who accepted it.
- **The other two prototypes**: B, «Başlıq» (the microphone as a round icon in the head, the speaker in the head's second line and on the names: no height, but an open microphone is only an icon, and the start screen grows); C, «Sıra» (a row of name pills, one's own name as the microphone: everyone visible, but names cut to their first word and moving during a question).

## What would make us revisit

A law that requires verifying age or recording consent where an instance runs; evidence that the voice is used to reach or harass anyone, a child above all — then the switch goes off first and the design is reopened; players who cannot connect from networks that allow only 443 often enough to matter (TURN over TLS, decision 4); rooms larger than 20; a request for video, recording or messages, each of which is a new decision and none of which this one prepares; a way to agree end-to-end keys without the operator.

### The microphone

35. **Captured once per page, kept closed for the whole voice** (replaces decision 31's `stopMicTrackOnMute: true`; amends decisions 25 and 26). Capture starts only at its owner's first «Mikrofonu aç» on the page — never at a join, never by itself — and that is the browser's one question, where it asks at all. «Mikrofonu söndür» then disables the track: the voice server is told it is muted and the browser sends silence, never a sound; «Mikrofonu aç» enables it again, with no new `getUserMedia` and so no question (the source above). The page owns that one track (`stopMicTrackOnMute: false`, `stopLocalTrackOnUnpublish: false`): a voice room closed under the page (a new epoch) and the library's own reconnects publish the same track again, closed unless its owner had it open a moment before, as decision 26 says. It is **released** — stopped, and the system's indicator goes out — at «Səsdən çıx», when the page leaves the voice (the voice off, a removal, a close, the room page left), at the host's mute (a muted player cannot speak, and «Danışmağa icazə ver» opens nothing, decision 10), and when the browser ends it (a call, the device gone); the next «Mikrofonu aç» captures again, and on an iPhone asks only if more than ten minutes passed or the person reloaded. A hidden page closes it at once, as decision 26 says, by disabling it: nothing is sent while nobody sees the page, and it stays closed when the page is shown again until its owner taps; the capture is kept (an iPhone interrupts it in the background anyway). **The trade, said as it is:** after the first «Mikrofonu aç», the device's microphone indicator (iOS's orange dot, Android's green microphone, a desktop browser's tab icon) stays on while the player is in the voice, the microphone closed or open, and goes out at the release; the head's button, not the indicator, says whether the microphone is open, and `/privacy` says so (decision 17). Why not capture at the join instead: a listener who never speaks would get the question and a lit indicator for nothing, and "off at every join" would be a lit indicator at every join.
36. **A line where the browser can be told to always allow.** `rooms.voice.always_allow` ("Words"), one muted line under «Mikrofonu aç» in the panel, only while the browser would ask — the Permissions API's `microphone` is `prompt`, or the API cannot say. It names no menu: every browser names its settings differently, and in the phone's own language. With the site allowed, Safari has persistent access (row 10, by the source) and Chrome keeps «Allow while visiting the site» (row 14).

### Who is in the voice

37. **On from the moment a room opens** (replaces decision 7's "null: off, as every room starts", decision 13's "the voice being off in every room until a host turns it on" and the rejected "Voice always on, or on by default"). `OpenRoom` sets `voice_at` and stamps the host row's `voice_admitted_at` while the instance's voice is on — its controller tells it, as `RoomState::for(…, voice:)` is told (architecture §12 "As built (C3)": the domain never reads the switch). It makes no call to the voice server: the first ticket makes the voice room (`JoinRoomVoice`, decision 19), so opening a room still waits for nothing. The host turns the voice off and on as before (`SetRoomVoice`, from «Otağın idarəsi» and the panel), and «on» again admits every present player (decision 9). The default is derived from the switch alone, like `RoomDefaults`: never an account's preference, never a column. A room opened while the switch was off keeps its voice off, and a room opened before this amendment keeps what it has. Never _always_ on: the host's switch stays, for a table where everyone sits together or a game played in silence.
38. **The limit counts voice rooms in use** (replaces decision 9's refusal of «on» and amends decision 30). With every room's voice on, counting `voice_at` would fill `max_rooms` with rooms nobody talks in and refuse the sixth host's voice for nothing. `SetRoomVoice` no longer refuses. `JoinRoomVoice` counts instead: while its own voice room has nobody in it, it lists the voice server's rooms (`ListRooms`, already one of `RoomVoiceServer`'s six calls, each room with its `num_participants`) and refuses the ticket while `elifba.rooms.voice.max_rooms` (5) other voice rooms have someone in them — 503 `{status: "busy"}` (`Rooms\Exceptions\RoomVoiceBusy`), said with `rooms.voice.errors.busy` and «Yenidən cəhd et» (decision 26's table). A voice room in use always takes one more. No column; two first joins at one instant may make six, as C3 accepted for «on». A room past the limit shows its voice as on and each phone's button as that failure; nothing of the game changes.
39. **A player who comes in before a game is in at once** (amends decisions 12 and 13; the rejected "A newcomer who listens until admitted, or who is in at once" is rewritten below). `JoinRoom` stamps `voice_admitted_at` on a row that has none while the room's voice is on and no game is running — the start screen, between games, the final — where the host is looking at the list as it grows, which is decision 9's own reason for admitting everyone present at «on». A player who comes in while a game runs waits for «Səsə burax», as decision 12 says; a player who returns keeps what their row had, and a row with no admission that comes back before a game is let in like any newcomer; one who came during a game and is still in the room waits for the host, not for the game's end. The protection changes, and is said: a stranger who guessed a code and comes in before a game hears and is heard at once, until the host removes them — a removal cuts at once (decision 22) — and the gate is the code (six digits, ten tries a minute per account, `room-join`), the player role with its confirmed e-mail address, and the host's eyes on the list before a game. Why not every newcomer at once: during a game the host's eyes are on the round, and a stranger could listen unseen.
40. _(Amended by decision 45: «Səsə qoşul» is gone — out of the voice, the microphone's tap brings the page back in, its microphone open.)_ **In without a tap** (replaces decision 11; amends decisions 1 and 34). A page joins the voice by itself — listening, its microphone off — when it opens, when its player is admitted, when the voice comes on, and when it is shown again after the library left the voice with the hidden page (decision 26), for a viewer who is admitted (`open` or `muted`), whose account agreed (`voice.agreed`), and who has not left that room's voice on this device. «Səsdən çıx» and «İndi yox» are remembered for that room on that device — `localStorage` `elifba.rooms.voice.out`, the ids of the last twenty such rooms, every read and write in `try`/`catch`, never sent — and the page never undoes them: only the player's tap of «Səsə qoşul» brings them back in and takes the room off the list. A browser whose storage is unavailable forgets them at a reload, and the page joins by itself again. A failure — unreachable, busy, another device (decision 26) — waits for a tap: the page never tries in a loop, and two devices of one account do not take the voice from each other back and forth. Joining by itself loads the library at that moment (decision 1's tap becomes it) and asks for the ticket as a tap did; it starts no capture, so it never brings the browser's question. The screen still asks for no ticket and joins nothing.
41. _(Amended by decision 45: the microphone's tap, not «Səsə qoşul», opens it for an account that has not agreed.)_ **The sentence opens by itself, once** (amends decision 14). For an admitted viewer whose account has not agreed, the sheet with the sentence opens by itself — on the start screen, between games or at the final, never over a round (it waits for the round's end) — unless «İndi yox» was tapped for that room on that device; «Səsə qoşul» opens it as before. «Razıyam, qoşul» stamps the account once, and from then on every room's page joins by itself. The sentence and its stamp are unchanged: it says "I am joining the live voice", which joining by itself still is, and an account that agreed before this amendment is joined by itself too. What follows the agreement is said beside it, in the first of the three lines (`rooms.voice.note.mic`, decision 14 as amended), and on `/privacy` (decision 17).
42. **Where the browser holds the voice back.** (amends decision 25's line on iOS and docs/frontend.md rule 15 "As built (C4)"'s "no text says it"). A page that joins by itself starts its voices without a tap, and the browser may hold them back: an iPhone always, since WebKit plays a live stream without a tap only while the page captures or already plays audio (rows 15–18); Chrome only on a page nobody has tapped yet — after a reload, or a link opened from outside the app such as a scanned QR (rows 19, 20); a player who came by taps in the app is heard at once there. The page keeps C4's unlock — the next tap anywhere runs the library's `startAudio()`, which on iOS also starts a silent element, so every later voice plays (row 18) — and since joining by itself makes this the usual first moment on an iPhone, it says so: while the browser holds the voice back, the head's button carries its dot, and its label and the panel's first line say `rooms.voice.tap_to_hear` ("Words"). One tap per page, not per voice; the same tap starts the game sounds where they are held, and decision 25's `rooms.sound.unlock` stays the screen's own button. After the page's first «Mikrofonu aç» nothing is held: the page captures.
43. _(Amended by decision 46: a reload of the page within a minute of it going away with the microphone open opens it again.)_ **What does not change.** Nothing turns a microphone on but its owner's tap — a page that joins by itself listens and captures nothing, and only «Mikrofonu aç» starts a capture or enables it (the restore of decision 26 gives back only what its owner had open a moment before); the microphone is off at every join; the agreement is once per account, and the player role's sentence is untouched; the host mutes, lets speak and removes as before; nothing is recorded, transcribed or kept; the screen gets no ticket and joins, plays and captures no voice; the state stays `v: 5`, the routes and the columns are the same, and the room channel carries `room.changed` and nothing else; and with the instance's switch off every page draws and asks exactly what it did before — `OpenRoom` sets nothing, no page joins, the library is never loaded.

### The head's microphone

44. **One tap in the head opens the microphone** (amends decision 34; the owner during the live test of this build, 2026-11-19: «olmazmı ki headerdə mikrafona vurduqda birbaşa mikrafon açılsın, iki dəfə vurmaq məcburiyyətdə qalır istifadəçi»). With the page in the voice by itself (decision 40), opening the microphone through the panel was two taps — the head's button, then «Mikrofonu aç». In the voice — connected, not resuming — the head's control is a pill of two buttons: the microphone, which says what a tap does (`rooms.voice.mic.open` / `.close`, `aria-pressed` while open) and opens or closes it at once — `MicOff` on the voice's tint while closed, `Mic` filled with its ring while open, a spinner while the browser asks, `MicOff` dashed for a player the host muted, whose tap opens nothing and says `rooms.voice.muted_you` — and beside it a narrow ⌄ that opens the panel «Canlı səs» (who speaks, the rows, «Səsdən çıx», the host's tools), carrying the host's count of newcomers who wait and the dot of a held voice (decision 42). Out of the voice — invited, waiting, in trouble, insecure — the head stays the one round button that opens the panel, as decision 34 drew it: there is no microphone to open. It is still the owner's tap that opens the microphone (decision 43), now the head's instead of the panel's; the first one captures, and the browser asks where it asks (decision 35). Since the panel's line to always allow (decision 36) is then seldom seen, a tap in the head that brings the browser's question says it as a note for its moment (`NOTE_MS`), with the same words. The panel keeps «Mikrofonu aç» / «Mikrofonu söndür»: two ways to the same tap. Why not a long press for the panel: nobody finds it. Why not the microphone alone with the panel elsewhere: «Səsdən çıx» and the host's «Səsə burax» need a way in from every moment. The pill is 4.75rem wide where the button was 2.75rem; the round's title gives up the difference.

45. **No «Səsə qoşul»: the microphone is the way in** (amends decisions 11, 34, 40, 41 and 44; the owner during the same live test, 2026-11-19: «ümumiyyətlə səsə qoşuluda aradan götürək birbaşa mikrafon»). With the page in the voice by itself, joining was a step a player met only after leaving or declining, and it read as a second thing to do before speaking. So there is no step called joining: wherever the viewer is admitted and the page is secure, the head is the microphone and its ⌄ (decision 44). In the voice its tap opens or closes the microphone. Out of it — after «Səsdən çıx», after «İndi yox», for an account that has not agreed — the same tap brings the page into the voice and opens the microphone, in one tap: the owner's tap, the first capture, the browser's question where it asks (decisions 35, 43). An account that never agreed meets the sentence first (decision 14 holds word for word, and «Razıyam, qoşul» keeps its words: it is the agreement's button); after it, the page joins and opens the microphone the tap asked for, while the sentence that opened by itself (decision 41) still joins listening. The panel of a viewer out of the voice says `rooms.voice.invite` and offers «Mikrofonu aç», which does the same. «Səsdən çıx» stays in the panel — a table of players in one room hears itself twice, and "What would make us revisit" names that echo — and it is remembered as before (decision 40); the microphone's tap takes the room off the list. The newcomer who waits keeps the hourglass (there is no microphone to open until the host lets them in), and a failure keeps its words and its way back (decision 26). The words `rooms.voice.join` and `rooms.voice.invite_line` go ("Words"); no word is added. Why not the microphone alone, with no leaving: the echo above, and a player who wants the game in silence.

46. **A reload keeps an open microphone open** (amends decisions 26, 35 and 43, and decision 17's second paragraph; the owner during the same live test, 2026-11-19: «səyfəni yeniləyəndə təkrar səsizə düşməsin ən son açıqdırsa açıq qalsın»). A reload made the microphone off again — off at every join — and a player who reloads mid-sentence had to find it again. Now, when a room's page goes away while its microphone is open — the library leaving the voice with the page (`beforeunload`, `pagehide`, a frozen tab: `VoiceLink`'s `onLeftOpen`, since the library leaves at `beforeunload`, before the page's own `pagehide` could see the microphone open), the page hidden as it goes (decision 26), or `beforeunload` / `pagehide` with it still open — the device notes that room and the moment (`localStorage` `elifba.rooms.voice.reopen`, one room, in `try`/`catch`, never sent, `rooms/voice-reopen.ts`); when the same room's page loads again **within 60 seconds** (`REOPEN_MS`), the page joins by itself as decision 40 says and opens the microphone with it, once: the note is taken at the load and gone. Shown again without a reload, the page forgets it (its microphone stays closed until a tap, as decision 26 says). It never opens for a player the host muted, a voice that is off, a viewer who is not let in, has not agreed or left that room's voice on the device (decisions 37–41), on an insecure page, or later than the minute: a phone put away and opened again ten minutes later starts closed. It is the owner's own open microphone carried over a reload, not a new one: nothing else turns a microphone on. On an iPhone a reload by the person clears WebKit's grants (row 6), so the reopened page may meet the browser's question at once — the one question of that page (decision 35); Chrome keeps «Allow while visiting the site» and «Allow this time» until it closes (rows 12–14). `/privacy` says it in decision 17's second paragraph. Why a minute: a reload is seconds; a longer note would open a microphone the person no longer expects.

### Consequences (as amended)

- A room's voice costs nothing until someone joins it: no call is made at the opening, and the limit counts only voice rooms with someone in them.
- A player who agreed once never taps to join: in every room with the voice on the page is in the voice, listening, when it opens — heard at once on Chrome after taps in the app, and on an iPhone after one tap anywhere on the page (decision 42) — and «Səsdən çıx» is one tap that the device remembers.
- On an iPhone the browser asks at the page's first «Mikrofonu aç» and not again on that page; it asks again after the person reloads, and after a microphone released more than ten minutes earlier — unless the site is allowed in Safari's settings, which the line under the button says.
- After the first «Mikrofonu aç», the device's microphone indicator stays on while the player is in the voice, the microphone closed or open: the page's own button is what says whether one is heard.
- A stranger who guessed a code before a game is heard at once, until the host removes them; during a game, as before, nobody new is heard or hears until the host says so.
- On an iPhone the call's audio session lasts from the first «Mikrofonu aç» until the player leaves the voice, not only while the microphone is open (decision 25's line about other audio played lower).

### Alternatives rejected (as amended)

- **Capture at the join**: every listener asked for a microphone and lit for nothing.
- **Asking for the microphone when the page opens, to be done with it**: a question nobody chose, and the indicator lit on every phone of every room.
- **One capture for the whole app, kept across rooms**: an indicator lit outside any voice, and a microphone that outlives the room it was opened in.
- **Every newcomer in at once, a game running or not**: decision 39's reason.
- **Letting in, at a game's end, everyone who waited**: the host had not looked at them; «Səsə burax» is one tap.
- **The default as an account's setting, or `voice_at` as a column default**: a preference the owner did not ask for, and a database default would turn the voice on where the instance's switch is off.
- **Retrying a full voice by itself**: a page that knocks every few seconds on a full server; a tap is enough.
- **A new sentence to accept, re-asked of every account**: the sentence is still true of what the account does; a stamp that changed under those who gave it is what the rejected "consent folded into the player role's sentence" warns against.

### What would make us revisit (as amended)

Players who find the voice on at the start unwelcome — echo at a shared table, or strangers before a game — often enough to matter: then the default goes back to off, which is one argument of `OpenRoom`'s caller, and the rest stays. A phone that shows a closed microphone heard, or the indicator going out while the microphone is kept: a defect first. The limit refused often (`rooms.voice.errors.busy` in players' reports, the release's checklist): a larger host or a higher `max_rooms`.
