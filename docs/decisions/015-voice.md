# 015 — The board speaks: prompts and praise in a synthetic voice, generated once

- **Status:** Accepted, 2026-11-11 (the owner's choices, after hearing samples). Built in V1 the same month (architecture §3 "Voice"), with three changes to the outline below: the generator is the artisan command `php artisan content:voice` (testable with faked HTTP and ffmpeg) instead of `scripts/voice/generate.php`, with its steps in `scripts/voice/README.md`; the clip list is its own file, `content/voice-clips.json`, which the command writes, so it never rewrites the hand-written `voice.json`; and the key is read as `config('elifba.voice.api_key')` from `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` in the shell or the gitignored `.env` (never `.env.example`), as every other setting is. One setting was added after the first sample: a short phrase sent alone («Pişik hansıdır?») came out with a Turkish accent, so every request also gets unspoken Azerbaijani context (`previous_text`, `next_text`, `voice.json`'s `context`, part of the hash), which the owner chose by ear over a higher Stability, no tag and a long text split by silences.
- **Sections:** architecture.md §3 "Voice", §8
- **Amends:** architecture.md §3 "Voice" (no recorded voice → prerecorded prompts and praise), the owner decisions of 2026-11-11 in §0 and row 2 of "Open questions for the owner"; [012](012-picture-and-number-games.md) context and its rejected "Recorded prompts or speech synthesis" (superseded here; 012 is not rewritten).
- **Why it is hard to reverse:** some nine hundred audio files enter the repository under a license we give them, and every self-hosted copy carries them from then on; the voice is a person's cloned voice that its owner has withdrawn from the provider's library, so after about a year no new clip can be made in it; and stored round snapshots carry `prompt.audio` URLs from the first voiced game on.

## Context

§3 "Voice" said: no recorded voice, the adult reads the prompt, `prompt.audio` stays null, and "adding voice later is content work only". The owner reopened it (2026-11-11): the board should say the prompt («Dovşan hansıdır?») and the praise («Doğru cavab!»), perhaps a student's or child's name, with ElevenLabs or a better fit, and never pay twice for the same audio.

What was checked, all on 2026-11-11:

**Language and quality.** ElevenLabs lists Azerbaijani for Eleven v4 ("90+ languages", `eleven_v4`) and for Eleven v3; Multilingual v2 (29 languages) and Flash v2.5 (32) do not have it ([Eleven v4](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech/eleven-v4), [languages](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/other/what-languages-do-you-support)). The owner listened in their own ElevenLabs account to the same test text — prompts of every kind, praise, the letters ə and ğ, the syllables «ba» and «ğa», single words, two invented names — in three voices with Eleven v4 and the language set to Azerbaijani: «Nigar – Ad Read» and «Arif – Azerbaijani Narrator» (Voice Library voices of Azerbaijani speakers) and «Sarah» (a default voice; the app itself warns "Use a Azerbaijani voice for best results"). The owner chose Nigar, then asked for more emotion: with Stability lowered to 0.13 and v4's audio tags (`[playful]`, `[curious]`, `[excited]`, `[cheerfully]`, `[warmly]`) the answer was «bax belə bəyəndim». Azure's two az-AZ voices were compared on paper only (the owner supplied no key), and no self-hosted model was heard (below).

**License.** ElevenLabs' free plan "does not include a commercial license", and what is published from it must be attributed to ElevenLabs in the title; every paid plan includes a commercial license, which covers what was generated during the subscription "forever" after it ends ([publishing](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/legal/can-i-publish-the-content-i-generate-on-the-platform), [after a subscription ends](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/account/general/what-happens-to-my-content-after-my-subscription-ends)). The Terms of Use (last updated 2026-10-09) say "you retain all rights in and to your Output" (§4(c)(ii)), that a paid user "may use the Services for commercial purposes" (§1(c)), and that downloaded Output may be used outside the service "always subject to these Terms" (§4(a)); they hold no clause against redistributing or sublicensing Output ([terms](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)). So free-plan audio cannot enter this repository (non-commercial, attribution in the title), and audio made in a paid month can.

**The voice is leaving.** «Nigar – Ad Read» is a Voice Library voice. It is no longer listed in the library; the owner's account keeps it under "My voices" with a one-year notice period (its sibling «Anar – Ad Read» showed the notice itself: "removed from the Voice Library by its owner … no longer accessible to you after a Notice Period of 365 days"). A removed voice stays available for the notice period to those who saved or used it ([notice period](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/voices/voice-library/what-is-a-notice-period)), and outputs generated before it ends "will continue to exist and remain available for use thereafter" (Voice Library Addendum, last updated 2026-03-06, [vla](https://elevenlabs.io/vla)). The exact end date was not visible in the account.

**Cost.** Starter is $6 a month ($1 the first month, $5 billed yearly) and is the cheapest plan with the commercial license; API use of Eleven v4 costs $0.08 per 1,000 characters and Starter includes 75,000 such characters ([pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing), [API pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). Everything Elifba could voice today is about 15,400 characters in 919 clips, about 24,500 with a tag in front of each:

| What                                                      | Clips | Characters (text only) |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----- | ---------------------- |
| «{x}» hansıdır? (picture words of animals, fruit, colors) | 79    | ≈ 1,200                |
| Neçə «{x}» var? (things counted)                          | 76    | ≈ 1,150                |
| «{x}» rəqəmini tap! (1–10)                                | 10    | ≈ 180                  |
| «{x}» hərfini tap! (32 letters)                           | 32    | ≈ 480                  |
| «{x}» hecasını tap! / qur! (263 syllables each)           | 526   | ≈ 8,730                |
| «{x}» sözünü tap! (184 reading words)                     | 184   | ≈ 3,400                |
| The seven templates without `{x}`                         | 7     | ≈ 150                  |
| Praise and the retry phrase                               | 5     | ≈ 60                   |

One paid month covers it three times over. As mono MP3 the clips are 10–12 MB in the repository.

**Alternatives looked at.** Azure AI Speech has two az-AZ voices, `az-AZ-BanuNeural` and `az-AZ-BabekNeural` ([language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts)), at $15 per million characters (Microsoft's [Retail Prices API](https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices), meter "S1 Neural Text To Speech Characters", West Europe) with 0.5 million free a month ([pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/cognitive-services/speech-services/)), and does not keep the text or the audio of real-time synthesis ([data and privacy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/speech-service/text-to-speech/data-privacy-security)). Its Product Terms are quoted on Microsoft Q&A as giving output rights "for Customers of the paid tier TTS Service only" ([thread](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5805156/please-clarify-the-conflicting-information-regardi)); the Product Terms page itself could not be read, so that clause is unverified. Google's Gemini-TTS lists az-AZ as Preview ([Gemini-TTS](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/gemini-tts)). Of the models one can host: Meta's MMS-TTS is CC-BY-NC 4.0 ([model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-azb)), which §8 forbids; «Anadil Azerbaijani TTS» has MIT weights on the Apache-2.0 VoxCPM2 but clones the voice of an unnamed speaker from a dataset whose license its card does not state ([model card](https://huggingface.co/Anadilorg/Anadil_Azerbaijani_TTS), [VoxCPM2](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2)), so its output cannot be credited; VoxCPM2 alone does not list Azerbaijani.

**Names.** Voicing a name means sending it to the provider when the student or child is added, or at play. ElevenLabs transfers personal information "to the United States", says its services "are not intended for … anyone under 18", may use inputs to train its models unless the account opts out, and offers zero retention to enterprise customers only ([privacy policy](https://elevenlabs.io/privacy-policy), last updated 2026-10-09; [zero retention](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/resources/zero-retention-mode)). §7 and ADR 013 promise that a child's name reaches no one but the account's own devices. There is no queue (`QUEUE_CONNECTION=sync`), so a call at runtime would block a request, and a self-hosted instance would need a key of its own to sound like the public one.

## Decision

1. **The board speaks prerecorded clips.** No speech is synthesised at runtime, by the server or the browser: every clip is a file under `content/assets/voice/`, made once, offline, and committed. A page still makes no third-party request, and an instance needs no key.
2. **Provider, model, voice.** ElevenLabs, model `eleven_v4`, language `az`, the voice «Nigar – Ad Read», Stability 0.13, Similarity 0.75, one audio tag in front of each text, MP3. These values live in `content/voice.json`, not in code.
3. **Every prompt is voiced, as a whole phrase.** Each of the fourteen templates of `prompts.json`: a template with `{x}` gets one clip per item it can name (the table above), a template without one gets one clip. Never a word clip stitched to a template clip: the owner's ear and the price both allow whole phrases, and stitched intonation breaks in an emotional reading. The clip says what the adult would read: the prompt text without « », a letter by its name from `alphabet.json`, a number by its Azerbaijani name.
4. **The voice never does the child's reading.** A clip says the prompt, never an item: in `find` the child still reads the items to find what was named, in `pop` the clip is only «Hər hərfi oxu və vur!», in `build_word` and `build_sentence` only the instruction. Tapping an item says nothing.
5. **Praise and a retry phrase.** When a round is solved the board says one of «Doğru cavab!», «Afərin!», «Əla!», «Çox gözəl!», after the `solved` sound. On a wrong tap it says «Bir də cəhd et!», after the `miss` sound, at most once in a round. The sound effects stay as they are. The phrases are data in `content/voice.json`.
6. **No name is ever voiced,** a student's or a child's: nothing about a child goes to a speech provider, and §7 and ADR 013 stand unchanged. Praise has no name in it.
7. **Teacher words are not voiced.** A teacher word is a row, may be a child's name and has no file: a round whose prompt names one has `prompt.audio` null and plays as today, the adult reading. The same holds for any text with no clip: null is always valid, and the board is silent for that prompt.
8. **Generate once, key by content.** A clip's file name is a hash of the spoken text, the tag, the voice, the model and the settings. The generator makes only the hashes that have no file, so nothing is paid for twice and a changed text or voice makes a new file. Generation is not deterministic (the API says so of its `seed`), so a clip, once accepted by ear, is kept and never regenerated.
9. **Who pays, and how long.** The owner buys one month of Starter in their own account, the generation session makes every clip in that month, and the owner cancels. New content later means another paid month. Free-plan audio never enters the repository.
10. **License of the files.** MIT, author "Elifba contributors", like the sounds synthesised for Elifba (§8), with one `credits.json` entry for `content/assets/voice/*` whose `source` and `changes` say that the voice is synthetic: made with ElevenLabs Eleven v4 and the Voice Library voice «Nigar – Ad Read» under a paid plan, on the date of generation. `/credits` therefore tells every visitor that the voice is a machine's.
11. **An account can turn it off.** Settings → Oyun gets two switches, «Tapşırığı səslə de» and «Tərif səsi», both on for a new account; a teacher who prefers to read aloud turns the first off. The board has a button that says the prompt again.

## Implementation outline

Nothing below exists yet; the names are the plan.

**(a) Built-in content and praise**

- `scripts/voice/generate.php` with a `README.md` (next to `scripts/sounds`, `scripts/art`): boots the app, lists every spoken text from `ContentRepository`, `prompts.json`, `alphabet.json`, `levels.json` and `Syllabifier`, hashes each, calls ElevenLabs only for a hash without a file, levels and trims with ffmpeg, writes `content/assets/voice/{hash}.mp3` and the clip list of `content/voice.json`. The key is read from `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` in the shell and appears in no file, `.env.example` and `config/` included. It never reads the database: no teacher word and no name can reach it.
- `content/voice.json`: a `version`; `voice` (provider, model, voice id and name, language, stability, similarity); `styles` (the tag of each template, of praise and of the retry phrase); `spoken` (number names, and a respelling for any text the voice says wrongly); `praise` and `retry` (the phrases); `clips` (hash → file).
- `Content\Data\VoiceData`, `Content\Support\VoiceClips` (`url(string $spoken): ?string`, the same hash as the script), rules in `ContentValidator` (every listed clip is a file under `content/assets/voice`, every file is listed, the folder has its credits entry, every praise and retry phrase has a clip) with a broken fixture per rule, and a `content:validate` line that counts the built-in prompts with no clip without failing on them.
- `Play\Support\PromptVoice`: template + target → spoken text → URL or null; null for a teacher word. The round builders (`FindRoundBuilder`, `PopRoundBuilder`, `CountRoundBuilder`, the build builders, through `RoundParts`) fill `PromptData::$audio` from it. The round contract does not change: `prompt.audio` has been a nullable URL since A6; the fixtures `round.find-picture` and `round.count` gain a URL, the others keep null.
- `Play\Data\BoardVoiceData` (`prompt`, `praise`: booleans), `UpdateBoardVoice`, `PUT /settings/play/voice` in the account group, `users.board_voice`; `Play\Support\BoardVoice` gives `BoardProps` a `voice` object: whether prompts are spoken, and the praise and retry URLs or empty lists.
- The board: `resources/js/game/voice.ts`, on the page's one `AudioContext` (`stage.ts`). It says `prompt.audio` when a round's items have arrived, but not when it is the URL the round before had (so «Hər hərfi oxu və vur!» is said once, not every round); praise after `solved`; the retry phrase once a round; it pauses with `BoardScene.setPaused()`; a «🔊» button repeats the prompt. The guest board and the home board behave the same: the URLs are public content files.
- `resources/js/pages/settings/play.tsx`: the two switches, with keys in `lang/az.json` and `lang/en.json`.
- Docs with the code: §3 "Voice" and "Where content lives" (the `voice.json` rules), §8 (the voice row), `docs/frontend.md`, `CREDITS.md`.
- Tests: `tests/Unit/Content/VoiceClipsTest.php` (the hash, a missing clip is null), `tests/Unit/Content/ContentValidatorTest.php` (the new rules), `tests/Feature/Play/PromptVoiceTest.php` (a built-in word gets a URL, a teacher word gets null, a `pop` round carries only the instruction), `tests/Feature/Settings/PlayVoiceSettingsTest.php`, `tests/Feature/Auth/RoleAreasTest.php` (the new route), `tests/Unit/TranslationsTest.php` (the new keys), `resources/js/game/voice.test.ts` (no repeat of the same URL, the retry phrase once a round, nothing when switched off) and the contract fixtures.
- The owner listens to every clip before the commit, on a review page the script writes outside the repository: 919 clips are about half an hour.

**(b) Names and teacher words**

Not accepted, so nothing is built. A later record that reopened it would have to settle: a provider that keeps no text; the privacy note and the agreement sentences of both roles; a cache whose paths hold no name; deletion of the clip with the student, child or word; what the guest board's bearer may fetch; self-hosted instances without a key; and a call that cannot block a request while there is no queue.

## Consequences

- A child who cannot read can hear what to find; the adult no longer has to read every prompt, and may still do so with the first switch off.
- Rounds with a teacher word, and any content added before its clips are made, are silent: voiced and unvoiced rounds can follow each other in one game. `content:validate` says how many built-in prompts have no clip.
- The repository grows by 10–12 MB of MP3, and every content change that adds a word, a syllable or a template needs a paid month and a generation run before it has a voice.
- **The voice has an end date.** Within about a year «Nigar – Ad Read» stops being available to the owner's account. Clips made before then stay usable, but later content cannot be voiced in it: then either all clips are made again in another voice (about one paid month, since the key includes the voice) or new content stays silent.
- **A license risk we accept knowingly.** ElevenLabs' terms give the account holder all rights in the Output and a lasting commercial license, and do not forbid redistribution; they do not say in so many words that Output may be relicensed under an open license. MIT lets anyone who gets the files do more with them than ElevenLabs' own rules allow its users (training a voice on them, say). The files sit in one folder with one credits entry, so they can be withdrawn or replaced together.
- `/credits` discloses the synthetic voice; `/privacy` does not change, since nothing new is stored or sent.
- A picture round can now be played by a child without an adult reading, which ADR 012 named as a reason to revisit; the account, the device and the adult's presence are still the parent's (ADR 013).

## Alternatives rejected

- **The ElevenLabs free plan:** non-commercial, and attribution in the title of whatever is published; neither fits an MIT repository.
- **Runtime synthesis** (the server or the browser calling a provider): a third party in every game, a key on every instance, a blocking call without a queue, and the same phrase paid for again and again.
- **Browser speech synthesis:** as in §3 before — classroom computers have no Azerbaijani voice.
- **Azure AI Speech (Banu, Babək):** cheaper and it keeps no text, but the owner chose by ear among ElevenLabs voices and did not hear these; its output terms for the free tier could not be verified. It remains the first candidate if names are ever reopened.
- **Google Gemini-TTS:** Azerbaijani is in Preview.
- **A self-hosted model:** MMS-TTS is non-commercial; the Anadil adapter clones an unnamed speaker from data of unstated license.
- **A default ElevenLabs voice (Sarah):** no notice period to worry about, but not an Azerbaijani speaker's voice, and the owner preferred Nigar.
- **«Arif – Azerbaijani Narrator»:** still in the library with a two-year notice period; the owner preferred Nigar.
- **A word clip stitched to a template clip:** fewer characters, but the cost is a few dollars either way and the joins are audible.
- **Praise only, no retry phrase** (the recommendation): the owner chose the retry phrase too, limited to once a round.
- **Voicing names or teacher words:** decisions 6 and 7.
- **A human recording:** the best provenance, and still possible later, since a clip is only a file; nobody is at hand to record nine hundred phrases now.

## What would make us revisit

The voice becoming unavailable before the clips are made; ElevenLabs' terms changing what may be done with Output; a native speaker willing to record the set; a self-hostable Azerbaijani model with a clear license and a named, consenting voice; or a wish to voice names, which needs a record of its own.
