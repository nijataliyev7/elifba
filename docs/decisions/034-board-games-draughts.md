## Context

Every room game so far is a **race**: everyone answers the same round at once, rounds are timed and built when the game starts (ADRs 021–023). Draughts is not: two people take turns on one board until the game ends, and the rest of the room watches. Pull request #9 built it beside the race rather than inside the room's machinery. What it got right stays (decision 4, decision 9, "PR #9: keep, reshape, drop"). What had to change:

1. **A second state.** `GET /rooms/{room}/match` with TypeScript types written by hand (`components/rooms/checkers/match.ts`), no zod schema, no fixture, outside `RoomContractTest` and `contract.test.ts` — against "the state is the one thing a room page reads" and "types are inferred from the schemas".
2. **A second lifecycle.** `StartRoomMatch`, `EndRoomMatch` and `room_matches` beside `StartRoomGame`, `EndRoomGame` and `room_games`; `ThrowMatchTaunt` and `room_match_taunts` beside `ThrowRoomTaunt` and `room_taunts`; `use-match.ts` and `use-match-sound.ts`, with a clock and a sound player of their own, beside `useLiveRoom()`, `useServerNow()`, `roomCues()` and `roomMoment()`; `rooms.board` checked by `OpenRoom`, `RoomPolicy`, `RoomList`, `RoomState`, both controllers and three pages.
3. **Nothing of the room's game around it**: no `RivalryStrip`, `FinalStanding`, winner's cheer (ADR 030), taunt clip (ADR 029, T4) or motion (ADR 025); one component of 881 lines.
4. **A record that contradicted itself**: its decision 6 said a match counts in no ranking, its decision 10 gave it 3 and 1.
5. **Rules unfinished and unchecked**: the only draw was thirty quiet king plies; no list of moves, no draw offer; a fixed clock of five minutes; nothing independent checked the move generator; the position stored beside the moves it follows from.

## Decision

### One game, two kinds

1. **A match is a `room_games` row.** A room game has a **kind** — `Content\Enums\RoomGameKind`: `race` (the six games of rounds) or `board` — and the kind follows from its family: `RoomFamily` gains `board` («Taxta oyunları»), `RoomMechanic` gains `draughts` («Şaşki»), and `RoomMechanic::kind()` is `family()->kind()`. A board game is one game of the room in every sense a race is: one runs at a time, by `position`; `StartRoomGame` starts it from the room's choice and `EndRoomGame` ends it; it is the state's `game`; it is listed in «Keçmiş oyunlar» and a closed room's results; its taunts are `room_taunts` rows of its `room_game_id`; it counts in `RoomRanking::ofRoom()`; `PruneOldRooms`, `CloseRoom` and `DeleteRoom` reach it through the tables they already know. **Why not a table of its own:** every one of those would need a second copy or an `if` — the pull request shows each — and a second "what is running in this room" would have to be kept from disagreeing with the first under the same lock.
2. **The game is called draughts in code and «Şaşki» in the interface.** `draughts`, `Draughts*`, English "Draughts": "checkers" names the English game, whose men do not capture backwards and whose kings do not fly; the oracle's variant is `russian` (decision 32). No file, class, key or route keeps the word `checkers`.
3. **One dispatch point per concern.** On the server the kind is read in one place: `Rooms\Support\Drivers\RoomGameDriver::of($mechanic)` returns the `RaceDriver` or the `BoardDriver`, and everything of a game that depends on its kind is a method of that interface — nothing else in `Rooms` asks for the kind, compares a mechanic with `Draughts` or tests a driver's class. Its concerns:

    | Concern                            | A race (`RaceDriver`, today's code moved)                                | A board game (`BoardDriver`)                                                    |
    | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | the choice: its rules and defaults | a level and a number of rounds (`RoomGameSetupData::rules()` as it is)   | a level — its clock — and two seats (decisions 6–8)                             |
    | the start                          | builds and stores the rounds                                             | seats the two players, copies the clock; refuses fewer than two present players |
    | when the game is over              | `RoomTimetable`                                                          | the outcome of `DraughtsMatch` (decision 14)                                    |
    | its closes and what each earned    | every round's close; `room_answers.points`                               | one close, its end; the outcome's points (decision 21)                          |
    | a taunt: who, at whom, the balance | any present player at any other; the points of closed rounds less taunts | a seat at the other seat; what the match can still pay (decision 22)            |
    | the state's part                   | `round`                                                                  | `match`                                                                         |
    | a presence that changed            | `RoomRoundClose::whenPresenceChanges()`                                  | a seat that left or was removed has lost (decision 13)                          |
    | a read (`seen_ms`)                 | `RoomWaits::readCountsFrom()`                                            | never: no round waits for anyone                                                |

    The data classes that hold a shape by kind — `RoomGameSetupData`, `RoomSetupData`, `RoomGameData`, `RoomStateData` — state it once each in their constructors, as `RoomRoundData` states a phase. On the page side each concern has one function that looks at the kind, listed in decision 27. A guard test on each side holds the lists (decision 34).

### The rules of the game

4. **Russian draughts as the official text has it, judged in PHP only.** The text is «Правила вида спорта "шашки"», approved by the order of the Ministry of Sport of Russia of 17 March 2022, no. 201 — its part 6.1, «Русские шашки», and the general articles 5.4 — as published by the Federation of Draughts of Russia (`shashki.ru/download/2022/doc/FSHR_shashki_pravila_2022.doc`); the second text, read where the first is silent, is the International Draughts Federation's "Official Rules of the game in draughts-64". Article numbers below are the first text's unless marked IDF. The judge is `Rooms\Support\Draughts\DraughtsRules`, reshaped from the pull request's `Checkers`, never mirrored in TypeScript:

    | Rule                                                                                                                                          | Article                       |
    | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
    | the dark squares only; a dark corner at each player's lower left; twelve men a side on the first three rows; white moves first                | 6.1.1, 6.1.3.1, 6.1.4.1       |
    | a man steps one square diagonally forward; reaching the last row it becomes a king                                                            | 6.1.4.3, 6.1.4.4              |
    | a king moves any number of free squares along a diagonal, never over its own pieces                                                           | 6.1.4.5                       |
    | capturing is compulsory, forward and backward, for a man too                                                                                  | 6.1.5.1, 6.1.5.3              |
    | a king captures a piece at any distance and lands on any free square behind it                                                                | 6.1.5.2                       |
    | among several captures the choice is free, whatever their number or kind                                                                      | 6.1.5.14                      |
    | a capture goes on to its end                                                                                                                  | 6.1.5.5, 6.1.5.6              |
    | a king that can go on capturing from some landing squares only must take one of them                                                          | 6.1.5.6 with 6.1.5.2; IDF 4.6 |
    | captured pieces leave the board only when the move is over; a square may be crossed again, a piece never jumped twice (the «Turkish strike»)  | 6.1.5.9, 6.1.5.12, 6.1.5.17   |
    | a man that reaches the last row while capturing goes on in the same move as a king; reaching it without a capture, it captures only next move | 6.1.5.15, 6.1.5.16            |
    | a side wins when the other has no piece left, has every piece blocked, or resigns                                                             | 6.1.7                         |

5. **The squares have their official names.** `a1`–`h8` (6.1.6.1): the files `a`–`h` from white's left, the ranks 1–8 from white's side, `a1` dark; white starts on `a1 a3 b2 c1 c3 d2 e1 e3 f2 g1 g3 h2`, black on `a7 b6 b8 c7 d6 d8 e7 f6 f8 g7 h6 h8` (6.1.6.2). A square is that name everywhere — a stored fact, the state, a fixture, the oracle's output — and never an index: `Rooms\Support\Draughts\DraughtsNotation` alone turns a name into a place of `DraughtsPosition` and back, and a page turns a name into a column and a row only to draw it.

### The choice: the clock and the seats

6. **A board game's level is its clock.** `content/rooms/levels.json` (version 5) gains the levels of `draughts`, each `{mechanic, difficulty, clock_ms, increment_ms}` and no other key: a level is "how much time", as the levels' own definition has it (ADR 023 decision 2). The host picks it with the same control as a difficulty (`DifficultySegments`), which shows the clock and never «Asan»; `rooms.setup_difficulty` and `room_games.difficulty` hold its number. A higher number is a faster game, as a higher difficulty is a harder one:

    | Difficulty | Clock | Each side                    | In the official texts                                                                        |
    | ---------- | ----- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | 1          | 10+5  | 10 minutes, 5 seconds a move | rapid: 7–15 minutes (6.1.9.2); «10 минут … плюс 3 или 5 секунд» (IDF competition rules 10.2) |
    | 2          | 5+3   | 5 minutes, 3 seconds a move  | blitz: 3–5 minutes (6.1.9.3); «5 минут … плюс 3 или 5 секунд» (IDF competition rules 10.2)   |
    | 3          | 3+2   | 3 minutes, 2 seconds a move  | the recommended blitz: «3 минуты плюс 2 секунды на ход до конца партии» (6.1.9.3)            |

    A new room takes `RoomDefaults`' difficulty, 2: five minutes and three seconds. `ContentValidator` requires of a board mechanic two to four levels numbered from 1 without a gap, `clock_ms` whole minutes from 1 to 30, `increment_ms` whole seconds from 0 to 30, a shorter clock at each higher number, and no key of a race level; a race mechanic keeps "all four or none". Another preset is a JSON entry.

7. **The two seats are part of the choice.** `rooms.setup_white_id` and `rooms.setup_black_id` (nullable, `room_players`) are facts the host wrote, like `rooms.setup_*`: only `ChooseRoomGame` — whose whole choice for a board game is `{mechanic, difficulty, white, black}`, two different present players of the room — and `StartRoomGame` (decision 8) write them. `Room::setup()` reads them through `Rooms\Support\RoomSeats`: a stored seat while its player is present, otherwise the present player who joined first and has no seat, white filled before black — so a room of two needs no choosing, and a seat whose player left is taken by the next. The state's `setup` carries them (`white`, `black`, a `room_players.id` or null), every device draws them, and the host changes them from the start screen on the phone or the screen. A choice with fewer than two seats does not start: `StartRoomGame` refuses it with `rooms.errors.needs_two`, and the start screen says so in place of «Başla». The choice of a board game has no rounds; `rooms.setup_rounds` keeps its number for the next race.
8. **White is the first seat, and the colours change with every game.** `StartRoomGame` copies the seats to `room_games.white_player_id` and `black_player_id`, the level's clock to `clock_ms` and `increment_ms` — so a running match keeps its clock when the content changes, as a race keeps `reveal_ms` — and then writes the room's two seats the other way round. «Yenidən oyna» is still `POST /rooms/{room}/games` with no body: the same two players, the colours changed. `setup.played` (ADR 026 decision 11) is, for a board game: the latest game is over, with this mechanic and level and these two players, whichever their colours.

### The facts and what follows from them

9. **Acts are the only facts of a match.** `room_acts` (`Rooms\Models\RoomAct`): one thing a seat did — `kind` (`Rooms\Enums\RoomActKind`): `move`, `offer`, `accept`, `decline`, `resign`, `leave` — with its player, the `ply` it was done at (the number of moves made before it), a move's `path` (the square it started on and every square it landed on) and `acted_ms` (`RoomClock`). Only `Rooms\Actions\RecordRoomAct` writes one: `POST /rooms/{room}/acts` `{event_id, game_id, kind, ply, path}` (`Rooms\Data\RoomActData::rules()`: a ULID; the game the device saw — as built in D5; one of the five kinds a device may send, never `leave`; `ply` for every kind but `resign`; `path` of 2–13 square names for a move, absent otherwise), in one transaction under the room row's lock, as the pull request's `PlayRoomMove` ordered it:
    1. the `event_id` is stored → `duplicate`;
    2. the room's game is not a live board game or not the game the act names, or the account is no present player → `late`; the player holds no seat → 403;
    3. by kind — a `move`: before `starts_ms`, not the seat's turn, another `ply` than the match's, or after the flag (decision 12) → `late`; a path that is no legal move → 422. An `offer` the seat may not make now, an `accept` or a `decline` without the other seat's standing offer at that `ply` → `late` (decision 15). A `resign` is taken while the match is live;
    4. otherwise the row is stored with `acted_ms` = now, `last_activity_at` touched, the revision raised and `RoomChanged` dispatched after commit → `accepted`.

    The answer is `{status: accepted | duplicate | late, revision}` (`Rooms\Data\RoomActReceiptData`, JSON, `no-store`). The player is the session's account: a body names nobody. `leave` is written by the server alone (decision 13), without an `event_id`.

10. **The position, the clock, the standing offer and the outcome are derived, never stored.** `Rooms\Support\Draughts\DraughtsMatch` replays a game's acts from the starting position — pure: the game's start facts, its acts and a moment in, everything else out — and every reader goes through it: the state, `RecordRoomAct`, the ranking, the taunts. No column holds a position, a turn, a count, a time left, a result or its reason. A captured square is not stored either: it follows from the path. A match has at most a few hundred acts; D3 measures the state of a 200-move match and «Keçmiş oyunlar» with fifty matches before it lands, and a replay may be remembered within one request, never between two.
11. **A move travels as its squares.** The path is the start and every landing, which names a capture's taken pieces without ambiguity, since each leg of it is one diagonal with exactly one piece jumped. A page offers only the paths the state gives it and computes none (decision 17, `legal`).
12. **The clock is each side's own, with an increment, on the server's clock.** `Rooms\Support\Draughts\DraughtsClock`, pure:

    ```
    starts          = room_games.started_ms                  // the start + elifba.rooms.countdown_ms, as a race's round 0
    since(p)        = p = 0 ? starts : acted_ms(move p − 1)  // when the side to move at ply p began to think
    left(side, p)   = clock_ms + increment_ms × (the side's moves before p)
                      − Σ (acted_ms(q) − since(q)) over the side's moves q < p
    flag(p)         = since(p) + left(side to move at p, p)
    ```

    A move is timed by its receipt under the room's lock (`RoomClock::nowMs()`), never by anything a device says; the increment is added once the move is made («плюс N секунд на ход», 6.1.9). A side whose `flag` has come has lost (5.4.2.6) — **derived**: nothing writes it, every reader sees it from that moment, a move received at or after it is `late`. The one exception of the official text, a flag that falls after a move that left the other side without a piece or a move (5.4.2.10), cannot arise: such a move is received before the flag and ends the game itself. There is no pause, no time added for a slow network and no clock of the device: the increment is what pays for the network.

13. **Who leaves, loses; who stops the game, decides nothing.** `LeaveRoom` and `RemoveRoomPlayer` call the driver where they call `RoomRoundClose::whenPresenceChanges()` today, in the same transaction (`SetHostPlays` is refused while a game runs, as before): a seat of a live match that is no longer present gets a `leave` act and has lost. A phone that vanishes without leaving loses when its clock runs out. `EndRoomGame` (the host's «Oyunu bitir») and `CloseRoom` set `room_games.ended_ms` as for a race: the match is over **without a result**.
14. **The outcome** (`Rooms\Enums\RoomMatchOutcome`: `white`, `black`, `draw`, `none`; its reason `Rooms\Enums\RoomMatchEnding`, the pull request's enum with the official cases) is the earliest of:

    | Ending        | Outcome             | When                                                              | Article             |
    | ------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
    | `no_moves`    | the mover wins      | after a move the side to move has no piece or no legal move       | 6.1.7               |
    | `resigned`    | the other side wins | a `resign` act                                                    | 6.1.7               |
    | `left`        | the other side wins | a `leave` act (decision 13)                                       | 5.4.2.9, by analogy |
    | `time`        | the other side wins | `flag(p)` has come                                                | 5.4.2.6             |
    | `agreed`      | draw                | an `accept` act (decision 15)                                     | 5.4.13.1            |
    | `repetition`  | draw                | decision 16, rule a                                               | 5.4.13.1, 5.4.13.2  |
    | `three_kings` | draw                | decision 16, rule b                                               | 6.1.8.3, 6.1.8.4    |
    | `main_road`   | draw                | decision 16, rule c                                               | 6.1.8.5             |
    | `balance`     | draw                | decision 16, rule d                                               | 6.1.8.6             |
    | `kings_only`  | draw                | decision 16, rule e                                               | 6.1.8.8             |
    | `stopped`     | none                | `room_games.ended_ms`: the host ended the game or the room closed | —                   |

15. **The draw offer is a fact, made as the official text says.** A player may offer a draw only after making a move (5.4.13.3): `offer` is taken from the seat that is **not** to move, from ply 1, while no offer stands. The other seat accepts without replying — `accept`, and the game is drawn by agreement — or declines in words (`decline`) or by its reply move, which ends the offer; nothing withdraws an offer. The same player may offer again only after the other has used the right (5.4.13.4): a seat whose last offer was not followed by an offer of the other seat may not offer. All of it is derived from the acts in order; the state says whose offer stands (`offer`) and whether the viewer may offer now (`can_offer`), so no page mirrors the rule. Not taken: the classical game's twenty moves before an agreement (6.1.8.1) and the six pieces of a rapid game played without a record (6.1.10.2) — a room's game is a friendly one whose record the server keeps.
16. **The draws the server declares**, as the arbiter who may record a draw without a player's claim (5.4.13.6) in a game whose record and clock the platform keeps (5.4.12.3). `Rooms\Support\Draughts\DraughtsDraws`, over the replayed positions; each is checked after every move, and the match is drawn at that move:

    | Rule | The official text                                                                                                                                                                                      | As the server counts it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
    | ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | a    | 5.4.13.1–2: the same position — the same placement of the pieces — arises three or more times with the same side to move; the repetitions need not follow one another                                  | the position after the move, with the side to move, has occurred **3** times in the game                                                                                                                                                                        |
    | b    | 6.1.8.3: three kings against one king, holding the main road, and the **15th** move does not capture it; 6.1.8.4: three kings and more pieces against one king, the same                               | a side has at least three kings — and any other pieces — against a lone king: drawn when that side has made **15** moves since each side's men and kings last changed and the lone king stands                                                                  |
    | c    | 6.1.8.5: three pieces (kings and men in any mix), none on the main road, against a lone king on the main road, and the **5th** move cannot capture it — "except a position in which one side can win"  | drawn at the stronger side's **5th** move in a row made while exactly that holds and the lone king stands. The exception is an arbiter's judgement and is not judged: "What is not built"                                                                       |
    | d    | 6.1.8.6: both sides have kings and the balance has not changed — no capture, no man crowned — for **5** moves in endings of 2 and 3 pieces, **30** in endings of 4 and 5, **60** in endings of 6 and 7 | both sides have a king, the pieces on the board are 2–3, 4–5 or 6–7, and 5, 30 or 60 moves — a move being one side's move with the other's reply, as the record numbers them (6.1.6.2), so 10, 60 or 120 plies — have passed since the last capture or crowning |
    | e    | 6.1.8.8: in endings of 4 and 5 pieces the players moved only kings, without moving a man or capturing, for **15** moves; in endings of 6 and 7 pieces for **25** moves                                 | the pieces on the board are 4–5 or 6–7 and the last 30 or 50 plies were king moves without a capture                                                                                                                                                            |

    The main road is the diagonal of eight dark squares (6.1.2), `a1`–`h8`. Where the text says «своим 15-м ходом», «своим 5-м ходом», the count is that side's own moves; where it says «на протяжении … ходов», «в течение … ходов», the text does not say whose, and the count is read as numbered moves — the reading the oracle's library also takes for its own rule — and D2 writes a fixture at each boundary. The first text has no rule for kings moving alone among eight pieces or more; the IDF's (7.2.5, fifteen moves whatever the material) is not taken, since repetition and the clock end such a game. A draw is said to the players with its rule (`rooms.match.ending.*`).

### The state, the protocol and the channel

17. **The state is `v: 6`, and the match is in it.** `RoomStateData::VERSION` 6, `ROOM_STATE_VERSION` 6; the game and the choice are unions by kind, and a board game — live or over — has its `match` where a live race has its `round`:

    ```
    RoomState { v: 6, …,                                           // every other key as in v 5
      game:  RoomGame | null,
      round: RoomRound | null,                                     // a live race, as before
      match: RoomMatch | null,                                     // the latest game when it is a board game, live or over
      setup: RoomSetup | null }
    RoomGame  = { kind: race,  id, mechanic, difficulty, rounds, phase }
              | { kind: board, id, mechanic, difficulty, clock: RoomClockLevel, phase }
    RoomSetup = { kind: race,  mechanic, difficulty, rounds, played }
              | { kind: board, mechanic, difficulty, clock: RoomClockLevel, white: id | null, black: id | null, played }
    RoomClockLevel { base_ms, increment_ms }
    RoomMatch {
      phase: countdown | play | over,                              // over exactly when game.phase is over
      starts_ms,                                                   // the clock starts: the end of the countdown
      sides: [RoomSide, RoomSide],                                 // white, then black
      turn: white | black | null,                                  // null: over
      ply,                                                         // moves made
      since_ms,                                                    // when the side to move began to think
      flag_ms: moment | null,                                      // when its time runs out; null: over
      position: [{ square, color, king }],                         // every piece, by square
      last: { path: Square[], captured: Square[], crowned: bool } | null,
      moves: string[],                                             // every move in the official notation, in order
    ```

### Points, taunts and the strip

21. **A match gives 3, 1 or 0** (the owner). `RoomPointsRule::MATCH_WIN` (3) and `MATCH_DRAW` (1), the pull request's constants; a loss and a game without a result give nothing. They are the game's points at its one close — its end — and enter `RoomRanking::ofGame()` and `ofRoom()` through the driver's "what each earned", where a race's answers enter: one ranking code path, each game netted on its own (`max(0, earned − taunts)`) as T2 built it. The official scale is 2, 1, 0 or 1, ½, 0 (5.4.7.1); the room's is its own.
22. **A taunt in a match costs what it costs in a race, paid from what the match gives.** `RoomPointsRule::TAUNT_COST` (1) is taken from the thrower's points of this game, counted at the game's first close after the throw — for a match, its end — and never below 0: a winner who threw twice gets 1, a loser pays nothing more than the loss. The **balance** — what a seat may still spend — is `MATCH_WIN − TAUNT_COST ×` the taunts it threw in this match, so at most three a match; a throw needs a balance of 1, read under the room's lock; `no_points` after that. The thrower is a seat and the target the other seat — the driver's rule; a watcher throws nothing and is thrown nothing. The window is the live match, its countdown included. The cooldown, the host's switch, the receipt, the list, the clip, who sees and hears one, `show_ms`, what is kept and forgotten: ADR 029 as it is, through `ThrowRoomTaunt` and `room_taunts`. `match.result.spent` says the taunts counted at the end, as a reveal's `spent` does.
23. **The strip shows the two seats, and "ahead" is more pieces.** `RivalryStrip` stands above the board from the countdown to the end, as it stands above a round. `rivalPair()` takes its rows from one function, `stripRows(state)`: a race's are the ranking's, a match's the two `sides` with `pieces` as their totals. A match is always a duel: a seat's phone shows [the viewer, the other seat], a watcher's phone and the screen [white, black]. Each side shows a piece of its colour beside the name and its clock in the names row, in place of a place label; 👑 stands by the side with more pieces and by neither when they are level; the gap says «:count daş öndəsiniz», «:count daş geridəsiniz», «:name :count daş öndədir», «Bərabərsiniz». Men and kings count alike: a count is a fact every player can check on the board, and a weight for a king would be an evaluation of ours. The sides stay sky and orange (ADR 027): a seat's colour is the piece beside its name, never the bar's.
24. **The strip moves when a capture arrives.** In a race it may move only in a reveal, because a ranking that moved earlier would tell of the open round. A match hides nothing, so its totals change with the move that changes them: after a capture the page **saw arrive** (decision 30) has been drawn on the board, the split slides and the totals count from the pieces before to the pieces now, as a reveal's do; a change of the leading side between two pairs the page drew is an overtake, with its ribbon. A page opened on a position draws the strip still. The rival's half is the taunt's button for a seat, as T4 built it.

### The final and the voice

25. **A match's final is the match's.** When a board game is over and its outcome is not `none`, `FinalStanding` opens `match.result.standing` — the winner first and the loser second, or both first in a draw, by the outcome and never by net points, each with the points the match gave it — and under it the room's standing as the plain card («Otağın sıralaması»). The rows a final opens come from one function, `finalRows(state)`: a race's are the state's ranking, as today. The confetti, the places' hits, the roll and the fanfare follow `finalTimeline()` unchanged. A game without a result has no final: the page says «Oyun dayandırıldı» and shows the room's standing.
26. **The cheer names who won the match.** ADR 030 as it is, over `finalRows()`: after the fanfare the first place's first name from the name list's clip and «Əla oynadın!» — after a draw both names and «Əla oynadınız!» — on the screen, or on every phone of a room without one. A taunt's clip is said as T4 has it. Nothing else speaks: no move, clock or result is voiced.

### Pages, motion and sound

27. **The pages draw a match with the parts a round has.** `rooms/show` in `RoomFrame` and `rooms/screen` follow the same `useLiveRoom()`; nothing fetches a match by itself, and no hook of a match keeps a clock or a sound player. One function per concern looks at the kind — `roomMoment()` (the moment), `phaseEnds()` and `waitsForOthers()` (when a page asks), `roomCues()` (sound), `stripRows()` (the strip), `finalRows()` (the final), `GAME_VIEWS` (`components/rooms/kinds.ts`: the live view of each kind on the phone and on the screen), `choiceParts()` (the start screen's controls and the choice's line) — and no component compares a mechanic with `'draughts'`. New parts live in `components/rooms/draughts/`: `DraughtsBoard` (the pull request's board, reshaped), `LiveMatch`, `ScreenMatch`, `MoveList`, `DrawOffer`, and the pure `taps.ts` (the pull request's `movesFrom()`, `nextSquares()`, `completeMove()`). The start screen is `GameSettings` with «Şaşki» as the seventh game under «Taxta oyunları», the level's segments showing clocks, and `SeatRows` in place of the rounds. docs/frontend.md rule 15 "Board games" has the layout, the sizes and the words.
28. **A list of the moves, in the official notation.** A quiet move is `c3-d4`, a capture `c3:e5`, and a capture of several pieces names every square it landed on, `c3:e5:g7` — the form the text uses when the direction must be shown (6.1.6.2), always, so that a line of the list is the path it stores; crowning has no mark (none in either text). The server writes each line (`DraughtsNotation::text()`); the page numbers them as the record does — white's move with black's reply is one number — marks the last, and never parses one: the squares it marks on the board are `last.path`.
29. **A match's moments** are the game, the ply and the phase (`roomMoment()`: `{game, round: ply, phase: countdown | play | over}`), so `sawBegin()`, `momentKey()` and `sameMoment()` serve it as they are.
30. **Motion follows ADR 025.** A move the page saw arrive — the state's `ply` is one more than the ply it drew — moves its piece along `last.path`, a leg at a time; the captured pieces go when it has landed, as the rule itself removes them; a man crowned on the way turns into a king where it crowned. A state that is more than one ply ahead, a reload, a late join and the screen opened mid-game are drawn still, at once. The viewer's taps are its own: a piece lifts, its landings show, «Geri» takes a tap back. A clock counts the side to move's `left_ms` down on the estimated server clock (`useServerNow()`), for show; it decides nothing. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` a piece stands on its new square, the taken ones are gone and nothing slides. Decisions 3 and 14 of ADR 025 hold in a match with nothing to hide: no fact of it exists that one device knows before another may. _Amended 2026-11-21:_ the board is wood and the pieces turned wood; a lifted piece rises over a shadow, a sliding one travels raised and settles as it lands, in 140 ms, when its sound plays ("Amended 2026-11-21" below).

## What is not built

- ~~A computer opponent~~ (built from K2: [036](036-the-computers-seat.md)), PDN export or import, a replay of a finished game, an analysis, premoves, takebacks, a pause, time added by a player, spectators' chat (the owner; ADR 024).
- The judgement clauses of the official text: «явно ничейная позиция» (6.1.8.7), rule c's exception, the rapid game's "theoretically won position without time" (6.1.10.5). They need an arbiter's eye; the mechanical rules, repetition and the clock end every game without them.
- Drawn openings and micro-matches of draughts-64 (6.2), other variants, another board game. Another one later is a `RoomMechanic` case of the `board` kind with its own rules class; nothing is abstracted for it now, and `room_acts`' `path` and the state's `match` are draughts' until then.
- Pairing a room's players into several boards at once, or a tournament table: one match runs at a time and the host seats the next.

## Consequences

- A room plays races and matches in any order, and its standing adds their points up: a race can give 200 and a match gives 3. The owner chose the match's points; a room that mixes the two will see matches weigh little. If that is wrong in play, the remedy is a standing per kind — a change of the page, not of the facts.
- Every read of a live match replays its acts. It is bounded by the rules — a game cannot go on without a capture, a crowning or a man's move for long — and measured in D3 (decision 10).
- A match costs a phone the network's time on its own clock. On a slow connection a blitz player loses seconds; the increment is the only allowance.
- The round games' pages gain `kind` in two objects of the state and nothing else; their fixtures change by those keys alone, which `RoomContractTest` holds.
- The contributor's move generator stays almost line for line, now proved against an independent library; what was rebuilt around it is the room's.

## Alternatives rejected

- **A table and a lifecycle of its own for a match** (`room_matches`, `StartRoomMatch`, `EndRoomMatch`; the pull request): a second copy of what `room_games` already carries — the start, the end, "what is running", the list, the standing, the taunts, the prune — each kept in step by hand.
- **A board game outside `RoomMechanic`** (`RoomBoardGame`, `rooms.board`; the pull request, whose reason was that every round-game reader would grow a case without meaning): with a second name for "which game", every reader of the game's **identity** — the choice, the catalog, the lists, the state, the page's names and drawings — grows a second field and an `if`. With one name and a kind, the readers of a **round** are reached only through the race's driver and never meet a board game.
- **A second state endpoint, typed or not:** two things to fetch, two revisions to hold together, two fixtures to keep in step; the match is small enough to ride in the state.
- **A stored position, turn, clock or result** (the pull request's `board`, `turn`, `quiet_plies`, `*_left_ms`, `result`, `ending`): each is a second truth beside the moves; a flag written "by the next writer" is a derived thing stored late.
- **A table of moves and columns for the rest** (the resignation and the standing offer on `room_games`): six more nullable columns, a history that cannot say who offered when, and no `event_id` for a tap sent twice.
- **Squares as numbers** — 0–63 (the pull request) or the library's 1–32: a convention every reader must share, where the official names need none.
- **Judging or generating moves on the phone:** against "the server alone judges"; the legal paths are sent instead, which costs nothing since the board is public.
- **The device's time for a move, or time given back for the network:** the server alone keeps the clock; an allowance a device can ask for is a clock it controls.
- **A taunt that costs time on the clock:** the clock would follow from two kinds of fact, and a joke could flag a player.
- **Free taunts in a match:** a voiced taunt every ten seconds for as long as a match lasts, and the one rule of a taunt — it costs its thrower — gone for one game.
- **A weighted material count on the strip, or an evaluation:** a number of ours that players would read as the server's judgement of the position.
- **The official text's draws as claims** (a button «Heç-heçə tələb et»): the count is the arbiter's work, which the server does for both players alike.
- **`pydraughts`, or any rules library, at run time or in CI:** the judge is ours and in PHP; a library decides nothing about a game played here, and the gate needs no Python.
- **A fourth preset to keep "all four levels or none":** a clock nobody asked for; the validator's rule for a board mechanic says what is true instead.

## What would make us revisit

A room that asks for longer games (a level more); matches drowning in a room's race points; a wish to watch a finished game again (the acts are all there); the official text amended; a second board game — then the `board` kind's driver gets an interface of its own for the rules, decided with that game in hand.

## Amended 2026-11-21 — whose turn it is

**Why.** The owner, after the matches on elifba.io: «şaşki oyununda növbəsi olan oyunçu xəbəri olmur, peşəkar şəkildə bu problemi həll edək» — the player whose turn it is does not find out. The design below was taken under the owner's standing rule of 2026-11-18 (the session's recommended options; "Taken without asking" at the end lists them). Nothing of the server, the state, the protocol or a race changes.

1. **One function says whose turn it is.** `yourTurn(match, viewer)` (`components/rooms/draughts/board.ts`, Vitest): true while the match is played (`phase: play`) and the viewer holds the side to move (`seatOf()`); false in the countdown and after the end, for the other seat, a watcher and the screen (no viewer). The band, the strip's clock, the tab's title, the wake lock and the seat's cues all read it; no component compares a seat with `turn` by itself, and none compares a mechanic with a name (decision 27).
2. **The seat sees it** (decision 30). In the line under the board, for the seat to move, «Sizin növbənizdir» — and «Sizin növbənizdir: vurmaq məcburidir.» — is a band: the line's whole 44px row beside «Geri», 8px radius, filled in the viewer's side colour of the strip — the left side, sky, where «Siz» stands on a seat's phone (decision 23, ADR 027) — in its deep shade, `rival-left-deep` (sky-700 in both themes; white words 5.9 : 1), with `Hand` and the words in white, semibold. Never right's or wrong's colour (emerald, red) or the lamp's (amber), and never colour alone: the words and the icon say it, and screen readers hear the line's `status` change as before. In the strip the viewer's own clock, while it runs, is drawn active: a 2px ring in the left side's colour round its figures, on the side's tint; under ten seconds the figures turn the wrong colour beside `Timer` as before, inside the ring. The band pops in once (`animate-room-pop`, 240 ms) when a turn the page saw begin starts — the clock's start for white, then each ply the page saw arrive (`RoomMotion.phase`); a page opened on its turn, a state fetched again and the seat's own taps draw it still, and under `prefers-reduced-motion` it stands still. 3. **The seat hears it** (decision 31). The seat to move's phone plays the room's cue of the moment its turn began — `go` as the clock starts (white's first turn), then the other seat's `move` or `capture` as its piece lands, and `crown` after it — and its own `clock` in its last 5 s, ending at its flag and stopped by its move, also when a screen carries the room: `roomCues()` gives that seat `hearMatch()`'s cues of the ply, as it gives a seat `offer` (D4 item 1), at a phone's level and under the phone's mute. In a room without a screen every phone already plays them (ADR 028 as amended 2026-11-19), and nothing is heard twice. The files are the room's, `move`, `capture` and `crown` as the owner chose them by ear: no new file and no new `RoomSound` case. A seat's phone fetches those five files (`go`, `move`, `capture`, `crown`, `clock`; the player's `seat()`) once it holds a seat in a live match, not the room's whole set. The seat that just moved, a watcher and the screen hear what they heard before: nothing new is played for the seat whose turn ends, and the countdown stays the room's.
4. **The seat feels it.** When the turn begins — as the other seat's piece lands, or as the clock starts — the seat to move's phone vibrates once, `navigator.vibrate(50)` (`BUZZ_MS`), where the browser has the API (Chrome on Android; Safari on iOS has none, and the page asks nothing instead), under the same mute as its sounds, only once sound is unlocked by a gesture and never on a hidden page; no new setting. It is a cue of the moment like the others (`{ sound: 'buzz' }` from `roomCues()`, the player's `buzz()`): a moment the page did not see begin — a reload, a state more than 4 s late — buzzes nothing, and a buzz waiting for its landing is dropped when the moment ends. It is the only vibration in a room: ADR 025 decisions 2 and 14 ("no vibration") are amended for this one.
5. **The phone stays awake.** A seat's phone holds a screen wake lock (`useWakeLock()`, the board's and the remote's hook) while its match is live — the countdown and the play — so it does not sleep while the other seat thinks; the browser drops the lock while the page is hidden and the hook takes it again when the page is shown. A watcher's phone, a race, the start screen and the final hold none.
6. **A background tab.** While it is the viewer's turn the page's title is «● Sizin növbənizdir» (`rooms.match.turn_title`; "● Your move"), restored to the room's title when the turn passes — the browser's tab strip and a phone's list of tabs show it.
