- **Why it is hard to reverse:** it renames what every player calls the area and prints a new address on every screen; it moves the host's choice from one page's memory into the room row and the state, which every device reads; and it lets the big screen write. Players learn the words and the address once, and a contract field is read by pages that may be open during a deploy.

## Context

B2–B7 built the adults' area as rooms: «Otaqlar» in the navigation, «Otaq aç» with «Mən də oynayıram» on its home, and the game chosen last, in a dialog on the host's phone (`components/rooms/game-setup.tsx`) that posts `mechanic, difficulty, rounds` to `POST /rooms/{room}/games`. Until the countdown no other device knew what would be played: the screen said «Oyun başlayanda burada görünəcək.» and a player's phone «Oyunun başlamasını gözləyin.».

The owner said on 2026-11-18 that the purpose is the game, yet the area presents itself as a room, and decided before any drawing:

1. The area is known as «Oyunlar»; «otaq» moves to the background and stays only where it is needed.
2. A game is chosen first; the room opens behind that choice.
3. After the choice there is one start screen — the game, the difficulty, the rounds, the QR and the code, who has joined, «Başla» — on the host's phone and on the big screen, driven from either. Playing without a big screen stays possible.

G1 drew three clickable prototypes (one HTML file each, not in the repository: the host's phone, a player's phone and the big screen on one state), which differed in substance:

- **A, «Vitrin»:** shelves by family, a page per game, then a start screen with every choice in one column; «otaq» kept in the host's tools.
- **B, «Bir ekran»:** the code field first on the home; one hub between games, where the game changes in place and the final opens at its top; «yarış» instead of «otaq».
- **C, «Dəvət»:** a «Hazır oyun» on the home; a start screen that is an invitation first — a large QR and the code — with the game, the difficulty and the rounds as one row that a sheet changes; no name for the room at all.

The owner chose **C**, with three changes — no «Hazır oyun»; the code field at the bottom of the home as in A, but compact, to type the code there; a camera icon inside that field, to scan a QR — and answered two more questions: «otaq» **stays in the background** (A's words), and the typed address becomes **`/oyun`, with `/otaq` still working**.

Two things constrain the design. A room still holds several games in a row with a standing across them, so ending a game and closing the room need two different names. And every rule of ADRs 020–024 holds: an account behind every name, nothing typed, nothing of a room shown before joining, nothing about a person kept across rooms, the server alone judging and keeping the time, a room page sending and hearing no client event.

## Decision

### Words

1. **Code names stay.** The `Rooms` domain, the `rooms` tables, `{room}`, `{member}`, the `rooms.*` routes and the `rooms.*`, `welcome.rooms.*` and `privacy.rooms.*` translation keys keep their names. Only interface text changes, and the entrance's address (decision 6).
2. **The rule of the two words** (G3). «Oyun» is what a person does: the area, choosing, the code, joining, coming back, leaving, the past list. «Otaq» is the thing that holds the players and several games, and is written only where a text must name that thing: the host's tools («Otağın idarəsi», «Otağı bağla», «Otaq kilidlidir»), the standing across games («Otağın sıralaması»), a closed room («Otaq bağlanıb», «Otağı sil», «Adımı sil»'s line), the player role's sentence, and `/privacy`, which says exactly what is stored per room. So **«Oyunu bitir»** ends the game that is running and the players stay together; **«Otağı bağla»** ends everything and kills the code. The two never stand on one screen: the first is in a round, the second on the start screen, on the final and in the tools.
3. **The host is «aparıcı»** ("host") wherever the interface names them; «otağı açan» is gone. The player role stays «Oyunçu», and its sentence stays word for word (ADR 019 decision 3): it is a stamped consent, and it is where «otaq» says exactly whom a name is shown to. The role's card introduces the word in its second sentence (the table below).
4. **Every text that changes** (G3), in `lang/az.json` and `lang/en.json`; a `rooms.*` key not listed here keeps its text. New keys are marked +.

5. **The READMEs** (G3). The section is headed «Böyüklər üçün oyunlar» / "Games for adults" (the links to its anchor follow), and its first paragraph says it the new way round: one of them chooses a game, a room opens behind it, and its six-digit code or its QR code brings the others in. 6. **The typed address is `/oyun`** (G2). `GET /oyun`, `GET /oyun/{code}` and `POST /oyun/{code}` are the entrance, under the same route names (`rooms.entrance`, `rooms.join`, `rooms.join.store`), the same limiter and the same refusals as §12 "Rooms and joining" has them when G2 starts; the QR is `{APP_URL}/oyun/{code}` and the line under it names `{host}/oyun` (`RoomJoinLink`). `GET /otaq` and `GET /otaq/{code}` stay in the player group as permanent redirects to them (`rooms.entrance.old`, `rooms.join.old`; a query is kept), so a link, a habit and a README of before still arrive; nothing posts to `/otaq`. The code space, `RoomCode` and what a code grants do not change, and `/pult` still does not know a room code.

### A game is chosen first

7. **Choosing a game opens the room behind it** (G2). `POST /rooms` (`rooms.store`, `throttle:rooms-open`) takes `{mechanic}` — `Rooms\Data\OpenRoomData::rules()`: required, one of the mechanics `RoomPlayable` lists that have levels — and calls `OpenRoom`. `plays` is gone from the request: the host is the room's first player, and stepping out is a tool (decision 12). Under the lock on the host's `users` row:
    - **the account hosts no open room:** a `rooms` row with a code, the host's player row, and the choice written into it — the mechanic, with the difficulty and the rounds of decision 10;
    - **it hosts an open room and no game is running there:** no second room. The open room's choice takes this mechanic and keeps its difficulty and its rounds, the revision rises and the room is signalled;
    - **it hosts an open room and a game is running:** nothing is written, and the redirect carries `rooms.errors.game_running`.

    Every case redirects to `rooms.show`. One open room per host stays the rule, and `rooms.errors.already_open` has no case left. An account that only plays in someone else's room opens its own as before.

8. **The choice lives in the room row** (G2): `rooms.setup_mechanic` (string), `rooms.setup_difficulty` and `rooms.setup_rounds` (small unsigned integers), nullable for a room opened before G2 (migration `add_setup_to_rooms`). It is a fact the host wrote — never text, never derived — and `Room::setup()` reads it as a `RoomGameSetupData`; a row without one answers with its latest game's, else with the first playable mechanic at the defaults of decision 10. Two actions write it and nothing else does:
    - `OpenRoom` (decision 7);
    - `Rooms\Actions\ChooseRoomGame` — `PUT /rooms/{room}/setup` (`rooms.setup.update`), the whole choice `{mechanic, difficulty, rounds}` by `RoomGameSetupData::rules()`, as before only a level `RoomPlayable` can play. Under the room row's lock it refuses a closed room and a room whose game is running (403, as `StartRoomGame` does), writes the three columns, touches `last_activity_at`, raises the revision and signals. It answers 204: every device, the one that sent it too, learns the choice from the state;

    `StartRoomGame` reads it, and a game that ends leaves it as it is.

9. **Starting reads the choice** (G2). `POST /rooms/{room}/games` (`rooms.games.store`) takes no body: under the room row's lock `StartRoomGame` builds the game from `Room::setup()`, and a body that still arrives is ignored. Two devices of the host can no longer start two different games, and what starts is what every device was shown. **«Yenidən oyna» is the same request**: the choice is still the game just played. «Başqa oyun» is `ChooseRoomGame` with another mechanic and the same difficulty and rounds.
10. **Defaults are derived, never stored** (G2). `Rooms\Support\RoomDefaults::for($host, $mechanic)`: the difficulty and the number of rounds of the newest game in the newest room this account hosted, when the chosen mechanic has a level at that difficulty and the rounds are inside `elifba.rooms.rounds`; otherwise `elifba.rooms.defaults.difficulty` (2, «Orta») and `elifba.rooms.defaults.rounds` (10). It reads rows the account's own rooms already keep for the retention period, is shown to that account alone, and goes when the room is deleted: no column on `users`, no profile, no statistic (ADR 022 decision 8).
11. **The state says the choice** (G2). `RoomState` gains one key, and the state's version becomes 2 (`RoomStateData::VERSION`, `z.literal(2)`); `room.changed` stays `{v: 1, revision}`.

    ```
    RoomState {
      v: 2, revision, server_ms, room, me, players, game, round, ranking,       // as before
      setup: { mechanic, difficulty, rounds, played: bool } | null               // null: a closed room
    }
    ```

    `Rooms\Data\RoomSetupData` on the PHP side, `RoomSetup` (a strict object) in `resources/js/rooms/contract.ts`, the type inferred from it. `played` is derived in PHP alone: the room's latest game is over, and its mechanic, its difficulty and its number of rounds are the choice's — "this is what was just played". A page shows the final while `setup.played` and the start screen otherwise; it compares nothing itself. Fixtures: every `tests/Fixtures/rooms/state.*.json` of an open room gains `setup` (`state.over.json` with `played: true`), and `state.chosen.json` is new — a game over and another chosen (`played: false`).

    **Who gets it:** the host and every present player, as the rest of the state — after joining. The join page still shows a code and the account's own name and nothing of the room (ADR 020 decision 4), and a player who left or was removed gets no state.

12. **Who may change it and start: the host, from any of the host's pages** (G2). `rooms.setup.update` and `rooms.games.store` are guarded by `RoomPolicy::start` — the host of an open room with no game running — and by nothing about the page that sends them. The big screen is a page of the host's own session (`RoomPolicy::screen`), so it sends the same two requests as the phone; it gets the catalog the phone gets, and no credential, route or policy of its own. A player's device can send neither (403). **«Mən də oynayıram»** is `PUT /rooms/{room}/plays` `{plays}` → `Rooms\Actions\SetHostPlays` (`rooms.plays.update`, `RoomPolicy::start`): under the room row's lock, `false` sets the host's own row to left, as `LeaveRoom` does; `true` clears it, or makes the row for a room opened without one — never refused by the lock, which stops new players and not the host, and refused when the room is full (`rooms.errors.full`). It raises the revision, signals and answers 204.

### The pages (G3; docs/frontend.md rule 15 "Games first")

13. **«Oyunlar»** (`rooms/index`): the six games as a grid of tiles, all on a 375 × 667 screen, each a button that chooses it (decision 7); above them a card for each open room the account is in («Oyuna qayıt»); at the bottom, stuck above the tab bar, the code field — compact, typed in place, with a camera button inside it and «Qoşul» beside it. No «Otaq aç», no «Mən də oynayıram», no «Hazır oyun», no past list. Its props: `catalog` (`RoomGameSetupData::options()`), `open` (each with the mechanic of its choice and whether a game is running) and `entrance` (the address of the code field, for the scanner).
14. **«Keçmiş oyunlar»** (`rooms/past`, `GET /rooms/past`, `rooms.past`, any player account): the past rooms as `rooms/index` listed them, on a page of their own with a second entry in the player's navigation. Nothing else about it changes: one row per room, the account's own place in that room, no total across rooms.
15. **The camera** (G3; pages only, no server). The button shows where the browser can open a camera (`navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia` in a secure context). The picture is decoded on the device — `BarcodeDetector` where the browser has it, else a decoder bundled with the app, an npm dependency under MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD or ISC, loaded as its own chunk from the app's host when the scanner opens. No frame, picture or decoded text is sent, stored or logged, and no third-party request is made (rule 9). The scanner accepts one thing: an address of this app (the page's own origin, or the origin of `entrance`) whose path is `/oyun/{six digits}` or `/otaq/{six digits}`. It never opens what it read: it takes the six digits and visits `rooms.join` with them, so a scanned code meets the route, the limiter and the refusals a typed one meets. Anything else is «Bu, Elifba oyununun QR kodu deyil.» and the scanner keeps looking; a refused permission is «Kameraya icazə verilmədi. Kodu əllə yazın.». The camera is stopped when the scanner closes, when a code is read and when the page is hidden. The rule lives in a pure `scannedCode()` with Vitest cases.
16. **The start screen** (`rooms/show` while no game is live and `setup.played` is false) is an invitation first: the QR and the code large, then the choice as one row — the game's drawing and name, «:difficulty · :count raund», «Dəyiş» — then who has joined, as chips, and «Başla · :count oyunçu» in the bar. «Dəyiş» opens «Oyun ayarları»: the six games, the four difficulties with what the level holds, the round steps; each tap sends the whole choice (decision 8) and «Hazırdır» closes. A player's start screen is «Qoşuldunuz», the same row without a button, how many have joined, «Dostunu çağır» (the code and its QR on the player's own phone) and the waiting line. The host's tools — «Mən də oynayıram», «Kilidlə», «Böyük ekran», «Otağı bağla» — are a sheet behind one button, «Otağın idarəsi».
17. **The big screen** (`rooms/screen`) before a game is the invitation as a poster — the QR, the code at its largest, the address, who has joined — over one strip with the choice, «Dəyiş» and «Başla». After a game it is the final beside «Yenidən oyna» and the five other games. It holds no lock, removal, closing or ending: those stay on the phone.
18. **After a game** (`setup.played`): the final as ADR 025 has it; for the host «Yenidən oyna» in the bar with a button for «Oyun ayarları» beside it, the five other games as chips under «Başqa oyun:», and «Otağı bağla» apart; for a player the final and «Aparıcı növbəti oyunu seçir.». Choosing another game, or changing the difficulty or the rounds, makes `played` false, and every device is on the start screen again — the standing so far as a quiet card at its top.

### What does not change

19. Everything else of ADRs 019–025, and in particular: the role and the switch; an account behind every name, shown under `users.name`; a code that admits and grants nothing else; nothing of a room before joining; no name, title, chat or free text; each device posting only its own answer; the server alone judging, scoring and keeping the clock; the derived timetable; the channel carrying `room.changed` and nothing else, and a room page sending and hearing no client event — a changed choice is a revision and a signal like a join; points, the ranking and what is kept; no total, rating or profile across rooms; a game's motion, sound and final; no text that promises a benefit; nothing of rooms on the children's side, and no room file importing the children's catalog — the idea of a catalog is borrowed, never a component (`RoomsApartTest`).

## Consequences

- From the home, a game is two taps away: a tile, then «Başla». Joining is the code typed where the thumb is, or one tap and a camera.
- Every device sees the game before it starts: a player knows what is coming, and the screen is of use from the first second.
- One more write path, guarded like the others: the host's choice is a row's three columns, changed under the room's lock, announced by the signal every page already hears.
- The screen can write. It is the host's session and sends the host's requests; it is still not a credential of its own, and a guest board's bearer still reaches no room (`BearerReachTest`).
- A page loaded before the deploy reads a `v: 2` state as it reads any state it does not know: G2 makes that a reload, once.
- Two words live in one area. The rule of decision 2 is what keeps them apart, and `RoomsWordingTest` still reads every text.
- The camera is a browser permission the area did not ask for before. It is optional, asked only when the button is pressed, and `/privacy` says what happens to the picture. An instance whose proxy sends a `Permissions-Policy` must allow `camera=(self)`; Elifba sends none.
- Existing open rooms at the deploy have no stored choice; `Room::setup()` answers for them, and they close by themselves within `elifba.rooms.idle_hours`.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Variant A** (shelves, a page per game, every choice in one column): one more page before the room, and a start screen that scrolls on a phone before the players are seen. Its words and its place for the code field were taken.
- **Variant B** (one hub, the final at its top, «yarış»): the QR sat behind a second tab on the phone, and «yarış» renames what every earlier text calls a room.
- **«Hazır oyun»** on the home (C as drawn): taken out by the owner. What it remembered — the last difficulty and rounds — stays as the defaults of decision 10.
- **No name for the room** («Bitir və bağla», «Ümumi sıralama») **or «yarış»:** the owner chose to keep «otaq» in the background. A thing with no name is hard to explain on `/privacy`, where it must be said what is stored per room.
- **Keeping `/otaq` as the shown address:** the one word a joiner has to type would be the word the area no longer says. **Dropping `/otaq`:** breaks links and habits for nothing.
- **The choice only on the host's page, sent to the others as a client event:** a room page sends and hears no client event (ADR 021 decision 8), a reload would lose it, and the screen could not start what the phone chose.
- **The choice in the broadcast:** the channel carries `{v, revision}` and nothing else.
- **A second room when a host with an open room chooses a game:** two codes for one evening; one open room per host is what lets a home page say «Oyuna qayıt».
- **The last choice stored on the account** (`users` columns, a settings row): something about a person kept across rooms. It is derived from rooms the account still has.
- **`StartRoomGame` still taking the choice in its body:** two devices of one host could start different games, and a start could differ from what the others were shown.
- **A «Hazıram» button for players, or the host approving who plays:** one more write and one more wait for a game among people who share a table.
- **Scanning through a hosted library, an upload of the picture, or the phone's file camera** (`<input capture>`): a third-party request, a picture leaving the device, or a photo saved in the phone's gallery. The phone's own camera app still opens the QR's address, as before.
- **The screen as a host credential of its own** (a code or a bearer for a shared computer): ADR 021 decision 11 and question 21 stand; the screen is the host's session.
- **Borrowing `GameCatalog` or its cards:** `RoomsApartTest` keeps the two sides apart on the page side too.

## What would make us revisit

Hosts asking for the choice to be kept per game (a difficulty for each mechanic), a seventh game and more — a grid of six fits a phone, a longer catalog would need the shelves of variant A — players who cannot find the word «otaq» when they look for how to close, or a browser that takes `BarcodeDetector` away.
