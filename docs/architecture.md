## 0. The product

Elifba is an open-source (MIT) web platform for primary-school teachers in Azerbaijan, and for parents at home. It teaches children to read Azerbaijani — letters, syllables (heca), words and sentences — through short games played on the classroom wall; children who cannot read yet play the same way with pictures: animals, fruit, colours, numbers and counting (R5, R6).

```
 classroom wall (projector)                     teacher's phone
┌────────────────────────────┐                 ┌──────────────┐
│  "Ə hərfini tap!"          │   tap "Ə"       │  [Ə] [E] [A] │
│   (E)   (Ə)   (A)   (O)    │ ◀────────────── │  [O]         │
│          ✸ burst ✸         │   ≤ 300 ms      │  At the board│
└────────────────────────────┘                 │  🐼 Aylin     │
   a child touches "Ə"                         └──────────────┘
```

1. A **teacher** registers, creates a **class** and adds **students** by pasting a list of names. Students have no accounts.
2. The teacher opens a **game** on the laptop connected to the projector — or, on a shared school computer, sends it there from the phone: the **board**. A game is one **mechanic** (find, read and tap, build a syllable, build a word, build a sentence) dressed in one **theme pack** (train, balloons, animals, fruit, our cities, national symbols) at one **level**.
3. A child goes to the wall and touches an item. At the same moment the teacher taps the same item on the phone (the **remote**). The board bursts the item and moves on — to the class it looks as if the wall reacted to the child. On a touch screen or with a mouse the board also works without a phone.
4. Every solved round gives the student at the board points. Each class has a **daily** and a **weekly** ranking; places 1–3 get a **podium** page that the teacher saves as a 1080×1920 image and posts to WhatsApp status.

At home (R2–R6):

5. Right after registration an account chooses a **role**: «Müəllim» (teacher) or «Valideyn» (parent). One account may hold both and switch between the teacher area and the parent area (§1 "Accounts and roles", ADR 010).
6. A **parent** adds their own **children** — a first name and an avatar, no class — and starts a game for one child on whatever screen is at hand: the phone or tablet the child touches, or the television with the phone as the remote. A 3–6 year old plays the **picture and number games** (the parent says «Pişik hansıdır?» and the child taps the picture; «Neçə alma var?» and the child taps the digit); a 6–10 year old plays the reading games (§2, ADR 011, ADR 012).
7. Every solved round gives the child **stars**. The parent sees each child's stars; there is no ranking and no podium between siblings (§5).

Owner decisions (fixed): name `elifba`; interface Azerbaijani first, bilingual foundation (az default + en) from day one; MIT unless the owner changes it (roadmap step O1); public GitHub repository; reuse ready-made packages where they fit.

Owner decisions of 2026-11-11 (fixed; the records say where each one lives): two roles, `teacher` and `parent`, chosen right after registration by e-mail or with Google (ADR 010); the roles come from `spatie/laravel-permission` (ADR 010); one account may hold both (ADR 010); a parent plays with their own children without a class, the 3–6 year olds with pictures and the 6–10 year olds with the reading games (ADR 011, ADR 012); stars and progress per child, no ranking or podium between siblings (ADR 011, §5); no recorded voice, the parent reads the prompt aloud (§3 "Voice"; reopened the same day after R1 and replaced by ADR 015: the board says the prompt and the praise in a prerecorded synthetic voice, and never a name; names reopened on 2026-11-12, below); the picture games are open to both roles (ADR 012); the numbers 1–10 mean recognising a digit and counting (ADR 012); colours are plain shapes drawn as our own SVGs (ADR 012, §8); the landing page and the READMEs get a parent section last (R7).

Owner decisions of 2026-11-12 (fixed; ADR 016, built in the step called V2): the board says a student's or a child's first name only when it is on Elifba's built-in name list of common Azerbaijani first names, voiced once, offline, in the board's voice and committed as content; any other name stays silent and is still shown; no name from the database ever reaches a speech provider, and an instance needs no key. The name is said before the praise after a solved round (a chosen student, or the home child), before «Salam!» at the start of a home game and before «Əla oynadın!» at the end of a game; never when «Kim oynayır?» picks a student, and never in rankings, on the podium or on the share card. A name's clip is the name alone, said before the phrase, the order the owner chose by ear (§3 "Voice").

## 1. Domains and data model

**Decision.** Code lives in `app/Domain/{Language,Content,Classroom,Family,Play,Rankings}` (`Family` from R3). A domain may use the ones before it in that list, never the ones after. Each domain has `Actions/`, `Models/`, `Enums/`, `Data/`, `Events/`, `Support/` and `Policies/` as needed. Only Actions write. Controllers, commands and channel callbacks validate, call an action and shape the response.

**Why.** It is the owner's proven shape (the owner's earlier Laravel project): agents find code by domain, a one-way order prevents cycles, and "only Actions write" gives every write one place to test.

**Rejected.** Laravel's flat `app/Models` + fat controllers: fine for a CRUD app, but the game rules (judge, points, calendar) would end up spread over controllers. A package per domain (modules with their own service providers): too much ceremony for six small domains.

| Domain      | Owns                                                                                                                                     | Tables                                                                          | Depends on                        |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| `Language`  | The 32-letter alphabet, Azerbaijani casing, normalization, syllabification, name sorting, ordinals                                       | none                                                                            | —                                 |
| `Content`   | Theme packs, levels, prompts, avatars, asset credits; teacher words; `ContentRepository`, `ContentValidator`, `TeacherPacks`             | `custom_words`, `hidden_words` (built-in content: files, §3)                    | Language                          |
| `Classroom` | Classes and students                                                                                                                     | `school_classes`, `students`                                                    | Language, Content (avatar keys)   |
| `Family`    | A parent's children (R3)                                                                                                                 | `children`                                                                      | Language, Content, Classroom      |
| `Play`      | Game sessions, rounds, answers, guest boards, remembered setups; round generation, the judge, points, pairing codes, the school calendar | `game_sessions`, `game_rounds`, `game_answers`, `guest_boards`, `setup_choices` | all four before it                |
| `Rankings`  | Derived scores: daily and weekly class rankings, the podium, a child's stars (R3)                                                        | none (reads Play)                                                               | Language, Classroom, Family, Play |

`Family` stands before `Play` because a game may be for a child (below); it uses `Classroom` only for the display-name rule. `Rankings` stays the read-only end: it derives everything from Play's rounds, for a class or for a child.

The account is the starter kit's `App\Models\User` (table `users`), kept where the starter kit puts it, with its authentication code. It was the teacher and nothing else until R2; from R2 it holds the role `teacher`, `parent` or both ("Accounts and roles" below), and R2 adds three columns: `area` (string, nullable: `teacher` | `parent`, the area an account with both roles is in), `teacher_agreed_at` and `parent_agreed_at` (timestamps, nullable: when the account accepted each role's sentence, §7). Before R2 it gained five columns: `locale` (`az` | `en`), `terms_accepted_at` (§7), `phone_remote` (boolean, not null, default true: whether the teacher board offers the phone remote, §4 "Pairing"), `board_sounds` (JSON, nullable: the sounds the account chose for its boards, a `content/sounds.json` key per moment, §3 "Sounds"; hidden from the page props), `google_id` (string, nullable, unique: the OpenID `sub` of the teacher's Google account, §7 "Google sign-in", ADR 008; hidden from the page props) and `email_changed_at` (timestamp, nullable: when the teacher last changed the address in their profile, so Google sign-in never takes over an account whose new address is unconfirmed). Its `password` is nullable: a teacher who signed up with Google has none until they set one. It is not a domain: every domain may reference `User`, and `App\Enums\Role` with it.

The share card (§6) is front-end only and needs no domain.

### Tables

All public identifiers are ULIDs (`HasUlids`), so URLs and channel names cannot be enumerated. Timestamps are stored in UTC (`APP_TIMEZONE=UTC`).

**`custom_words`** (`Content\Models\CustomWord`) — a teacher's own word, played in every theme pack (§3 "Teacher words", ADR 007, ADR 009)

| Column      | Type              | Notes                                                                                                  |
| ----------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `id`        | ulid              | how routes address the word; its text never goes into a URL                                            |
| `user_id`   | fk users, cascade | the teacher; the word plays in all of their classes                                                    |
| `text`      | string(32)        | normalized, as written: lowercase, or capitalised as a proper noun; ≤ `elifba.custom_words.max_length` |
| `word_key`  | string(40)        | `word:{lower}` (`Az::lower`), the content key the word plays under                                     |
| `syllables` | json null         | the teacher's override; null = `Syllabifier::split()`                                                  |
| timestamps  |                   |                                                                                                        |

Unique: `(user_id, word_key)`. Azerbaijani casing is computed in PHP and stored in `word_key`, so the database enforces "one word per teacher, case-insensitively" on every engine; `AddCustomWord` and `UpdateCustomWord` still check first, under a lock on the teacher's `users` row, to answer with a message.

**`hidden_words`** (`Content\Models\HiddenWord`) — a built-in word a teacher has hidden from their own games

| ------------ | ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| `id`         | bigint            | never in a URL                                 |

**`guest_boards`** (`Play\Models\GuestBoard`) — a board on a shared computer, claimed from the teacher's phone (§4).

| Column            | Type                                  | Notes                                                      |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `id`              | ulid                                  |                                                            |
| `code`            | char(6) null, unique                  | pairing code while unclaimed; cleared on claim             |
| `code_expires_at` | timestamp null                        |                                                            |
| `secret_hash`     | string                                | hash of the secret that only the board's browser tab holds |
| `user_id`         | fk users null, cascade                | the teacher who claimed it                                 |
| `game_session_id` | fk game_sessions null, null on delete | the game it shows now                                      |
| `claimed_at`      | timestamp null                        |                                                            |
| `expires_at`      | timestamp                             | creation + `elifba.guest_board_hours` (12)                 |
| `closed_at`       | timestamp null                        |                                                            |
| timestamps        |                                       |                                                            |

A guest board is **active** while it is neither closed nor past `expires_at`; an unclaimed one also needs its code to be valid, so it dies with the code (`GuestBoard::isActive()`, scope `active`). The secret is 32 random bytes (URL-safe base64, 43 characters); `secret_hash` is its SHA-256 — a fast hash is enough for a random secret of that size — compared with `hash_equals`. `secret_hash` is hidden from every serialization.

**`setup_choices`** (`Play\Models\SetupChoice`) — what a class or a child last chose on «Yeni oyun» (§2 "Remembered setup")

A row holds no name and nothing about a child (ADR 013): a mode, level keys, cases, letters and a flag. It belongs to its class or child and goes with it (cascade); its owner is theirs, so the setup routes' scoped binding guards it and no policy asks for a role.

### Status lifecycle

`GameSessionStatus::allowedTransitions()`:

```
live ──▶ paused ──▶ live
live | paused ──▶ finished    (last round solved or skipped, or the teacher ends it)
live | paused ──▶ abandoned   (no activity for elifba.abandon_after_hours, by the scheduler)
finished, abandoned: final
```

A session is created `live` by `StartGame`. Only `TransitionGameSession` changes the status; it locks the game row, sets `ended_at` and clears the join code when the game ends, and dispatches `GameSessionTransitioned` after commit (the event implements `ShouldDispatchAfterCommit`). Asking for the status a game already has changes nothing and dispatches nothing, so a repeated pause or end is harmless; any other change outside the diagram throws `Play\Exceptions\InvalidTransition`. The current round is derived (the first round neither solved nor skipped), never stored. `game_sessions` has no `created_at`/`updated_at`: `started_at`, `ended_at` and `last_activity_at` (set by the start, every claim, every applied sync event and every status change) say what happened. `seed` fits a signed 32-bit integer, the same on every engine.

### Accounts and roles

**Decision** (R2, ADR 010). An account holds the role `teacher`, `parent` or both. The roles come from `spatie/laravel-permission` (`HasRoles` on `User`), as roles only: no permission is defined, `register_permission_check_method` is off and teams are off. `App\Enums\Role` is the one list of names; code passes the enum.

A role opens an **area**; ownership still decides rows. Every authenticated route is in one of four groups:

| Group   | Middleware                                                | Routes                                                                                                         |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Teacher | `auth`, `role.chosen`, `verified`, `role:teacher`         | classes, students, class games (`/classes/{class}/games…`), rankings, the podium, teacher words                |
| Parent  | `auth`, `role.chosen`, `verified`, `role:parent`          | children, home games (`/children/{child}/games…`) (R3)                                                         |
| Shared  | `auth`, `role.chosen`, `verified`, `role:teacher\|parent` | the board page, the join code, the remote, `/pult`, the next game of a guest board                             |
| Account | `auth` (and what each route has today)                    | `/role`, `/start`, `/area`, the profile; security, appearance, play and roles settings also take `role.chosen` |

The board endpoints a guest board's bearer may call (`auth:board,web`: claim, state, sync, status, closing a board) and the public routes name no role: a bearer is not an account, and `GameSessionPolicy::play` and `GuestBoardPolicy` decide (§4).

- **`role`** is the package's `RoleMiddleware`, which routes write as `Role::middleware(Role::Teacher)` (`role:teacher`; `role:teacher|parent` for the shared group), never as a string. `User::roleList()` gives the roles an account holds in the enum's order, `User::area()` the area it is in. A wrong area is 403; the exception handler turns a page visit (GET, not JSON) into a redirect to `/start`. Route binding resolves first, so another account's id is a 404 whatever the role.
- **`role.chosen`** (`App\Http\Middleware\EnsureRoleChosen`) sends an account with no role to `GET /role`; a JSON or non-GET request gets 409 `{error: "role_required"}`. A guest board's bearer passes.
- **`GET /role`, `POST /role`** (`RoleController`, page `auth/choose-role`, `auth` only — before e-mail confirmation): «Müəllim» or «Valideyn», each with its sentence (§7). `GrantRole` takes `App\Data\RoleChoiceData` (`role`, `agreement` accepted), gives the role, stamps `{role}_agreed_at` and sets `area`. An account that already has a role is sent on to `/start`.
- **`GET /start`** (`StartController`): no role → `/role`; one role → its home (`/classes`, `/children`); both → the home of `users.area`. Fortify's `home`, `redirectUsersTo`, the `/dashboard` redirect, the Google callback and the logo all point here.
- **The second role**: Settings → «Rollar» (`GET /settings/roles`, `POST /settings/roles` → `GrantRole` again). Roles are never removed.
- **The switch**: `PUT /area` (`AreaController` → `SwitchArea`; `App\Data\AreaData`: `area` must be a role the account holds) stores `users.area` and redirects to that area's home. Only an account with both roles sees it, in the user menu.
- **Pages** get `auth.roles` and `auth.area` as shared props: `area` is the route's own area inside the teacher or parent group, otherwise `users.area` (or the one role). The shell draws one navigation per area from one definition (`components/app/nav-areas.ts`).
- **Registration** creates an account with no role, by the form and by Google alike; `terms` there is the privacy note only (§7).
- **Existing accounts**: the R2 migration makes every one a teacher (`teacher_agreed_at` = `terms_accepted_at`, `area` = `teacher`), with the query builder.
- **Factory**: `User::factory()->create()` is a verified teacher with the role, as every test before R2 assumes; `parent()`, `teacherAndParent()` and `withoutRole()` are states. A created account gets the roles whose agreement it has, the invariant `GrantRole` keeps. `tests/TestCase` forgets the package's cache before every test, as its testing guide says.

**Why.** Areas by middleware make the role a property of the route, which a test can enumerate; rows by ownership keep isolation where it already is and tested. One `/role` page and one `GrantRole` serve registration by form, registration with Google, an account without a role and the second role.

**Rejected.** A role system of our own; the role as a registration field; role checks in policies; permissions; one navigation for both areas; the area in the session (ADR 010 has the reasons).

`tests/Feature/Auth/RoleAreasTest.php` enumerates every route with `auth` and fails for one that is in no group.

## 2. Game model

**Decision.** A game is **mechanic × theme pack × level**:

| Part           | Is                                                                                                                    | Where                                                                                                  | Adding one needs                   |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| **Mechanic**   | The rules of a round: what is asked, how many hits solve it, in which order                                           | Code: a round builder in `Play\Support\Mechanics\*` and a Phaser scene in `resources/js/game/scenes/*` | Code                               |
| **Theme pack** | The look and the vocabulary: item style, backdrop, palette, effect, sounds, words with pictures, sentences            | Data: `content/packs/{key}.json` + `content/assets/{key}/`                                             | A folder and a JSON file — no code |
| **Level**      | Which unit (letter, syllable, word, sentence; picture, number from R5), how hard, how many items, which mechanics fit | Data: `content/levels.json`                                                                            | A JSON entry — no code             |

The server generates all rounds of a game when it starts, stores each as a snapshot (`game_rounds.payload`) and sends the same snapshots to the board and the phone. Neither client invents a round.

**Why.** Six themes × five mechanics × six levels give dozens of valid games; written as separate games that is unmaintainable, written as data it is one engine. Snapshots keep old games, answers and rankings valid after a pack changes, make the board and phone agree by construction, and let a reloaded board resume exactly where it was.

**Rejected.** A theme as code (one Phaser game per theme): every new theme would need a developer, and the owner wants teachers to contribute content by pull request. Generating rounds on the board: the server could not judge answers or resume a game, and the phone would need the same generator.

### Mechanics (code)

`Content\Enums\Mechanic` (with `units()`, `supports()` and `builds()`). The enum lives in Content, not Play: `levels.json` and `prompts.json` name mechanics and `ContentValidator` must check them, and Content may not use Play (§1). The rules of each mechanic stay code in Play (`Play\Support\Mechanics\*`) and in the scenes.

| Key              | UI name (az)                                          | Units                  | Round                                                                                                       | Solution                  |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| `find`           | Tap! («find!»; "Şarı partlat" with the balloons pack) | letter, syllable, word | A prompt names a target; 3–6 items float on the board, one is the target                                    | `any`, one item           |
| `pop`            | Oxu və vur («read and tap»; for beginners)            | letter, syllable, word | No target: the level's items float on the board, the child reads any one aloud and hits it; all are right   | `any`, every item         |
| `build_syllable` | Heca qur                                              | syllable               | Build the shown syllable from letters into slots (train wagons in the train pack)                           | `sequence`, its letters   |
| `build_word`     | Sözü yığ                                              | word                   | A picture; assemble the word (2+ syllables) from its syllables (+1 decoy)                                   | `sequence`, its syllables |
| `build_sentence` | Cümlə qur                                             | sentence               | Put 2–4 words (the level decides) in order, +1 decoy; the final punctuation stays fixed after the last slot | `sequence`, its words     |
| `count`          | Say («count»; R5)                                     | number                 | The prompt shows one picture N times; the items are numbers, one of them N                                  | `any`, one item           |

From R5 `find` and `pop` also take the units `picture` and `number` ("Units" below); at those levels the setup form names `pop` «De və vur» («say and tap»), since nothing is read.

**Units.** `Content\Enums\Unit` says what an item of a level is (ADR 012):

| Unit       | An item is                         | `text`                         | `image`     | `content_key`      | `reads()` |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------- | ------------------ | --------- |
| `letter`   | a letter                           | the letter, cased              | null        | `letter:{lower}`   | yes       |
| `syllable` | a syllable of the pack's words     | the syllable, cased            | null        | `syllable:{lower}` | yes       |
| `word`     | a word of the pack                 | the word, cased                | null        | `word:{lower}`     | yes       |
| `sentence` | a sentence's words (tiles)         | each word                      | null        | `word:{lower}`     | yes       |
| `picture`  | a word of the pack, as its picture | the word as the pack writes it | its picture | `word:{lower}`     | no (R5)   |
| `number`   | a number of the level's range      | its digits ("7", "10")         | null        | `number:{n}`       | no (R5)   |

`Unit::reads()` separates the reading units from the two for children who cannot read yet. Learned letters, the letter case, chosen `letters` and `own_words` apply only to a reading unit; a picture or number level ignores them all, so a class that has learned three letters can still play it. The board draws a picture item as the picture alone; its `text` is for the phone and for "item texts are unique".

Rules shared by all mechanics:

- Item texts are unique within a round, so a tap on the phone names exactly one item on the board.
- In a `sequence` round an item is a reusable **tile**: it stays on the board and a copy flies into the slot, so a solution may name the same item more than once: with the tile `nə` as item `a`, the word `nənə` is `["a", "a"]`; the syllable `nən` uses the `n` tile twice. Common first words (`ba-ba`, `nə-nə`, `də-də`) need this. The judge expects `solution.item_ids[progress.length]`. In an `any` round (`find`, `pop`) an item hit correctly bursts and is gone, and a wrong item wobbles and stays. Each wrong hit counts as a miss. A `pop` round has no wrong item: every item is in its solution, so it is never missed and is solved when every item is gone.
- Decoys for letters come first from the teacher's chosen letters, when given (so a round shows only chosen letters whenever at least the level's `items` are chosen), then from the letter's confusables in `content/alphabet.json` (e/ə, i/ı, o/ö, u/ü, s/ş, c/ç, g/ğ…) among the learned letters; for syllables and words, from the same pack and level; for a sentence's words, from the level's other sentences in the same pack ("The build builders" below).
- At a reading level only content whose letters are all in the learned letters — the class's, or the child's in a home game (§3) — is used; a game whose level has too little such content cannot start (`GameSetupData::rules()` says why). Picture and number levels do not read them.
- The same target never appears twice in a row.

### Item styles and effects (code, chosen by data)

A pack picks its look from closed lists implemented in `resources/js/game/styles/`: item style `balloon` | `wagon` | `card` | `bubble` | `animal` | `fruit` | `picture` (R5); slot style `wagon` | `frame`; effect `burst` | `sparkle`. A new style is code; a new theme that reuses the styles is data only.

**`picture`** (R5, ADR 012; `styles/picture.ts`; restyled for ADR 014's art in its session 3) is a white tile standing on a ledge of a palette colour — the tile's thickness, darker at its foot — with a soft shadow under it in the colour's deep shade, a face that fades to a tint of the colour at the bottom and a thin rim in that tint, drawn once per colour and size into a canvas texture. The item's picture fills it as far as the corners allow (0.84 of the side; Fluent's pictures fill their square) over a soft oval of shade that grounds it, since Fluent draws no shadow of its own; an item without a picture shows its text. The tile pops in and its picture follows with a bounce; while it waits the picture breathes — rising a little and swelling as its shade shrinks — each tile out of step with the others; a miss wobbles the tile gently (±4°) while the picture shakes its head; a correct hit makes the picture hop as the round's effect takes the tile (`burst`: the tile swells for 90 ms; `sparkle`: it lifts and fades whole in about a second, as every item does); when the round is over the tiles float away. A tile is at most 500 px, 0.86 of its column. `scenes/count.ts` draws the pictures it asks about in the same tile (`picture.draw()`), in the pack's first colour, so a counting round and a picture round look alike. `stage.ts` draws every round of the `picture` unit in it, whatever `pack.style.item` says — a picture item on a balloon or a wagon would hide the picture — and a pack made for pictures may name it as its own style (`colors`). It has no figures (`hasFigures()` is false), and its layout gives fewer, larger tiles: a picture level shows 2–4.

The code (A6, A8.2, A8.3): one registry, `styles/registry.ts`, maps each key to its implementation — `ITEM_STYLES` (`balloon.ts`, `wagon.ts`, `card.ts`, `bubble.ts`, `animal.ts`, `fruit.ts`), `SLOT_STYLES` (`slot-wagon.ts`, `slot-frame.ts`) and `EFFECTS` (`burst.ts`, `sparkle.ts`); a scene takes the item style from `pack.style.item`, the slot style from `pack.style.slot` (both through `stage.ts`) and the effect from `round.effect`, and asks the registry, never a style module directly. An item style (`styles/types.ts`) is `layout(count, band?)` (where the items rest and how big: across the 1920×1080 board for `find` and `pop`, or inside a band — a build round's tiles under its slots — sized to fit it), `stagger` (ms between items entering; 0 for wagons, which move as one train), an optional `scenery(band?)` drawn once (the train's rails), `draw(scene, text, look)` — a still picture of one item, which the build scenes fly into a slot — and `create()`, the living `ItemView` built on that picture (`styles/item.ts`, `BoardItem`): it enters, idles, wobbles on a miss, `pop()`s for `burst` or `lift()`s for `sparkle`, and leaves when its round is over. All drawing is code and Andika text in the pack palette; the only asset files are the figures of `animal` and `fruit` (below).

**The Fluent manner** (ADR 014; `styles/fluent.ts`). Every item style and both slot styles are drawn as ADR 014's own art is, so the pieces that carry letters sit among the Fluent pictures: no outline — an edge is where a lighter surface meets a darker one — light from the top left, each surface a radial gradient light → base → shade laid out in its own box, a shade from its bottom right and one soft highlight; shade turns towards red and violet and light towards yellow (`tones()`: a yellow turns only a little, so it stays yellow; a grey leans violet), never grey. A balloon darkens to violet at its rim and hangs on a string in its own shade, fading out; a wagon's roof and the frame under it are in its shade and its text's board is set into the body; a card is a surface in its colour whose border and bands show round a pale face set into it; a bubble is pale and see-through, its rim deepening into the colour; the figures' plate is a face set into a plate in its colour (`paintPlate()`, shared with the card, a filled frame, the solved unit's plate and sign, and `build_word`'s question frame). An empty slot is a pale well with its upper inner edges in shade (`paintRecess()`), a filled one lit in the colour, and the next one to fill has a soft glow behind it, pulsing. Parts not in a pack colour take the guide's materials: tyres, chassis, couplers and chimney its violet black, wheel discs, rails and the rod metal, sleepers wood, the lamp brass with a glow, the cab window sky, steam cloud. Shadows under a card, a plate or a frame are soft and in the colour's deep shade. White text sits on a soft shade of its ground's deep tone (`itemLabel()`), never an outline; which of dark or white text a ground gets is unchanged (`textOn()`). Each look is painted once per colour and size into a canvas texture (`texture()`, as `picture.ts` paints its tile), sizes rounded up to 8 px and the image scaled to the exact size, so the board never redraws a look per frame and a new tile count does not paint a texture per pixel of width; wheels turn as images whose shading is round their middle. Motion, sizes and layouts are as in the table below.

| Key       | Enters                                                     | Waits               | Miss                   | Leaves                           |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| `balloon` | rises from below                                           | floats and sways    | wobbles                | drifts up                        |
| `wagon`   | the train rolls in from the left, wheels turning, on rails | chugs               | rattles on its wheels  | the train rolls off to the right |
| `card`    | dealt from above, turning face up                          | hovers              | shakes                 | slides down                      |
| `bubble`  | grows from a small bubble low down                         | drifts and breathes | squashes and stretches | floats up                        |
| `animal`  | hops up from below the board                               | hops now and then   | shakes its head        | hops up and away off the bottom  |

**Figures** (`animal`, `fruit`). Each item is a picture — an animal or a fruit — standing behind a rounded plate with the item's text: a light face rimmed in a palette colour, over the picture's feet, so the picture never covers a letter. The layout decides each item's box and the item fills it: tall on the free board (`find`, `pop`), where the picture stands almost whole above the plate, compact in a build band (tiles, and the copies in their slots). The text is `card`'s size for the width (0.46), never smaller than `card`'s (for `animal`) or `bubble`'s (for `fruit`) at the same layout (Vitest pins it), is set on the middle of its glyphs (Andika's capitals with a dot or breve over Ş or Ç reach 1.21 em) and shrinks only to fit a long word (`böyürtkən`, `dəvəquşu`) on the plate. **Which picture.** A word of the pack stands in front of its own picture: the ostrich behind «dəvəquşu», the cow behind «inək». A letter (`find`, `pop`, build_syllable's tiles) stands in front of a word of the pack that begins with it — «A» behind the horse, the bear or the bee, «Ş» behind the peach in fruit — and a syllable (`find`, `pop`, build_word's tiles) in front of a word whose first syllable it is — «al» behind the apple, the sour cherries or the plums (al-ma, al-ba-lı, al-ça), «a» behind the pineapple (a-na-nas), not the apple — or, when it begins none, a word it is a later syllable of, since a closed syllable often ends a word («ruz» behind the rooster, xo-ruz; «şik» behind the cat); chosen by the round id, never the round's own picture, and alone, with no picture, when no word of the pack begins with it (animals have none for «Ə», «O», «U»…, fruit none for «ma»): a wrong animal there would teach a wrong initial (owner, 2026-11-10). So a build_word tile whose only word is the question (`li` and `mon` of «limon») stands alone. Only a word without a picture of its own — a sentence's other words, a teacher's word — stands in front of one of the pack's figures that shows none of the round's words: not a figure whose word is what an item says or begins it (`word:quşlar`, `word:narı` in a sentence, `syllable:nar`), not a picture already behind one of the round's words, not the round's own picture (`prompt.image`, build_word's question, compared by path); no figure twice in a round while unused ones remain, and the same choice for the same round id, so a reload shows the same pictures (`figures.ts`, a pure function). A picture behind an item reads as that item's picture, so another animal there (a rabbit over «dəvəquşu») would teach the wrong name; the owner chose the right picture (2026-11-10) knowing that in `find` a child who hears the target can spot its picture without reading the plates. **The data.** The pictures are data, not code: `BoardProps` sends `pack.pictures`, the pictures that show a content key — a built-in word's own, for a letter those of the pack's words it begins, and for a syllable those of the words it begins or else those it is a later syllable of (taken on the server: `Az::lower`, `WordData::$syllables`); a letter or syllable that is not listed stands alone — and `pack.style.figures`, which the pack lists (§3: its own word pictures or other pictures of the pack's set or own art, never an avatar, which marks a student, §7), each with the content keys of the words it shows (`{url, words: ["word:ayı"]}`); both are empty for the other styles. The scene rasterises them once in `preload()` at the largest size an item draws them (`load.svg` with a size), so `draw()` reuses them for the build copies. The validator asks for at least twice as many figures as the most items or tiles a round of any level can show, so the choice never runs short.

`burst` swells the item and breaks it into shards of its colour with a ring of air. `sparkle` never breaks anything (§8): the item glows, lifts and fades away whole while four-pointed stars in its colour and white twinkle over it and rise with it.

A slot style (A8.3) draws a build round's slots: `create(scene, {count, band, aspect, tail, palette})` gives a `SlotView` — `spot(i)` (where slot `i` is now, and the box a copy fits in; `aspect` is the item style's height ÷ width, so a slot hugs the copy it takes), `enter()`, `progress(filled)` (filled slots solid, the next one glowing), `complete(text)` (the unit whole) and `leave()` — plus an optional `scenery(band)` drawn once. The build scene keeps each landed copy on its slot's `spot()` every frame, so the copies ride with a moving train without belonging to it.

| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| `wagon` | empty wagons coupled behind a locomotive that leads on the left, on their own track; the train rolls in from the right, puffs and chugs | a short end car carrying the mark | the wagons turn solid and carry one long sign with the unit | the train pulls away leftwards |
| `frame` | plain rounded frames in a row, popping in                                                                                               | the mark after the last frame     | the frames give way to one plate with the unit              | floats up and fades            |

In both the next slot to fill glows. A correct hit flies a copy of the tile (`draw()`) into it and the tile stays; when the last copy lands the round's effect takes the copies — they burst, or sparkle away whole for `symbols` and `cities` — and the slots show the unit whole: «TUT», «nənə», «Tar gözəldir.».

Default looks: balloons → `balloon`; train → `wagon` (slots `wagon`); animals → `animal` with `sparkle` — an animal is never broken; our cities → `card` with `sparkle`; fruit → `fruit`; national symbols → `card` with `sparkle` — a state symbol is never burst (§8).

The closed lists that data names are enums in `Content\Enums`: `Unit`, `Mechanic`, `ItemStyle`, `SlotStyle`, `Effect`, `Sound` (`hit` | `miss` | `solved` | `arrive` | `leave`, the keys of a pack's `style.sounds`; `Sound::choosable()` names the first three, the moments an account may choose a sound for, §3 "Sounds") and `License` (§8).

### What the board can draw

**Decision.** `Play\Support\Playable` is the one server-side list of what the board can draw: the mechanics with a round builder and a scene (`mechanics()`, `builder()`), and the item styles, slot styles and effects implemented in `resources/js/game/styles/` (`itemStyles()`, `slotStyles()`, `effects()`). A6 draws `find` in the `balloon` style with the `burst` effect, so "Şarı partlat" (find × balloons) is the only game that can start; A8 extends the lists: A8.1 lists the three build mechanics with their builders (below), which stay `not_playable` while `slotStyles()` is empty; A8.2 adds the item styles and `sparkle` (every key of `ItemStyle` and `Effect`, so `find` plays in all six packs), A8.3 the slot styles `wagon` and `frame` (every key of `SlotStyle`). Since A8.3 every mechanic plays in every pack: with all 32 letters learned, all 90 options of the setup page (15 mechanic × level pairs × 6 packs; 60 before `pop` joined the five letter, syllable and word levels) start, and `POST /classes/{class}/games` starts each build mechanic in each pack (`GameSetupTest`). No pack reaches `not_playable` today; it stays the answer for a key added to a closed list before the board draws it. `tests/Fixtures/playable.json` pins the three lists (`PlayableTest`), and Vitest (`styles/registry.test.ts`) checks every key in it has its code in `styles/registry.ts`, and nothing more. A pack is playable with a mechanic only when the board draws its item style and its effect (and its slot style for a build mechanic; `find` and `pop` have no slots, `! Mechanic::builds()`). `pop` needs no scene of its own: `stage.ts` gives it the `find` scene, which already bursts each correct item and keeps the rest. `Playable::reason()` says why a mechanic × pack × level cannot start for a class's learned letters (the level lacks the mechanic → from R5, the pack does not list the level's unit, `pack_level` → letters chosen for a non-letter level → own words asked where they do not play → the board cannot draw it → no own word in the pack → the builder's content shortage), as a translated message; `GameSetupData::rules()` (through the `StartableGame` rule on `level`) and the setup page's `options` (`Playable::options($teacher, $learned)`) ask exactly that question, both about the pack as the class's teacher plays it (`TeacherPacks`, §3 "Teacher words"), which `StartGame` then builds from. Rounds carry the pack's own effect, so the `symbols` and `cities` packs (`sparkle`) are never burst (§8).

R5 adds `count` to `mechanics()` (with `CountRoundBuilder` and the scene `scenes/count.ts`) and `picture` to `itemStyles()`; `playable.json` and `styles/registry.ts` follow. `Playable::options()` lists a level with a pack only when the pack lists the level's unit (`PackData::$units`, §3), so the setup page never shows «Cümlələr» for the colours or a picture level for the cities; a request built by hand gets `pack_level`. `takesOwnWords()` stays false for the new units. The "class" in this section is whoever the game is for from R3: `Playable` takes the owner and the learned letters, and `GamePlayers` (below) supplies them for a class or a child.

**Why.** A game the board cannot draw must not start, and the setup page must grey out exactly what the rules refuse; one list read by both cannot drift. Keeping the list in Play, not in the content, means a pack contributor never has to know which styles exist yet.

**Rejected.** A `playable` flag in each pack's JSON: content would have to change when code does, and a pack could claim a style the board lacks. Hiding packs in the setup page only: a request built by hand (or A7's phone) would still start an undrawable game.

`pop` («Oxu və vur», «De və vur» at a picture or number level) comes first in each list, so it is the setup page's default; `find` follows it, and `count` comes last at the number levels.

The picture and number levels (ADR 012) are offered only with a pack that lists their unit (§3 "Where content lives": `units`): `pictures-easy` and `pictures` with `animals`, `fruit` and, from R6, `colors`; the number levels with `animals` and `fruit` (the cats and the apples are counted) and, from R6, `numbers`. Their titles are «Şəkillər: asan», «Şəkillər», «1-dən 5-ə», «1-dən 10-a». The setup form groups levels by `Unit::reads()` — «Şəkilli oyunlar» and «Oxu oyunları» — and puts the picture group first in the parent area and the reading group first in the teacher area; both roles get both groups.

Syllables are not listed by hand: they come from the pack's words through the syllabifier (§3), so every pack has syllable levels for free. The titles shown to teachers are "Qısa hecalar" / "Uzun hecalar" (short / long syllables: V, CV, VC have one or two letters, the closed shapes three or four), "Qısa sözlər" / "Uzun sözlər", "Hərflər", "Cümlələr".

A level entry (`Content\Data\LevelData`):

```json
{
    "key": "words-short",
    "title": { "az": "Qısa sözlər", "en": "Short words" },
    "unit": "word",
    "syllables": { "min": 1, "max": 2 },
    "mechanics": ["pop", "find", "build_word"],
    "items": 4,
    "decoys": 1
}
```

- Each unit has exactly one filter: `shapes` (syllable shapes as `Syllabifier::shape()` names them) for syllable levels, `syllables` (`min`/`max` per word) for word levels, `words` (`min`/`max` per sentence, within 2–4) for sentence levels, `numbers` (`min`/`max`, within 1–10, `min` < `max`) for number levels (R5), none for letter and picture levels.
- `mechanics` must suit the unit (`Mechanic::supports()`); their order is the order the setup form offers them in, and the first one the class can start is its default: «Oxu və vur» (`pop`) leads, the beginners' game; `items` (2–8, the items a `find` or `pop` round shows) is present exactly when the level has a mechanic that does not build (`find`, `pop`: `! Mechanic::builds()`); `decoys` (0–4, the decoy tiles a build round adds) exactly when it has a build mechanic.
- `LevelData::keysFrom($pack, $letters)` lists the distinct items of a pack that fit the level and the learned letters, as content keys; the round builders (A6, A8) start from it. For a picture level (R5) they are the `word:` keys of the pack's words that have a picture, and for a number level the `number:{n}` keys of its range; neither reads `$letters`.

### Game setup

**Who a game is for** (R3, ADR 011). `Play\Data\GamePlayers` is the one thing Play knows about it: `forClass(SchoolClass)`, `forChild(Child)` and `of(GameSession)` give the owner (`User`), the learned letters (`learned()`: the class's or the child's, all 32 when null), and the class or the child. `GameSetupData::rules()` and `validate()`, `GameSetupData::learned()`, `StartGame::handle()`, `GameSetupOptions` and `StartableGame` take a `GamePlayers` where the text below says "class"; a class game starts at `/classes/{class}/games`, a home game at `/children/{child}/games` (the same controller, form and rules). Nothing else in a setup differs between the two.

`Play\Data\GameSetupData::rules($class)`: `mechanic`, `pack`, `level` (must allow the mechanic, and the game must be playable with enough content for the class: `Playable::reason()`), `rounds` (5–30, default 10; the setup page sends none since 2026-11-13, so a new game has 10, and the board changes the count before «Başla», §4 "Round count"), `letter_case` (`upper` | `lower`, default `upper` for a letter level, `lower` otherwise), optional `letters` (a subset of the class's learned letters, only for a letter level; at least two, so targets can alternate), optional `own_words` (boolean, default false: play only the teacher's own words in the pack, §3 "Teacher words"; only where `Playable::takesOwnWords()` — syllable and word levels except `build_word` — else `own_words_level`; with no own word in the pack `no_own_words`, with too few the usual `too_few_*`). Every refusal is a translated message on its field (`games.setup.errors.*`), and the content shortages name the counts ("… :found heca var, ən azı :count lazımdır"). An archived class is hidden from games: its setup page and `POST /classes/{class}/games` answer 404.

At a picture or number level (R5) `letter_case` is not offered and is stored as `lower` — the content as written — and `letters` and `own_words` are refused as at any level they do not belong to.

**What the catalog receives** (`Play\Support\GameSetupOptions`, one builder for the setup page and the guest board pages; the catalog is docs/frontend.md rule 13, chosen by the owner on 2026-11-13, which replaced the form of three questions of 2026-11-11). `shared()`: `packs` as `{key, title, cover, palette}` — `cover` the URL of the pack's `style.cover` (§3, `ContentAssets::url()` with the pack's version, as a backdrop's) and `palette` its `style.palette`, which the card's scene is drawn in; no other `style` (only the board needs it); `levels` as `{key, title, unit, mechanics, hint}` — `hint` one line the server builds from the level's data in the interface language (`games.setup.level_hints.{unit}`: «lövhədə :count hərf», «:min–:max hərfli hecalar» from the lengths of its syllable shapes, «:min–:max hecalı sözlər», «:min–:max sözlü cümlələr», «lövhədə :count şəkil», «lövhədə :count rəqəm»; `:count` is `items`), so TypeScript never composes it; `games` as `{unit, mechanic, mode, name, hint}`, one per (unit, mechanic) some level offers, in catalog order (`Unit` order, then the level's mechanics) — `mode` `Play\Enums\SetupMode::of($mechanic)` (`easy` for `pop`, `normal` for every other mechanic), `name` `games.names.{unit}_{mechanic}` («Heca qur») and `hint` `games.setup.mechanic_hints.{mechanic}` (`pop_say` where the unit does not read), both in the interface language; `letterCases` and `wordMaxLength` as before (`rounds` went with the form's round count, 2026-11-13; the board's limits are `BoardProps.roundLimits`, §4 "Round count"). `for($players)`: `options` as before, each with `code` — the key of its reason under `games.setup.errors.*` (`too_few_syllables`…), null when it can start — and `own.code` likewise (`Playable::message()` makes every reason, of `reason()` and of the builders' `shortage()`, and remembers its key; `Playable::code($reason)` gives the key back, so the two cannot drift); `cards` as `{pack, unit, mechanic, title, sample}`, one per pack and game the content offers (the pack lists the unit and a level of the unit lists the mechanic), in the packs' order within each game — `title` the card's name, `__('games.titles.{unit}_{mechanic}', ['with' => the pack's `with` in the interface language])` («Qatarla cümlə qur», "Build a sentence with the train"; §3, so the instrumental case is content, written once per pack, and the word order belongs to each language's key), and `sample`, at most three strings the card's scene shows as the board would (`Play\Support\CardSamples`, with no randomness, so the props are stable): at the card's default level, from the pack's built-in content the owner has not hidden (a teacher word is never a sample) and the players' learned letters, cased by `LetterCase` with the remembered case — for `pop` and `find` three items (letters, syllables or words; at a number level three digits of the level's range), for `build_syllable` the letters of its first syllable, for `build_word` the syllables of its first word, for `build_sentence` the words of its first sentence, for `count` three digits of the level's range, and none at a picture level or when the card cannot start; `letters` as before; `allLetters`, true when the class or child has not narrowed its learned letters (all 32); and `remembered` (below). No recent games: the owner took «Son oyunlar» out on 2026-11-11. Not built yet: `BoardProps` is to gain `title` (the card's name, built as above) and `level: {key, title, unit}` (title in the interface language) for the start screen, which will say the card's name over «:level · :count raund» (docs/frontend.md rule 13).

**Remembered setup** (the owner, 2026-11-13: a choice made once is kept). Each class and each child has at most one `setup_choices` row (§1). `GameSetupOptions::for($players)` sends it as `remembered: {mode, levels, letterCases, letters, ownWords}`, filtered on the way: `mode` is the row's, else `normal` for a class and `easy` for a child; `levels` keeps only level keys that still exist for their unit; `letters` keeps only letters still learned, and is null when fewer than two remain; `ownWords` is null where never chosen. The catalog decides from it which cards show and how a game page opens (docs/frontend.md rule 13); whether the result can start is still `options[].startable`. Two writes, both actions: `Play\Actions\RememberSetupMode` (`Play\Data\SetupModeData`: `mode` in `SetupMode`) from `PUT /classes/{class}/games/mode` (teacher area) and `PUT /children/{child}/games/mode` (parent area), with scoped binding as the setup page's (an archived class answers 404), answering with a redirect back that the catalog sends with `preserveScroll`; the phone's guest board uses the same routes for the chosen players, and both routes join the isolation test. And `StartGame`, which in the transaction that stores the game also stores its setup through `Play\Support\SetupMemory::remember()`: the mode of its mechanic, its level under its unit, its letter case under its unit (reading units), its `letters` (the letter level) and its `own_words` (only where `Playable::takesOwnWords()` and a game of the owner's own words could start, so «Hamısı» was a choice and a teacher without words is not remembered as having refused them). `ReplayGame` starts through `StartGame`, so a replay remembers the same; `ChangeRoundCount` remembers nothing (the round count is the board's). `SetupMemory::recall()` reads the row; `SetupMode::first()` is the mode before any.

`LetterCase` (`Play\Enums`): `upper` is every letter capital with Azerbaijani casing (`Az::upper`: i → İ, ı → I); `lower` is the content as written — letters and syllables lowercase, a word as the pack spells it, so a proper noun keeps its capital ("Bakı"), the syllables of a `build_word` round as split from that word ("Ba", "kı"), a sentence's words as the sentence writes them ("Tar", "gözəldir"). Text is cased by the builder on the server, never on the board.

`StartGame` takes the pack as the class's teacher plays it (`TeacherPacks::pack()`, §3 "Teacher words": the built-in words the teacher has not hidden, then the teacher's own words), hands it to the mechanic's builder (`RoundBuilder::build($setup, $pack, $learned, $random)`), builds every round with `Random\Randomizer` on `Mt19937($seed)` (a random seed unless a test gives one, stored on the game), so the same setup, learned letters, teacher's pack and seed give the same rounds (only the round ids differ), and stores the game (`live`, `board_epoch` 0, the class's teacher as owner) with its round snapshots in one transaction. From R3 the game is stored for the class or for the child of its `GamePlayers` (the owner is the teacher or the parent), and each round row copies the same id. From 2026-11-13 the game also stores the rest of its setup, `letters` and `own_words`, so its rounds can be built again: `GameSetupData::inputOf($game, $rounds)` gives the setup a stored game was started with (a game stored before has null and false), `StartGame::build()` the pack and rounds a seed gives with it, and `StartGame::storeRounds()` stores them; `ChangeRoundCount` (§4 "Round count") and `ReplayGame` use them.

**«Yenidən oyna»** (owner, 2026-11-13). The result screen of a teacher board or a home board starts the same game again: `POST /games/{game}/replay` (shared area, `update` — the owner only; never on a guest board, whose result waits for the phone's next game). `ReplayGame` builds the setup from the game — mechanic, pack, level, letter case, letters, own words and as many rounds as it had, kept within `elifba.rounds.min`–`.max` — validates it with `GameSetupData::rules()` for the same `GamePlayers` and calls `StartGame` with a new seed, so the rounds differ; the answer is a redirect to the new game's board. A game still live or paused is sent back to its own board; a setup that can no longer start (the learned letters narrowed, words hidden) sends the owner to the setup page of the same class or child with the reason as a toast (`games.board.replay_refused`); an archived class's game answers 404, as its setup page does.

### The `find` builder

`Play\Support\Mechanics\FindRoundBuilder` (behind the `RoundBuilder` interface that the build builders implement too). Everything below but the solution and the prompt lives in `AnyRoundBuilder`, which the `pop` builder shares (next section):

- **Content.** A letter level takes the class's learned letters (targets: the teacher's `letters` among them when given; the chosen letters also fill the round first, see Decoys); syllable and word levels take `LevelData::keysFrom($pack, $learned)` of the teacher's pack. The pool must hold at least the level's `items` (4) and two targets; otherwise the game cannot start.
- **Targets** go round in shuffled decks of every target; a new deck never starts with the target that ended the last one. Every target comes up equally often (±1) and none twice in a row. The deck, the item ids and the content as written live in `RoundParts` (`deck()`, `itemId()`, `value()`, `written()`), which the build builders share.
- **Decoys** for a letter, the first `items` − 1 of: (1) its confusables among the teacher's chosen `letters` (`Alphabet::confusables()`, most confusable first), (2) the other chosen letters at random, (3) its confusables among the learned letters, (4) other learned letters at random. So with at least `items` (4) letters chosen a round shows only chosen letters (a, ə, o, ö → every round is exactly those four); with fewer, the learned letters fill the rest, confusables first. Without chosen letters only (3) and (4) apply. For syllables and words, other items of the same pack and level at random. Item ids are `a`, `b`, `c`… in display order; the target's position is random.
- **No pictures.** `find` items carry `image: null`, and so does the prompt: a picture next to the target text, or on the items, would let a child match pictures instead of reading. The prompt shows the target as text (`show`, filled into `prompts.json`'s template with `PromptTemplateData::fill()`). Pictures belong to `build_word` (A8), where the picture is the question.
- **Sample rounds.** The welcome page's wall (frontend.md rule 10) plays rounds from this builder: `Play\Support\SampleRounds` sets up `find` at the first letter level with `elifba.rounds.min` rounds, upper case and every letter learned, in one built-in pack, with a seed from the Baku date. The pack and the letters change once a day. There is no teacher, so the pack comes from `ContentRepository` and not `TeacherPacks`; a letter level takes only the pack's look anyway. Nothing is stored.

### The `pop` builder

`Play\Support\Mechanics\PopRoundBuilder` — «Oxu və vur», for beginners (five-year-olds) who cannot yet find a named item: **a `pop` round is a `find` round whose solution is every item.** Both builders extend `AnyRoundBuilder`, which picks a round's items — the same pool, the same target deck (`RoundParts::deck()`), the same decoys (for a letter, the chosen letters first, then confusables), item ids, casing and `shortage()` (so the same `too_few_*` reasons) — and leaves each builder only its solution and its prompt. The same setup and seed give a `pop` game exactly the items of the `find` game it mirrors.

- **Items.** The level's `items` (4): the deck's item and `items` − 1 others. The deck still spreads the pool evenly over the rounds and never leads two rounds in a row with the same item; for a letter level the teacher's chosen `letters` lead the rounds and fill them first, as `find`'s Decoys say, so with enough chosen letters the child reads only those.
- **Solution.** `any` with every item id in display order; `slots` 0. Whatever the child hits is right: it bursts (or sparkles), the rest stay, and the round is solved when the last one goes; then the next set comes in. `RoundJudge` and `engine.ts` need nothing new: an `any` round already takes its solution's items in any order (`judge.json`).
- **Points.** There is no wrong item, so a solved `pop` round always has 0 misses and gives 3 points, credited to the student at the board and counted in the rankings like any other round (§5; `PointsRule` is unchanged).
- **Prompt.** The template takes no text: «Hər hərfi oxu və vur!», «Hər hecanı oxu və vur!», «Hər sözü oxu və vur!» — the child reads each item aloud before hitting it (the board does not listen, §3 "Voice"). So `show` is null and the prompt text is the template as written. No pictures, for the reason `find` has none.
- **Board and phone.** The board draws it with the `find` scene. The phone marks no item as the answer when every item is one (`remote-items.tsx`); a hit item shows as done.

### Picture and number rounds

R5, ADR 012. `find` and `pop` at a picture or number level are built by the builders above: `AnyRoundBuilder` already picks a pool, a target deck, decoys and item ids, and only the pool, the decoys and what an item carries differ.

- **Picture pool.** The pack's words that have a picture (`LevelData::keysFrom()`), from the pack as the game's owner plays it (`TeacherPacks`): a built-in word the owner hid is gone, and a teacher word, which has no picture, never enters. The pool must hold the level's `items` and two targets (`too_few_pictures` otherwise).
- **Picture items** carry `text` (the word as the pack writes it; never upper-cased), `image` (`ContentAssets::url()` of its picture, versioned like every content URL) and `content_key` `word:{lower}`. Decoys are other pictures of the pool at random.
- **Picture prompts.** `find`: `«{x}» hansıdır?` filled with the word, which is also `show`, for the adult who says it; `image` is null — the prompt never shows the answer's picture. `pop`: «Hər şəklin adını de və vur!», `show` null.
- **Number pool.** The numbers of the level's range (`numbers-5`: 1–5). No pack content is needed for `find` and `pop`.
- **Number items** carry `text` (the digits), `image` null and `content_key` `number:{n}`, in the pack's own item style, as letters are. In the `animal` and `fruit` styles a number stands alone, with no picture behind it (`figures.ts`: a cat behind «5» would say five cats). Decoys for a number are the nearest numbers of the range first (±1, then ±2…, at random within a distance), the number's "confusables".
- **Number prompts.** `find`: `«{x}» rəqəmini tap!`, `show` the digits. `pop`: «Hər rəqəmi de və vur!».
- **The rule of `find` at a reading level stays:** no picture on an item or in the prompt there. Here it is turned round on purpose — the item is the picture and the prompt is the word — because the child is to know the thing, not to read it.

### The `count` builder

`Play\Support\Mechanics\CountRoundBuilder` (R5, ADR 012) — «Neçə alma var?».

- **What is counted.** A word of the pack with a picture, from a deck over those words (`RoundParts::deck()`), so each round counts another thing and none twice in a row. The pack must list the `number` unit and have at least two such words (`too_few_pictures`).
- **How many.** N comes from a second deck over the level's range: every number equally often (±1), never the same N twice in a row.
- **Prompt.** `text` from `Neçə «{x}» var?` filled with the word; `image` the word's picture; `count` N — the board draws the picture N times; `show` null: the number is never written in the prompt.
- **Items.** The level's `items` numbers: N and its nearest neighbours in the range, as number items, shuffled; ids `a`, `b`, `c`… in display order.
- **Solution.** `any`, the one item that says N; `slots` 0. A wrong number is a miss and stays, as in `find`. The effect is the pack's.
- **Board.** `scenes/count.ts`: the question band holds the N pictures in rows of five (a ten-frame: 7 is a full row and two), the items stand in the lower band in the pack's item style (`layout(count, band)`, as a build round's tiles do). `layouts.ts` holds the bands; Vitest checks that ten pictures fit.
- **Phone.** Above the items the remote shows the picture and «× N» (`components/games/remote-question.tsx`); the items are number buttons, the answer marked «Cavab».

### The build builders

`Play\Support\Mechanics\BuildSyllableRoundBuilder`, `BuildWordRoundBuilder` and `BuildSentenceRoundBuilder` share one flow (`BuildRoundBuilder`) and, with the find builder, `RoundParts` (the target deck, item ids, content as written). A build round is a target, built from its tiles in order, plus the level's `decoys` (1) decoy tiles.

- **Targets.** Each builder starts from `LevelData::keysFrom($pack, $learned)` and keeps what its mechanic can build:
    - `build_syllable`: syllables of two or more letters; a one-letter `V` syllable would be a one-slot `find`.
    - `build_word`: words of two or more syllables, as the Mechanics table says, so the one-syllable words of `words-short` are left out; with a picture, which every built-in word has (§3) and a teacher word never has, so teacher words never play in `build_word`.
    - `build_sentence`: every sentence of the level.

    A target is kept only when it can get all of the level's decoys (below). Targets go round in the find builder's decks (`RoundParts::deck()`): every target equally often (±1), never twice in a row.

- **Minimum pool.** Two targets for every build mechanic, the find builder's rule for chosen letters: a target needs another one to alternate with. A build round takes nothing from the pool but its target and its decoys, so the level's `items` play no part. With fewer, `shortage()` names the counts — `too_few_syllables`, `too_few_words`, `too_few_sentences` (`:found`, `:count` = 2), where `:found` counts the targets that can get their decoys. With all 32 letters learned every pack has at least 7 targets per build level.
- **Answer tiles.**
    - `build_syllable`: the syllable's letters (`letter:{lower}`), each cased; the syllable itself is the prompt's `show` (cased), filled into the template.
    - `build_word`: the word's syllables (its override when it has one, §3), each cased: in lower case «Bakı» gives «Ba», «kı», in upper case «BA», «KI»; content keys `syllable:{lower}`.
    - `build_sentence`: the sentence's words as written (lower case keeps the capital of the first word and of a proper noun; upper case is `Az::upper`); content keys `word:{lower}` (`word:tar` for «Tar»). The final `.`, `?` or `!` goes into `prompt.tail` and is never a tile.
- **Decoys**, always case-insensitively different from every answer tile and from each other:
    - `build_syllable`: letters. Confusables of the syllable's letters among the learned letters first (`Alphabet::confusables()`): the most confusable of each letter, then the next ones, in random order within a rank; then other learned letters at random. Never a letter of the syllable.
    - `build_word`: syllables of the level's other words with a picture (same pack, learned letters; the one-syllable words of `words-short` count as sources; teacher words do not), at random, written lowercase: a decoy belongs to no word on the board, so a proper noun's capital would give it away.
    - `build_sentence`: words of the level's other sentences (same pack, learned letters), at random, written as in their own sentence (the first of those sentences, in pack order, that has the word), so a capitalised decoy competes for the first slot.
- **One tile per text.** Item texts are unique within a round after casing. A part that comes again reuses its tile, and the tile's id repeats in `solution.item_ids`: `tut` → `t`, `u`, `t`; `nə-nə` → `nə` twice. The judge already expects that (`judge.json`'s `nə-nə`).
- **Order.** The tiles are shuffled and get ids `a`, `b`, `c`… in display order. The shuffle is redone while the answer's tiles (two or more, in order of first use) stand side by side in the answer's order, so the board never shows the answer already built; their reverse order is always allowed, so the redo ends.
- **Prompt.** `build_syllable` shows the cased syllable. `build_word` shows the picture: `prompt.image` is `ContentAssets::url($word->image, $pack->version)`, built like the board's other content URLs, and `show` is null. `build_sentence` has neither, only `tail`. The `build_word` and `build_sentence` templates take no text (`{x}` would put the answer on the board), so their prompt text is the template as written. Items carry `image: null`.
- **Round.** `slots` is the solution's length, `solution.order` is `sequence`, the effect is the pack's own (the `symbols` pack's `sparkle`).

### The round contract

One shape, shared by the server (PHP readonly DTOs in `app/Domain/Play/Data`), the board and the phone (zod schemas in `resources/js/game/contract.ts`, with the TypeScript types inferred from them, so schema and type cannot differ). JSON keys are snake_case. Both sides are pinned to the same fixtures in `tests/Fixtures/contract/*.json`:

- Pest (`tests/Unit/Play/ContractTest.php`): for every fixture, `Dto::fromArray($fixture)->toArray()` equals the fixture exactly (same keys in the same order, same values, same types).
- Vitest (`resources/js/game/contract.test.ts`): every fixture parses with the strict schema (`z.strictObject`, so an extra key fails as well as a missing one) and comes out unchanged.

The prefix of a fixture's file name names its DTO and schema: `round.*` (`RoundData` / `Round`: at least one `find` round per unit it supports, a round of every mechanic and a sequence round with a reused tile), `state.*` (`GameStateData` / `GameState`), `sync-request.*` (`BoardEventsData` / `SyncRequest`), `sync-response.*` (`SyncResultData` / `SyncResponse`), `game-status.*` (`GameStatusData` / `GameStatus`); a file with another prefix fails both tests. `judge.json` holds the judge cases (below).

R5 (ADR 012) changes the contract once, on both sides and in the fixtures together: `prompt.count` (below) joins every `round.*` and `state.*` fixture as `null`; `Mechanic` gains `count` and `Unit` gains `picture` and `number`; the new fixtures are `round.find-picture.json`, `round.find-number.json` and `round.count.json`. Rounds stored before R5 have no `count` in their payload: `RoundData::fromPayload()` reads a missing one as null, a Pest case pins it, and no stored round is rewritten.

A key added on one side breaks that side's test until the fixture changes, and the changed fixture then breaks the other side's test. The same schemas validate every client event the board receives (§4), so nothing malformed reaches the engine.

**Rejected:** a type-only check (`fixture satisfies Round` under `tsc`). TypeScript widens the strings of an imported JSON file to `string`, so the check fails on every literal union (`mechanic`, `unit`…) and never catches an extra key. Generated TypeScript from PHP (ADR 003) is also rejected.

The **judge** — "is this item the next correct one?" — is the only rule both sides implement: `Play\Support\RoundJudge::judge($round, $progress, $itemId)` (authoritative, decides what is stored) and `judge(round, progress, itemId)` in `resources/js/game/engine.ts` (instant feedback). Both return `correct`, `miss` or `stale`, the progress after the hit and whether the round is solved: in an `any` round an unhit solution item is correct and the round is solved when every solution item is hit, a burst item hit again is `stale`; in a `sequence` round only `solution.item_ids[progress.length]` is correct; any other item of the round is a miss; an item not in the round, or any hit on a solved round, is `stale`. Both run the same cases from `tests/Fixtures/contract/judge.json` (Pest dataset and Vitest): each case has `items`, a `solution`, an optional starting `progress` and `hits` with the expected `status`, `progress`, running `misses` and `solved`; they include a reused tile (`nə-nə`), a miss, two misses on the same decoy, a burst item hit again, an unknown item and a hit after the round is solved. The 800 ms cross-source rule (§4) belongs to the board alone and has its own Vitest cases. Points (§5) exist only in PHP; the board shows the number the server returns. The picture and number rounds and `count` (R5) need nothing new from the judge — each is an `any` round with one or all of its items — and `judge.json` gains one `count` case to pin that.

## 3. Azerbaijani content

### Alphabet

**Decision.** `content/alphabet.json` holds the 32 letters in alphabet order with upper and lower form, vowel or consonant, Azerbaijani name and confusables:

```
A B C Ç D E Ə F G Ğ H X I İ J K Q L M N O Ö P R S Ş T U Ü V Y Z
```

Vowels (9): a, e, ə, ı, i, o, ö, u, ü. Consonants (23): b, c, ç, d, f, g, ğ, h, x, j, k, q, l, m, n, p, r, s, ş, t, v, y, z. `y` is always a consonant. Ə/ə are U+018F/U+0259 — never Ǝ (U+018E) or the Cyrillic Ә/ә (U+04D8/U+04D9).

`Language\Support\Alphabet` exposes it to PHP; a Pest test pins the count (32), the order, the vowel set and the names. Each letter has `upper`, `lower`, `vowel`, `name` and `confusables`, and the file has a `lookalikes` map (see Normalization). `Az` builds its case mapping from the same pairs, so no PHP or TypeScript file lists letters.

- **Names** (`Alphabet::name()`), as children hear a letter spelled: a, be, ce, çe, de, e, ə, fe, ge, ğe, he, xe, ı, i, je, ke, qe, el, em, en, o, ö, pe, er, se, şe, te, u, ü, ve, ye, ze.
- **Confusables** (`Alphabet::confusables()`, lowercase, most confusable first, symmetric — the validator rejects a pair listed one way only): a–ə, e–ə, i–ı, o–ö, u–ü, s–ş, c–ç, g–ğ, g–q, k–q, h–x, b–d, b–p, m–n. Letter decoys come from here first (§2). The owner reviews both lists in O2.

### Casing

**Decision.** `Language\Support\Az::upper()` / `Az::lower()` map i↔İ and ı↔I explicitly, then apply `mb_strtoupper` / `mb_strtolower` to the rest. All text that reaches the board or the phone is cased by the server; TypeScript never changes the case of content.

**Why.** `mb_strtoupper('i')` gives `I` and `mb_strtolower('İ')` gives `i̇` (i + combining dot) — both wrong for Azerbaijani. One implementation on the server means one set of tests.

**Rejected.** `toLocaleUpperCase('az')` in the browser: correct in current engines, but a second implementation, and the server needs casing anyway for names and the judge.

### Normalization

`Az::normalize()` runs on every name a teacher types and on every content string at validation: invalid UTF-8 repaired (`mb_scrub`), Unicode NFC (`Normalizer`, so `ext-intl` is required), the look-alikes in `alphabet.json` replaced (Cyrillic Ә/ә → Ə/ə, Ǝ/ǝ (U+018E/U+01DD) → Ə/ə, i + combining dot above (what `mb_strtolower('İ')` produces) → i), invisible characters removed (zero-width space, joiners, word joiner, soft hyphen, BOM), every run of whitespace (tabs, no-break spaces) turned into one space, trimmed. Content that still contains a letter outside the alphabet (besides space, hyphen and `. , ! ? « »`) fails validation.

`Az::title()` writes the first letter of every word and hyphenated part upper and the rest lower ("ƏLİ" → "Əli", "gül-ayşə" → "Gül-Ayşə"); the paste preview uses it for names.

### Syllabification

**Decision.** `Language\Support\Syllabifier` applies the standard rule for Azerbaijani (one vowel per syllable; consonants between two vowels):

| Between two vowels | Split               | Example                                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| no consonant       | between the vowels  | `sa-at`, `mü-əl-lim`                            |
| one consonant      | before it           | `a-ta`, `a-na`                                  |
| two or more        | before the last one | `al-ma`, `qar-pız`, `dost-luq`, `A-zər-bay-can` |

Consonants before the first vowel join the first syllable (`stol`, `qra-fin`); consonants after the last vowel join the last (`dost`, `üst`). Casing is kept as in the word.

A word may override the algorithm in its pack: `{"text": "qreypfrut", "syllables": ["qreyp", "frut"]}` (the rule alone would give `qreypf-rut`). The validator rejects an override whose parts do not join back to the word exactly (same letters, same case), whose part does not have exactly one vowel, or that equals what the rule gives already — so the overrides in the packs are exactly the words the rule gets wrong. The override, when present, always wins (`WordData::$syllables`, `WordData::$overridden`).

`Syllabifier::shape()` names a syllable's pattern of vowels and consonants (`a` → V, `ba` → CV, `al` → VC, `bal` → CVC, `üst` → VCC, `dost` → CVCC); syllable levels pick by it (§2). Vowels are tested through `Az::lower`, so `I` and `İ` count; `y` and `ğ` are consonants.

**Why.** The rule is short, deterministic and right for native words; loanwords and teacher disputes are exactly what per-word overrides are for, and they are reviewed in pull requests by the owner (step O2).

**Rejected.** A hyphenation dictionary (TeX/Hunspell `hyph_az`): hyphenation rules for line breaks differ from the syllables a first-grade textbook teaches, and the license and quality of an `az` pattern file are unclear. Storing syllables for every word by hand: slow to write and easy to get inconsistent.

The test set `content/tests/syllables.json` (235 words in A4, grouped by the rule they exercise and written with hyphens, `"A-zər-bay-can"`; reviewed by the owner in O2) runs as a Pest dataset. Its expectations come from the textbook rule and Azerbaijani usage, never from running the Syllabifier; it covers every row and example of the table above, `y` and `ğ` as consonants, words starting with i, ı, İ or I, words in capitals, hiatus (`a-i-lə`, `şe-ir`) and proper nouns.

R6 adds two packs, as data only. `colors` (`["picture"]`, item style `picture`): eleven colour words — qırmızı, sarı, yaşıl, göy, narıncı, bənövşəyi, çəhrayı, qəhvəyi, qara, ağ, boz — each with an own SVG (§8): the same round swatch in that colour, its light and shade of the same hue (ADR 014 "Colour swatches"), so nothing but the colour tells two items apart. «mavi» is left out on purpose: next to «göy» a small child cannot tell the two, and a round may show any two words of a pack; the hues are the swatch bases ADR 014 lists (OpenMoji's palette colours, kept), checked in pairs on a projector (red and orange, red and pink, blue and purple, grey and white). `numbers` (`["number"]`): its words are things to count, each with a picture; the digits come from the level's range. A pack without the reading units has no `sentences` key.
- **Sounds.** Every pack has `style.sounds` with `hit`, `miss` and `solved` (A5): the hit sound follows the effect — a pop for `burst`, a gentle chime for `sparkle` (a state symbol is never popped, §8). These three are the moments an account may choose a sound for, and `Sound::choosable()` names them: `SoundChoices`, the `sounds.json` check, `BoardSoundsData` and the settings page read it, never `Sound::cases()`. A pack may also have `arrive` and `leave`, the sounds of a round's items (and of a build round's slots) coming in and going: they belong to the pack alone — optional, never in `sounds.json`, never a choice in Settings → Oyun — and each is any audio file under `content/assets` with a credits entry. A scene plays them because the pack has them, never because of its item or slot style, and once per round each, never per item: `find` (and `pop`, `count` on it) plays `leave` when the rest of a solved round go (under `solved`) or when a new round replaces items still on the board, and `arrive` as the new round's items come in; a build scene plays `leave` when its row goes (the tiles left, the slots and the copies riding on them) and one `arrive` for the next row's slots and tiles together. A round shown already solved or skipped plays no `arrive`. Only `train` has them today: `train-arrive` and `train-leave`, a toy steam train synthesised for Elifba (§8). `content/sounds.json` lists, for each choosable moment, the sounds an account may choose instead (`Content\Support\SoundChoices`): a key matching `SoundChoices::KEY_PATTERN`, a `file` under `content/assets`, a `title` in az and en and, for a sound that suits only some effects, `effects` (the pop: `["burst"]`); the file has a `version` of its own that its URLs carry (`?v=`), bumped when a sound changes. Every pack's own `hit`, `miss` and `solved` is one of them, for the same moment and suiting the pack's effect. An account chooses in Settings → Oyun (`/settings/play`, `PUT /settings/play/sounds` → `UpdateBoardSounds` with `Play\Data\BoardSoundsData`: `hit`, `miss`, `solved`, each nullable and one of that moment's keys → `users.board_sounds`, only the moments with a choice; null = the pack's own), with a ▶ button that plays each sound on the page. `Play\Support\BoardSounds` gives `BoardProps` every moment: for a choosable one the game owner's choice where `sounds.json` still lists it and it suits the pack's effect, and the pack's own otherwise, so a teacher board, a guest board and a home board of the same account sound alike; for `arrive` and `leave` the pack's own or null. A board already open keeps what it loaded with. The board never stacks copies of one sound: `BoardScene.play()` does not start a sound that is still playing or paused (quick wrong taps at a `pop` board). `BoardScene.setPaused()` pauses `arrive` and `leave` with its tweens and clock and resumes them together, so a train stops rolling and chugging at once; a scene starts them on its clock (`playIn()`), so one due while the board is paused waits too. The short `hit`, `miss` and `solved` play out: a game that finishes pauses the board under the last round's celebration. Every pack's own `miss` is the pizzicato `pluck-down` and its `solved` the glockenspiel `bells`, which the owner chose by ear (2026-11-11).
- **Pictures.** A word's `image` is a Fluent Emoji file named by its code points (`fluent/1F34E.svg`, `fluent/1F9D1-200D-1F3EB.svg`) when an emoji clearly shows the word, otherwise an own SVG under the pack's folder (`fruit/qreypfrut.svg`); the `cities` maps are generated own art in the same manner (§8). No two words of one pack share a picture; words of different packs may. From A5 on every word has one: A5 drew own art for the words its emoji set lacked and switched the check on (below).
- **Backdrop.** `style.backdrop` is optional own art (§8 "Backdrops"): A5 drew the `cities` pack's (`cities/qiz-qalasi.svg`, Qız qalası); ADR 014's session 3 drew those of `animals`, `fruit`, `colors` and `numbers`.
- **Cover.** `style.cover` is required: the picture that stands for the theme on the setup page (docs/frontend.md rule 13). It is the `image` of one of the pack's own words, so it is already credited, never a backdrop or an avatar. The covers: `animals` `fluent/1F408.svg` (pişik), `balloons` `fluent/1F388.svg` (şar), `cities` `cities/baki.svg`, `colors` `colors/goy.svg`, `fruit` `fluent/1F34E.svg` (alma), `numbers` `fluent/2B50.svg` (ulduz), `symbols` `symbols/bayraq.svg`, `train` `fluent/1F682.svg`.
- **With.** `with` is required (2026-11-13): the pack's title as a game's card names it, «:with cümlə qur» (docs/frontend.md rule 13, `games.titles.*`). In Azerbaijani it is the title in the instrumental case, written out rather than suffixed by a rule; in English the phrase the title takes after a verb. The eight: `animals` «Heyvanlarla» / "with animals", `balloons` «Şarlarla» / "with balloons", `cities` «Şəhərlərimizlə» / "with our cities", `colors` «Rənglərlə» / "with colours", `fruit` «Meyvələrlə» / "with fruit", `numbers` «Rəqəmlərlə» / "with numbers", `symbols` «Milli rəmzlərlə» / "with national symbols", `train` «Qatarla» / "with the train".
- **Figures.** `style.figures` is the list of pictures an `animal` or `fruit` item that is a word without a picture of its own (a sentence's other words, a teacher's word) stands in front of (§2), required for those two item styles and refused for the others: image paths under `content/assets`, each once, never an avatar (`avatars/…`), at least twice as many as the most items or tiles a round of any level shows (find's `items`, or a build level's longest answer plus its `decoys`: 2 × 5 today). They are usually the pack's own word pictures (`"fluent/1F404.svg"`, the cow of `inək`) and may add others (`"fluent/1F995.svg"`, a dinosaur). Like every asset, each needs a credits entry.

`content/avatars.json` lists the avatars in the order new students get them (A3); each has a key that fits `students.avatar` and a picture:

`Content\Support\Avatars` reads it; page props carry each avatar's URL (`Avatars::all()`, built by `ContentAssets::url()`), so TypeScript never builds an asset path.

- **Versioning.** `version` is an integer bumped on every change to the pack; `game_sessions.pack_version` records it and asset URLs carry it (`?v=3`) for cache busting. Snapshots (§2) mean an old game never reads the current pack.
- **Serving assets.** `config/filesystems.php` `links` gains `public_path('content') => base_path('content/assets')`, so `php artisan storage:link` (run by `composer setup` and the Docker image) creates the link. The JSON files are not public.
- **Cache.** `php artisan content:cache` validates every file and, only when they are valid, writes one compiled PHP array to `bootstrap/cache/content.php` (`Content\Actions\WriteContentCache`, compiled from the JSON files, never from an older compiled file), which OPcache keeps in memory the way `config:cache` does; `content:clear` removes it (`DeleteContentCache`). The Content service provider (`app/Providers/ContentServiceProvider.php`) registers them with `$this->optimizes(optimize: 'content:cache', clear: 'content:clear')`, so the deploy's `php artisan optimize` builds it and `optimize:clear` removes it. Without that file (development) `ContentRepository` reads the JSON files directly, once per request (the container holds one instance), so an edited pack shows on the next reload. Tests never read the compiled file — the provider gives the repository no compiled path when `runningUnitTests()` — so a stale `bootstrap/cache/content.php` cannot change their results. `php artisan content:validate` prints every problem. (Rejected: the Laravel cache keyed by a hash of the files. Computing the key means reading every file on every request, and with the default database cache store each request would also fetch a large blob.)
- **Validation** (`ContentValidator`, run by a Pest test over the real `content/` and over `tests/Fixtures/content/`, one broken fixture per rule in `broken.json`). It takes a content root, so fixtures are validated the same way, and lists every problem as `file: where: what`:
    - **Every file:** valid JSON objects with exactly the documented keys (a typo such as `syllabels` is an unknown key); every string stored normalized — anything `Az::normalize()` would change (Cyrillic ә, Ǝ/ǝ, invisible characters, double spaces) is rejected, not fixed.
    - **`alphabet.json`:** every letter has an `upper`, `lower`, `vowel`, a name of alphabet letters and confusables that are other letters of the alphabet and list the letter back; look-alikes map to letters.
    - **`avatars.json`:** unique keys matching `Avatars::KEY_PATTERN`; every avatar has an `image` under `content/assets`, and no two avatars share one.
    - **`sounds.json`:** a `version` ≥ 1; for each of `hit`, `miss`, `solved` (`Sound::choosable()`, and no other moment) a non-empty list of sounds with keys unique in that moment matching `SoundChoices::KEY_PATTERN`, an audio `file` under `content/assets` listed once per moment, a title in az and en and optional `effects` from `Effect`.
    - **`voice.json`** (ADR 015): a `version` ≥ 1; `voice` with `provider` `elevenlabs` (the one provider `content:voice` calls), a `model`, a `voice_id` of 8–64 letters and digits, a `voice_name`, a two-letter `language` and `stability` and `similarity` from 0 to 1; `styles`, an audio tag (lowercase English words, no brackets) for every template of `prompts.json` keyed `{mechanic}.{unit}`, and no other; `spoken.letters`, how the voice says every letter of the alphabet, in lowercase letters (`r` → `rı`); `spoken.numbers`, a lowercase name for every number 1–10; `spoken.respell`, phrase → the text the voice is given instead; `context`, `previous` and `next`, Azerbaijani text of alphabet letters, spaces and `. , ! ? -` (or empty); `praise` and `retry`, each a list of at least one `{text, style}` with a text of alphabet letters, spaces and `. , ! ? -` ending in `.`, `!` or `?`, no phrase twice; `credit`, the credits entry the clips get, with an https `source`, a license of the allow list and `{month}` in `changes`.
    - **`voice-clips.json`:** `clips`, hash → the text the clip was made from: every hash 16 lowercase hex digits naming a file `assets/voice/{hash}.mp3` that exists, every file of `assets/voice` listed, every hash the clip of its text in the voice of `voice.json`, and every praise and retry phrase with a clip. A prompt without a clip is no problem (its round is silent); `content:validate` counts them. From V2 (ADR 016) also `names`, hash → text: every hash naming a file `voice-names/{hash}.mp3` that exists, every file of `content/voice-names` listed, every hash the clip of a name of `names.json` in the voice of `voice.json` (a clip whose name left the list fails until `--prune`, as a prompt's does); `content:validate` counts the names without a clip («Name clips made: 0 of …»). `voice.json` gains `names` (`style`, and `credit` with the rules of `credit`) and `greeting`, `finish`, `finish_class` (each a `{text, style}` with the rules of praise, none the same text as a praise or retry phrase or each other); each of the three needs a clip, like praise and retry (`ContentValidator::NAME_PHRASE_CLIPS_REQUIRED`, switched on with V2's clips, as `WORD_PICTURES_REQUIRED` was in A5). `content/voice-names/*` needs a credits entry like an asset folder, from its first clip.
    - **`names.json`** (V2, ADR 016): a `version` ≥ 1; `sources`, a non-empty list of https URLs; `names`, a non-empty list of first names, each 2–24 letters of the alphabet only (one word: no space, hyphen or apostrophe), equal to `Az::title()` of itself, unique by `Az::lower()`, the list in Azerbaijani order (`AzCollator`).
    - **`levels.json`:** unique keys; titles in az and en; the filter, `items` and `decoys` rules of §2; mechanics that suit the unit. From R5 a number level's `numbers` is `min` < `max` within 1–10 with at least `items` + 1 numbers in it.
    - **`prompts.json`:** one template per mechanic × unit that a level allows, no more; `{x}` at most once and only as `«{x}»` with nothing attached; alphabet letters, spaces and `. , ! ? -` only; a final `.`, `!` or `?`.
    - **Packs:** the key is the file name; `version` ≥ 1; titles in az and en; `with` in az and en, neither empty, the az one starting with the az title; style keys from the closed lists, `#RRGGBB` palette colours (1–8), sounds only for `hit`, `miss`, `solved`, `arrive`, `leave`, each optional: the first three each a sound of `sounds.json` for its moment that suits the pack's effect, `arrive` and `leave` any audio file; every picture, backdrop, figure and sound exists under `content/assets`; `style.cover` is the `image` of one of the pack's words; no two words of the pack share an `image`; `style.figures` as in Figures above. A word is a single word of alphabet letters (no space, hyphen or sign), lowercase or capitalised as a proper noun, unique in its pack by `Az::lower`, with a vowel; overrides as in Syllabification. A sentence is 2–4 words of alphabet letters separated by single spaces, starts with a capital letter and ends with exactly one `.`, `!` or `?`; its id matches `^[a-z0-9]+(-[a-z0-9]+)*$` and is unique in the pack, and its text is not repeated.
    - **Units** (R5): `units` is a non-empty list of `Unit` values, each once, in enum order; the four reading units together or none; `sentences` only with them. A pack that lists `picture` has at least 6 words with a picture — and every picture level gets its `items` + 2 from it; a pack that lists `number` has at least 4. The bar below applies to a pack that lists the reading units.
    - **The bar:** every pack has at least 20 words and 10 sentences (A4's bar). With all 32 letters learned, every syllable, word and sentence level gets at least 6 distinct items from every pack (`find` shows 4 and never repeats a target in a row); every word fits a word level and every sentence a sentence level.
    - **Credits** (§8): every file under `content/assets` and `resources/fonts` (hidden files aside) has an entry; every entry's patterns match a file and stay in those folders; licenses from the allow list; share-alike files only in `content/assets/openmoji/` (`ContentValidator::SHARE_ALIKE_FOLDER`; none are left since ADR 014, the folder is gone, and a share-alike file added later still has one place).
    - **SVG files** (§8): every `.svg` under `content/assets` has no `<script`, no `on…=` event attribute, no `<foreignObject` and no `href` (or `xlink:href`) other than an in-file `#…` reference.
    - **Every word has a picture:** `ContentValidator::WORD_PICTURES_REQUIRED`, on since A5 drew own art for the words its emoji set lacked; a word without an `image` is a problem.
    - **`tests/syllables.json`:** alphabet letters with a hyphen between parts of one vowel each, no word twice.
- **Child-facing text** (prompts, words, sentences) is Azerbaijani regardless of the teacher's interface language: the product teaches Azerbaijani reading. Prompt templates wrap inserted content in «» and never attach a suffix to it (`«{x}» hecasını tap!`), so vowel harmony never has to be computed.
- **Content keys** are stable strings: `letter:{lower}`, `syllable:{lower}`, `word:{lower}`, `sentence:{pack}:{id}` (`WordData::key()`, `SentenceData::key()`, `LevelData::keysFrom()`), and from R5 `number:{n}`. A picture item keeps its word's key, `word:{lower}`.

**Why.** Content is small (hundreds of entries), changes with the code, and must be reviewed by a teacher (O2) — a pull request diff is the right review tool. Files are one source of truth: no seeding step at deploy, no drift between the repository and a database, tests read exactly what production reads.

**Rejected.** JSON packs seeded into tables: two sources of truth, a deploy step that can be forgotten, and no query that needs SQL. Content edited in an admin panel: there is no admin role in v1, and it would hide content changes from review. Teachers' own words are not built-in content: they live in tables next to the files and are merged per teacher ("Teacher words" below, ADR 007) — additive, not a reversal.

### Teacher words

**Decision** (ADR 007, ADR 009). A teacher may add their own words, edit and delete them, and hide built-in words of a theme pack from their own games and show them again. A teacher's words belong to the teacher, not to a pack: they play in every theme pack. Built-in words stay the same for everyone; a teacher's words and hidden words apply to all of that teacher's classes and to no other teacher. Teacher words have no picture. Tables: `custom_words` and `hidden_words` (§1).

- **One merge.** `Content\Support\TeacherPacks::pack($teacher, $key)` — and `packs($teacher)`, every pack in key order, in two queries — gives a pack as that teacher plays it: the built-in words in file order without the hidden ones, then all of the teacher's words (the same in every pack) in the order they were added (`created_at`, then `id`), as `WordData` with `image: null` and their override or the Syllabifier's split. A teacher word whose `Az::lower` equals a built-in word of the pack, hidden or not, is dropped from that pack only, so the built-in one plays there with its picture (also when a later pack version adds a word a teacher already has), and the teacher's word plays in the other packs. Sentences, look, title and version are the built-in pack's. `TeacherPacks::merge()` is the pure function behind it; `ContentRepository` stays the reader of the files only.
- **Who reads it.** Every Play reader that builds or checks rounds: `Playable::options($teacher, $learned)` (the setup page), `StartableGame` in `GameSetupData::rules()`, and `StartGame`, which passes the pack to the builder (`RoundBuilder::build($setup, $pack, $learned, $random)`). The teacher is the class's teacher, who owns the game. From R3 it is the game's owner (`GamePlayers`): in a home game the parent, who has own or hidden words only when the same account is also a teacher — the words page stays in the teacher area. `BoardProps` and `GameSetupOptions::shared()` read the built-in pack: they need only its look and title.
- **Where a teacher word plays.** Syllables come from a pack's words (§2), so a teacher word gives its syllables to the syllable levels by itself: it plays in `find` and `pop` at word and syllable levels and in `build_syllable`. It has no picture, so `build_word` never takes it, as a target or as a source of decoys, and neither does a picture or `count` round (R5). A hidden built-in word is gone from every level of that teacher's games, and so are the syllables no other word of the pack gives; sentences are not affected.
- **Only my words.** Where a teacher word plays (`Playable::takesOwnWords()`), the setup may ask for the teacher's own words only (`GameSetupData::$own_words`, «Yalnız mənim sözlərim»). `TeacherPacks::pack($teacher, $key, own: true)` (and `packs($teacher, own: true)`) gives the same merge without any built-in word — exactly the teacher words that play (a teacher word equal to a built-in one stays dropped) — so the round items, their decoys and, at a syllable level, the syllables come only from them. `Playable::reason(…, own: true)` asks the usual question about that pack: `own_words_level` where the choice does not apply, `no_own_words` when the teacher has no word that plays in the pack, then the builder's `too_few_*`. The setup page's options carry the answer as `own` (`{startable, reason}`, or null where the choice is not offered), and the form shows «Hansı sözlər? Hamısı / Yalnız mənim sözlərim» only there, with «Yalnız mənim sözlərim» chosen by default whenever it can start (the form sends `own_words` only then; the server's default stays false). When it cannot, the reason shows with «Söz əlavə et»: a dialog (`AddWordDialog`) that posts to `POST /words` and stays open for the next word, and the page reloads its options in place, so the choice turns on as soon as there are enough words. The setup props carry `wordMaxLength` (`elifba.custom_words.max_length`) for it; `StartableGame` and `StartGame` read the same pack. Nothing new is stored: the rounds are snapshots, as with chosen `letters`.
- **Rules for a teacher word** (`Content\Data\CustomWordData`). The input is normalized first (`Az::normalize`, each override part too: phone keyboards type Cyrillic ә), then:
    - the rules every pack word follows, from the one checker `ContentValidator` also uses (`Content\Support\WordRules`, problems as `Content\Enums\WordProblem`): one word of alphabet letters with no space, hyphen or sign; lowercase, or capitalised as a proper noun; a vowel; an override that joins back to the word exactly with one vowel in each part. An override equal to the Syllabifier's split is dropped (stored as null) rather than refused: a pack file refuses it to keep diffs minimal, while a teacher's form may send the split back unchanged;
    - at most `elifba.custom_words.max_length` (20) characters;
    - it fits at least one word level (`LevelData::fitsWord()`, with the override when there is one), so no word is stored that no game can use (`no_level`);
    - checked by `AddCustomWord` and `UpdateCustomWord` under a lock on the teacher's `users` row: not already one of the teacher's words, compared with `Az::lower` (`taken`; the unique index on `(user_id, word_key)` backs it), and at most `elifba.custom_words.max_per_teacher` (200) words (`too_many`). A built-in word of some pack is not refused (ADR 009): it plays as the teacher's word in the other packs.

    Each refusal is a `ValidationException` on `text` or `syllables` with a `words.errors.*` message (`letters`, `casing`, `no_vowel`, `split`, `no_level`, `taken`, `too_many`, `not_in_pack`) that never contains the word.

- **Hiding.** `HiddenWordData` (`pack`; `word`, a built-in word of that pack, normalized and compared with `Az::lower`, `not_in_pack` otherwise). `HideWord` is idempotent (it holds the teacher's lock and finds the row before it adds one); `ShowWord` deletes the row and does nothing when there is none.
- **Shortages.** Hiding words can leave a level with too little content for a class. `Playable::reason()` then gives the builder's `shortage()` (`too_few_words`, `too_few_syllables`), exactly as for unlearned letters, on the setup page and in `GameSetupData::rules()`.
- **Snapshots.** A teacher word reaches the board only inside a round snapshot, under the key `word:{lower}` like any word; the round contract is unchanged. `game_sessions.pack_version` records the built-in pack's version.
- **Privacy.** A teacher word may be a child's name. It is never written to a log, an exception message or a URL (§7): routes address a teacher word by its ULID, and hiding or showing carries the pack and the word in the request body.
- **Page and routes** (`routes/words.php`, `Http\Controllers\Content\{WordController,HiddenWordController}`, page `words/index`, sidebar item «Sözlər»). `GET /words?pack={key}` shows all of the teacher's words, and the built-in words of one pack (the first one for a missing or unknown key): `customWords` (`id`, `text`, `syllables`, `override`, `levels` — for each word and syllable level, in `levels.json` order, the level's `title` and the `items` the word gives it (`LevelData::itemsFrom()`: the word, or its syllables that fit the level's shapes; levels it gives nothing are left out), and `built_in_in` — the titles of the packs that have the word built in and play theirs instead), `levelTotals` (each word and syllable level's `title` and the `count` of distinct items all the teacher's words give it, so a teacher sees why a level is short), `packs` (`key`, `title` in the interface language), `pack`, `builtInWords` (`text`, `syllables`, `hidden`) and `limits` (`max_length`, `max_per_teacher`, `count`). The theme tabs belong to the built-in words only. Syllables reach React joined by the server (`WordData::hyphenated()`, `al-ma`); the override field is typed the same way and `CustomWordData::prepare()` splits it at its hyphens (a phone's dash counts; empty parts are dropped, so an empty field is no override). `POST /words` (`AddCustomWord`), `PATCH /words/{word}` (`UpdateCustomWord`: text and override) and `DELETE /words/{word}` (`DeleteCustomWord`) redirect back to the page (the same theme tab); `{word}` is a ULID (`whereUlid`) bound only among the current teacher's words (`BelongsToOwner`) and checked by `Content\Policies\CustomWordPolicy`. `POST /words/hidden` (`HideWord`) and `DELETE /words/hidden` (`ShowWord`) take `pack` and `word` in the body. Validation messages name the fields in the interface language (`words.attributes.*`); flash messages never carry a word; `text`, `syllables` and `word` are not flashed back into the session. The page says that teacher words have no picture and so are not used in «Sözü yığ»; deleting asks first in a dialog.

**Why.** Teachers play with the words their class is reading this week, which no pack can predict, and some built-in words do not suit every class. Tables per teacher keep the files the single, reviewed source for everyone, and one merge read by every Play reader means the setup page never offers a game `StartGame` cannot build.

**Rejected.** Teacher words in the pack files (a pull request per word, and every teacher would get them). Words per class (a teacher with two classes would type them twice; learned letters already narrow each class). Deleting or copying built-in words per teacher (a copy misses pack fixes; hiding is undone with one click). Pictures for teacher words (uploads bring storage, moderation and licenses, §8; `build_word` is the only place pictures are used).

### Learned letters

**Decision.** The class page shows the 32 letters as toggles (with "all" and "none"); the teacher switches on the letters the class has learned (`school_classes.learned_letters`, default all 32). Every level uses only letters, syllables and words made of switched-on letters. No textbook order is built in.

**Why.** It works with any textbook edition and any pace, and the teacher knows exactly which letters were taught.

**Rejected.** A built-in teaching order with "up to letter X": it would tie Elifba to one textbook edition, orders differ between editions, and a teacher who goes at another pace would fight it. If a common order is wanted later, it becomes a preset in `alphabet.json` — data only.

**At home** (R3, ADR 011). A parent's child has their own learned letters (`children.learned_letters`, default all 32, `UpdateChildLetters`), with the same 32 toggles on the child's page: they are what the class's letters are to a class game, and `GamePlayers::learned()` gives Play whichever applies. They belong to the child, not to the parent, because siblings of 6 and 9 are at different letters. **They do not apply to picture and number levels** (R5): those items are not read, so no letter filters them — for a class or for a child.

### Voice

**Decision** (owner, 2026-11-11, [ADR 015](decisions/015-voice.md); built in the step called V1 here). Before V1 the board only showed the prompt in large text (with a picture where there is one) and played sound effects, and the adult read the prompt aloud. From V1 the board says the prompt and the praise in a prerecorded synthetic voice; the text stays on the board, and a prompt without a clip is still read by the adult:

- **Clips are content files.** Every clip is an MP3 under `content/assets/voice/{hash}.mp3`, generated once, offline, by `php artisan content:voice` and committed; nothing is synthesised at runtime by the server or the browser, so a page makes no third-party request and an instance needs no key. `content/voice.json` (written by hand) holds the voice (ElevenLabs `eleven_v4`, language `az`, the voice «Nigar – Ad Read» by its id, Stability 0.13, Similarity 0.75), the audio tag of each prompt template (`styles`, `find.picture` → `playful`, `count.number` → `curious`), how letters and numbers are said and respellings (`spoken`), the Azerbaijani `context` the provider is given around every phrase and never says (`previous_text`, `next_text`; without it a short phrase such as «Pişik hansıdır?» came out with a Turkish accent, and the context characters are not billed), the praise and retry phrases with their tags, and the credits entry the clips get. `content/voice-clips.json` (written by the command) lists every clip by hash with the text it was made from; `ContentRepository` reads both into `VoiceData`, compiled with the rest of the content. A clip's name is the first 16 hex digits of the SHA-256 of the provider, model, voice id, language, both settings, the context and the text sent (`VoiceClips::hash()`): the command makes only the hashes that have no file, and a clip accepted by ear is never made again.
- **Every prompt, as a whole phrase.** A template with `{x}` has a clip per item it can name (`VoiceClips::needed()`: every item a level of its unit and mechanic gives in a pack that lists the unit, all 32 letters learned; `build_syllable` only syllables of two or more letters; `count` the pictured words of a pack that lists `number`), a template without one a single clip; never a word clip stitched to a template clip. The clip says what the adult would read: the prompt text without « », with a capital first letter, `{x}` a letter as a first grade says its sound, from `voice.json`'s `spoken.letters` (a consonant with ı: «Bı hərfini tap!», «Rı hərfini tap!», but «Ge» for g; a vowel as itself; «ğı» and «ı» through `spoken.respell`, «ğğı» and «ıı», which the voice says right; the owner's choice by ear, not the alphabet names «be», «er» of `alphabet.json`), a number by its name in `voice.json` («Beş rəqəmini tap!»), a syllable or a word as the pack writes it («Bakı sözünü tap!»). The provider is given that phrase, or its respelling, after the template's tag: `[playful] Dovşan hansıdır?`. Built-in content needed 907 clips at V1.
- **Never the child's reading.** A clip says the prompt, never an item: in `pop` only «Hər hərfi oxu və vur!», in `build_word` and `build_sentence` only the instruction, and tapping an item says nothing.
- **`prompt.audio`.** The round builders fill it through `Play\Support\PromptVoice` (the find and pop flow in `AnyRoundBuilder`, the build flow in `BuildRoundBuilder`, and `CountRoundBuilder`): the URL of the prompt's clip from the app's own host, versioned with `voice.json`'s `version` like every content URL (`/content/voice/{hash}.mp3?v=1`), or null when the clip is not made. The contract does not change: `prompt.audio` has been a nullable URL since A6, and a stored round keeps the URL it was built with.
- **No teacher word; a name only from the list.** A student's or a child's name is never sent to a speech provider (§7); from V2 a first name on the built-in name list is said from its own clip ("Names" below), and any other name is silent. A word is voiced only when it is a built-in word of the round's pack (`PromptVoice` asks `ContentRepository`, never `TeacherPacks`): a teacher word has `prompt.audio` null even where another pack has a word spelled the same, and the adult reads it. A letter, a syllable or a number is content whatever word it came from. Null is always valid: any prompt without a clip is silent.
- **Praise and retry.** A solved round is followed by one of the praise phrases, at random («Doğru cavab!», «Afərin!», «Əla!», «Çox gözəl!», «Möhtəşəm!», «Mükəmməl!», «Bravo!», «Super!», «Çox yaxşı!», «Lap düz!», «Belə davam et!»), after the `solved` sound; a wrong tap by one of the retry phrases, at random («Bir də cəhd et!», «Yenidən yoxla!», «Tələsmə, yaxşı bax!», «Sən bacararsan!», «Narahat olma, bir də sına!», «Yox, bu deyil, bir də bax!»), after the `miss` sound, at most once in a round. Both lists are `voice.json`'s `praise` and `retry`; the owner chose them. The sound effects of "Sounds" above stay.
- **On the board.** `resources/js/game/voice.ts` (`VoiceCues`, checked by Vitest) decides what is said; `BoardScene` says it on the page's one `AudioContext` (`stage.ts`) through Phaser's sound manager, on one channel: the prompt 600 ms after the round's last item or tile starts to come in, and after the pack's `arrive` sound when that is still playing (the train), but not when it is the clip the round before had (so a `pop` game says its instruction once, and a voiced round after a silent one is said) nor for a round shown already solved or skipped; praise when the `solved` sound is over, waiting for a prompt still being said; the retry phrase when the `miss` sound is over, only into silence. A clip pauses with the board (`setPaused()`), and the 🔊 button beside the prompt (`board-prompt.tsx`, through `BoardBridge.repeatPrompt()`) says the prompt again at once. Praise, retry, the greeting and the end phrases load with the scene, a prompt as its round is shown. From V2 the channel says a **line**: a phrase and, when a name is due, the player's name before it, `NAME_GAP_MS` (150 ms) apart — «Abbas! … Afərin!», «Abbas! … Salam!», «Abbas! … Əla oynadın!» — the pause waiting on the scene clock as a clip does; whatever waits (the next prompt) waits for the whole line, so a name never cuts off a prompt, and 🔊 cuts the line with its name. The next round waits for the praise line of the round just solved: the scene tells the controller through the bridge (`BoardBridge.praise`, the scene → controller side) that a line is due for that round when it decides to say one, and that it is over once it has been said, dropped or cut (by 🔊); the controller shows the next round once the solved effect (1.6 s) has played and, with a line due, `PRAISE_GAP_MS` (300 ms) after the line is over, and never later than `SOLVED_CAP_MS` (6 s) after the solve, so a clip that never ends (autoplay refused, a decode error, a hidden tab) cannot stall a class. With no line due — silent mode, praise off, a skip — it is the 1.6 s alone. The lengths come from the decoded audio as it plays, never from a table; a long «Abbas! … Mükəmməl!» thus ends before the next round comes in, and its prompt is no longer queued behind the praise. A pause holds the next round until the game is live again; ending the game or another board taking it cancels the wait; after the last round the result follows the same wait, then the end line. The controller tells the scene who is at the board through the bridge: `startTurn(studentId, fresh)` on «Başla» or «Davam et» (`fresh` when no round is answered, which «Başla» shows) and on every change of the student during play — the board's students, «Növbəti», the phone, «Kim oynayır?» — and `gameOver(solved)` when the game ends under play on this board (the last round, «Bitir» or the phone; not for a game opened already over). The board freezes under the result at that moment and the voice goes on: a praise not yet begun gives way, the clip being said ends without the rest of its line, and the end line is said, its pause on the page's clock; when the clip being said is the name the end begins with, the end's phrase follows it, so the name is said once. A name's clip is fetched when its player's turn starts (a home game's child's with the scene) by `nameClip()` (`http.ts`, the board's own headers; a 404 is no clip, not a lost game) and decoded on the page's one `AudioContext` in `name-voice.ts` (`NameVoices`), kept in memory only and dropped with the stage; a name due before its clip has arrived is left out and the phrase is said alone, and a name waits for its phrase to have loaded, so it is never said alone. The teacher board, the guest board and the home board behave the same: the phrases are public content files, and a name's clip comes through the game, by ids.
- **An account can turn it off.** Settings → Oyun has «Tapşırığı səslə de» and «Tərif səsi», both on for a new account (`users.board_voice`, JSON, null for both on; `PUT /settings/play/voice`, `BoardVoiceData`, `UpdateBoardVoice`). The board's props carry `voice` (`Play\Support\BoardVoice`, from the game's owner): whether prompts are said, and the praise and retry URLs, or empty lists with praise off. With the prompt off there is no 🔊 either. V2 adds a third switch, «Adı səslə de» ("Names" below).
- **Names** (V2, ADR 016). V2 is built (2026-11-12): the list, the match, `content:voice`'s name clips, the route and the props below, and the board's half (`voice.ts`, `name-voice.ts`, the settings switch and the marks on the lists, ADR 016 (c)). The clips were made in the owner's paid month (ADR 016 (d), 2026-11-12/13): the 924 names of the first list (15,554 characters, 7.8 MB under `content/voice-names`) and the three phrases, the first 20 names heard first as a sample, which settled the order of name and phrase and the greeting's tone. A name added to the list later stays silent, its `name_audio` null and its phrase said alone, until `content:voice` makes its clip. The board says a student's or a child's first name only from **the built-in name list**, `content/names.json`: about 2,000 common Azerbaijani first names, girls' and boys', our own selection (MIT) seeded from Wikidata (CC0) and reviewed by the owner, each made into a clip «Nihat!» once, offline, by `content:voice` in the voice and settings above with `voice.json`'s `names.style` (`[excited] Nihat!`). The first list (2026-11-12) holds 924 names, 307 of them from the Wikidata seed and 617 added by hand; two further Wikidata queries of the Azerbaijani labels of people from Azerbaijan were used only to check spellings, and all three are its `sources`. The owner's review and contributors' pull requests grow it towards ADR 016's ~2,000 while the voice lasts. A name may say what a phrase says («Afərin»): it still gets its own clip under `content/voice-names`. The command reads the list, never a row, so no name from the database reaches the provider, and an instance needs no key.
    - **The match** (`Content\Support\NameVoices`): the display name's first name, as `Language\Support\PersonName::firstName()` reads it, must be one word on the list, compared with `Az::lower()` — «Əli M.» is said «Əli»; a name written with its surname or patronymic, either way round, is said by its first name («Nihat Əliyev», «Əliyev Nihat», «Nihat Rəşid oğlu» → «Nihat»; rows saved before the forms refused a surname, below in §7); a hyphenated or two-word first name is silent, with or without a surname («Ayşən Nur Əliyeva»): never half a name. A name typed without the Azerbaijani letters — on a keyboard or phone that lacks them, ə → e, ı → i, ö → o, ü → u, ş → s, ç → c, ğ → g (`Az::plain()`) — is said as the one listed name it can be («Lale» → «Lalə», «Huseyn» → «Hüseyn», «ILKIN» → «İlkin»; 602 of the 924 listed names hold such a letter); when two listed names are typed the same («Roya», «Röya»; three pairs in the first list) only the exact spelling is said. Never another near spelling: «Ali» is not «Əli» (a is not ə), «Nihad» not «Nihat». A name not on the list is silent and still shown; the phrase is said without it.
    - **What is said** (`VoiceCues`, `voice.test.ts`). The name alone, before a phrase, with a pause (`NAME_GAP_MS`), the order the owner chose by ear: before the praise of the 1st, 4th, 7th … solved round of a turn («Abbas! … Afərin!») (a turn is one player at the board; it starts when the board starts or reopens the game and when the student at the board changes; a pause does not end it), only for a chosen student or the home child, never for the whole class or on a wrong tap; before «Salam!» (said in the names' excited tone) when «Başla» starts a home game with no answered round, once, before the first prompt, which waits for it; before «Əla oynadın!» at the end of a game with a solved round, once — a home game's child, or a class game's student when every round the board saw solved was theirs; when the whole class or several students played, or the board saw no round solved (a reload), «Əla oynadınız!» with no name; after no solved round, nothing. Where there is no name to say, the phrase is said alone; a name is never said without its phrase. `greeting`, `finish` and `finish_class` are `voice.json` phrases with public clips. Never when «Kim oynayır?» picks a student, never in rankings, on the podium, the share card, the phone or the welcome page.
    - **How a board gets a name's clip.** A hash of a name can be computed from the public list and settings, so a name's clip never has a public URL: the clips live in `content/voice-names/{hash}.mp3`, outside `content/assets`, and a board fetches one only from `GET /games/{game}/players/{player}/voice` (`games.players.voice`, `PlayerVoiceController`, `Play\Support\PlayerVoice`), in the board's group (`auth:board,web`, `can:play,game`): `{player}` is an active student of the game's class or the game's child, through `GamePlayers`; anything else, a name without a clip, or names or praise switched off is a 404. The answer is `audio/mpeg` with `Cache-Control: no-store, private` and no header naming the file. The props carry `students[].name_audio` and `child.name_audio`, that URL as a path from the host's root (`/games/01J…/players/01J…/voice`, ids only) or null — off the list, no clip made, or names or praise off — and `voice` gains `names` (whether names are said: the name switch with praise on) and `greeting`, `finish`, `finish_class` (the phrases' public URLs, or null with praise off or before their clip is made); the board fetches a name's clip with its own client (a guest board's bearer in the header) and keeps it decoded in memory only.
    - **The switch.** «Adı səslə de», below «Tərif səsi», on for every account (`users.board_voice` without `names` reads as on; `BoardVoiceData::$names`, and `PUT /settings/play/voice` takes `names`, a request without it leaving names on); «Tərif səsi» governs praise, retry, greeting and end, so with it off no name is said (`BoardVoiceData::saysNames()`) and the page shows the name switch unticked and disabled, keeping its own choice for when praise is on again. The class page's student list and a child's card and page mark a name the board can say with a speaker icon, and say once that other names are only shown: `has_voice` on each active student row of `classes/show` and on each child of `children/index` and `children/show` (`PlayerVoice::says()`), true or false from the name and the list, null while the account says no name; derived on every request, never stored. A name the list spells otherwise (typed without the Azerbaijani letters, or in other casing) carries `voice_spelling` on the same student rows and on `children/show` (`PlayerVoice::spelling()`, `NameVoices::corrected()`): the display name with its first name spelled as listed («Lale M.» → «Lalə M.»), or null — spelled so already, more than a first name and an initial, or names off. The page offers it as a one-tap fix through the student's or the child's own update route; nothing is changed without that tap, since the board shows the name as stored to children learning to read.
    - **Nothing to delete.** A name clip is list content shared by every bearer of the name, not a child's data: deleting a student, a child or an account deletes no clip; a clip goes only when its name leaves the list (`--prune`).
- **A device can silence the board.** The board's «Səssiz» toggle (§4 "Board in the browser") mutes everything the board plays, voice and effects alike, names included, on that device only (`localStorage`), and hides the 🔊 meanwhile; the voice starts nothing new while it is on (`VoiceCues.mute()`), and a turn still counts its rounds. It changes nothing on the server.
- **Making the clips.** `php artisan content:voice` lists every clip content needs (`VoiceClips::needed()`), reading no database row, so no student's or child's name and no teacher word can reach the provider (from V2 the names it voices come from `content/names.json`, `--only=name`). `--dry-run` prints, per template, the clips needed, made and to make and the characters it would send (what the plan counts), and the credits entry it would add, and calls nothing; `--only=find.picture,praise` and `--limit=10` make a part (a sample to hear first); a real run asks first (`--force` does not) and needs the key `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`, read only as `config('elifba.voice.api_key')`, in the shell or the gitignored `.env` of the machine that makes the clips — never in `.env.example`, and never with `config:cache`. Each clip is one request (`Content\Support\ElevenLabs`, retried on 429 and 5xx), then trimmed of silence and levelled by ffmpeg to −16 LUFS with a limiter at −1.5 dBFS, mono MP3 at 64 kb/s without tags (`VoiceLeveler`). `GenerateVoiceClips` writes each file (a name's under `content/voice-names`, every other under `content/assets/voice`), writes the clip list even when a run stops half way, and with the first clip of each folder adds `voice.json`'s `credit` (or `names.credit`) to `credits.json` with the month of generation and writes `CREDITS.md`. A run ends with a page to listen to every clip on, outside the repository, the names in a section of their own (`WriteVoiceReview`); a bad clip is deleted (the next run makes it again) or given a respelling. `--prune` deletes the clips no prompt or listed name needs any more (a word left a pack, a name left the list, a setting changed); nothing else ever deletes one. `scripts/voice/README.md` has the steps. Tests fake the API (`Http::fake`) and ffmpeg (`Process::fake`).

**Why.** A child who cannot read needs to hear what to find, and the adult should not have to read every prompt. Everything Elifba says is known in advance (about nine hundred phrases), so it can be made once in one paid month, heard by the owner and shipped as files: no provider at play, no key on a self-hosted instance, nothing paid twice. A child's name is not known in advance and voicing it on demand would send it to a third party; common first names are known, so they are made once like the rest (ADR 016), and a name not on the list stays silent.

**Rejected.** Browser speech synthesis: school computers and phones usually have no Azerbaijani voice, and a voice of another language would teach wrong sounds. Synthesis at runtime, voiced teacher words, the provider's free plan, Azure AI Speech and the self-hosted models: ADR 015. A name made on demand, recorded by a parent, or served from a public URL by its hash: ADR 016.

### Ordinals and dates

Ordinals take vowel harmony (1-ci, 2-ci, 3-cü, 4-cü, 5-ci, 6-cı, 9-cu, 10-cu…). `Language\Support\Ordinal::make()` builds them from the last vowel of the last number word said aloud: 21 "iyirmi bir" → 21-ci, 40 "qırx" → 40-cı, 100 "yüz" → 100-cü, 110 "yüz on" → 110-cu, 1000 "min" → 1000-ci, 2500 "iki min beş yüz" → 2500-cü. Places start at 1. The server sends `place_label`, and TypeScript never concatenates a suffix: `Ordinal::label($place, $locale)` gives "1-ci", "2-ci", "3-cü" in the `az` interface and "1st", "2nd", "3rd", "11th", "21st" in `en`. Dates are formatted by the server with Carbon's month names in the interface language (`Language\Support\DateLabel`): "9 noyabr 2026" / "9 November 2026"; a range names each month and year once — "9–15 noyabr 2026", "26 oktyabr – 1 noyabr 2026", "28 dekabr 2026 – 3 yanvar 2027". TypeScript never formats a date or names a month.

## 4. Live play

### Devices

| Device                                                        | Page                                                                                               | Authenticated as                                                                | Writes                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Board** — the laptop/PC on the projector, or a touch screen | `/games/{game}/board` (teacher board) or `/lovhe` (guest board); full screen, no app shell, Phaser | the teacher's web session (teacher board) or a guest board bearer (guest board) | results: hits and skips (`POST /games/{game}/sync`)      |
| **Remote** — the teacher's phone                              | `/games/{game}/remote` (React, big buttons, no Phaser)                                             | the same teacher (web session, "remember me")                                   | nothing that counts; it sends client events to the board |

**At home** (R3, R4; ADR 011). Everything in this section is about the game's **owner**, and from R3 the owner is a teacher or a parent: where the text says "the teacher's session", "the teacher's phone" or "the teacher's games", read the account that owns the game. A parent's home game uses the same two board modes, the same codes, channel, client events and `/sync`. Three things differ, all on the surface:

- **Who is at the board** is the game's child, always. The board and the remote show the child's avatar and name instead of the student grid and «Növbəti»; `client-student` is never sent, and `RecordBoardEvents` ignores a `student_id` in a home game and credits the solving hit to the game's child (§1, §5).
- **The pairing card** does not open by itself on a home board: one device the child touches is the usual case. «Pultu qoş» brings it, and the board still joins the channel, so a phone that opens `/pult` finds the game. `users.phone_remote` off still means no channel at all.
- **`BoardProps`** carries `schoolClass` (null at home), `child` (`{id, display_name, avatar: {key, url}, stars}` for a home game, else null; `stars` is the child's total before this game, for the result screen) and `students` (empty at home). `PultChoices` offers a parent their children where it offers a teacher their classes, each with its own setup options; `GuestBoardGameData` takes `{game}`, `{class, …}` or `{child, …}`. A guest board's label for who claimed it names the account, not a role.

### Pairing

**Decision.** The phone is always logged in as the teacher. A 6-digit code (and its QR) only says which board; it grants nothing by itself. A board runs in one of two modes with the same protocol:

| Mode              | Where                                   | The board is authenticated as                | How a game gets there                                                                                                           |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Teacher board** | the teacher's own laptop                | the teacher's web session                    | the teacher starts the game on the laptop (`/classes/{class}/games/create`), then pairs the phone with the code the board shows |
| **Guest board**   | a shared school computer or smart board | a guest board bearer — never a teacher login | anyone opens `/lovhe`; the board shows a code; the teacher's phone claims it and sends a game to it                             |

**Codes.** `Play\Support\PairingCode` makes every code: 6 digits, unique across live games' join codes and open guest boards (one code space), valid for `elifba.pairing_code_ttl_minutes` (10). The board shows a QR (`qrcode.react`) of `{APP_URL}/pult/{code}` and the code in large type ("Telefonda {domain}/pult açın, kod: 482 913", where `{domain}` is the host of `APP_URL`). The phone opens `/pult/{code}` or types the code at `/pult` (`GET /pult?code=482 913` → `/pult/482913`; spaces and dashes are dropped, anything but 6 digits is a validation error on `code`); not logged in → login → back to the same URL. `/pult` also lists the teacher's live and paused games, newest first, so a logged-in phone can join without a code (with exactly one it opens its remote directly, unless the teacher turned the phone remote off — see "Teacher board" below; `/pult?list=1` keeps the list, so the code can still be typed). `/pult`, `GET /pult/{code}` and `POST /pult/{code}` share one limiter (`pult`, 10 a minute per teacher); an unknown, expired or malformed code, another teacher's game, a finished or abandoned game and a claimed or closed guest board all give the same 404, so a code reveals nothing.

No database constraint spans the two tables, so `PairingCode::issue()` skips a code that is valid in either and retries (up to 20 times) when the store hits a unique index — an expired code still sits in its column, and two requests may race. Each attempt writes in its own transaction (a savepoint inside an outer one), so a violation rolls back only that attempt. `PairingCode::resolve(code, teacher)` is deterministic when a code is found in both tables: the teacher's own live game with that join code first (→ its remote), then the open guest board (→ what to show on it).

**Teacher board.** `IssueJoinCode` puts a code on the game when the board shows its pairing card: `POST /games/{game}/join-code` on load and on «Pultu qoş» (teacher only). A code still valid is kept, so a QR already on the wall keeps working and a phone can scan it again; an expired one is replaced; it is cleared when the game ends, and an ended game gets 409 `{error: "game_over", game: {status, board_epoch}}`. The answer is `{pairing: {code, expires_at, url, entry_url}}` (`PairingData`): `url` is `{APP_URL}/pult/{code}` for the QR and `entry_url` `{APP_URL}/pult` for typing, both on `APP_URL`, not on the request's host, so `composer dev:lan` and a proxy give phones an address they can open. Issuing a code is not activity (`last_activity_at` stays). `/pult/{code}` finds a live game with that join code owned by the logged-in teacher and opens `/games/{game}/remote`. The board hides the pairing card once a remote says hello.

**Teacher board without the phone.** The phone remote is on by default; a teacher who plays on a touch screen or with the mouse turns it off in Settings → Oyun (`/settings/play`, `PUT /settings/play` → `UpdatePlaySettings` with `Play\Data\PlaySettingsData`: `phone_remote` required boolean → `users.phone_remote`). The teacher board's page props are `BoardProps` plus `phoneRemote` (the teacher's own value; `BoardProps` itself, the guest board's JSON and the remote's props do not carry it). With it off, the board never joins the game's channel (Echo is not created, as when Reverb is missing), never asks for a join code, and shows no pairing card and no «Pultu qoş»; the game is played by touching its items. It is a preference, not a permission: channel authorization, `PairingCode`, `IssueJoinCode` and the protocol are unchanged, and a board already open keeps what it loaded with. Guest boards always work with the phone. Since such a teacher's boards wait for no phone, `/pult` never opens a remote on its own for them: with one live game it still shows the list, so a game on a guest board is one tap away and the code field stays.

**Guest board.**

1. `/lovhe` (no login; the page itself writes nothing) posts `POST /guest-boards`, which calls `OpenGuestBoard`: a `guest_boards` row with a code and a random 32-byte secret. The answer, 201 `{board, token}`, is the only time the secret leaves the server: `token` is the bearer `{board_id}.{secret}`, kept only in the tab's `sessionStorage` (`elifba.guest-board`, as `{board_id, secret}`) and in memory, and sent only in the `Authorization` header — never in a URL, `localStorage`, a cookie, Inertia props, a log or an error; the row stores the secret's hash. A reload of the tab continues with the same board; a new tab starts with a new code. `POST /guest-boards` is throttled per IP (limiter `guest-boards`, 6 a minute); unclaimed rows expire with their code.
2. The teacher scans the QR. `GET /pult/{code}` finds the open guest board and asks what to show (page `pult/guest-board`, props from `Play\Support\PultChoices`: the code, the teacher's live games — each with its level as `{title, unit}` (the key as the title and a null unit for a level the content no longer has), so the list names the game as the setup does (docs/frontend.md rule 13) — their active classes, and per class the setup form's options and learned letters from `Play\Support\GameSetupOptions::for()` — the builder the setup page uses too): one of the teacher's live games or a new one (the same setup form, `components/games/game-setup-form.tsx`, with the class as a hidden field). `POST /pult/{code}` takes `{game}` or `{class, mechanic, pack, level, rounds?, letter_case?, letters?}` (`GuestBoardGameData::rules()`: the game must be the teacher's live or paused one, the class the teacher's active one — another teacher's id fails the same `exists` rule as an unknown one — then `GameSetupData::rules()` for a new game). `ClaimGuestBoard` locks the board, binds it to the teacher and clears the code; `ShowGameOnGuestBoard` attaches the game (starting it with `StartGame` when it is new), in the same transaction. A board claimed, closed or expired a moment ago is a 404. The phone opens `/games/{game}/remote`, whose props carry `guestBoard: {id} | null` (the active board of this teacher showing the game).
3. The guest board polls `GET /guest-boards/{board}` with `Authorization: Bearer {board_id}.{secret}` every 2 s while it waits (a code, or a game that is over), every 10 s while it shows a live or paused game, and at once (at most once a second) when one of its board requests answers 401 or 404 (its own limiter, `guest-board`, 120 a minute per board, apart from the opening limit). The answer is `{board: {id, expires_at, pairing: {code, expires_at, url, entry_url} | null, teacher: {name} | null, game: {id, status} | null}, props: BoardProps | null}`: a bearer cannot make an Inertia visit, so `props` carries exactly the teacher board's page props (`Play\Support\BoardProps`, one builder for both, and for the remote). When a game appears it loads it exactly like a teacher board (`ClaimBoard`, `/state`, `/sync`, `/status`, the channel), with the same bearer; the tab keeps the props of that game as first received, so a poll never rebuilds the board. The bearer reaches only the board's current game — never the teacher's classes, rankings or other games — and an expired, closed or wrong bearer is 401, which tells the tab to open a new board.
4. When a game ends, the board shows its result and «Növbəti oyun telefondan gözlənilir…»; the phone's finished screen offers «Növbəti oyunu lövhəyə göndər» (`GET /guest-boards/{board}/game`, the page of step 2 sending to the board instead of a code) and sends the next game (`PUT /guest-boards/{board}/game`, the same body as `POST /pult/{code}`, `ShowGameOnGuestBoard` again) without a new code; from then on the old game is a 404 for the bearer. Each game gets its own board in the tab (its own outbox key `elifba.board.{game}.outbox`, epoch and tab fencing); before the next game replaces the one on the wall, the old board sends what its outbox holds once (`BoardController::settle()`, at most 5 s). Since the bearer has already lost the old game by then, anything still unsent stays in the tab under that game's key and is sent first if that game comes back to the tab. The board closes after `elifba.guest_board_hours` (12) or when either side presses «Lövhəni bağla» (`DELETE /guest-boards/{board}`, `CloseGuestBoard`: 204 to the board's `fetch`, a redirect back to the phone's Inertia visit); the bearer stops working at once, the game goes on, and `PruneGuestBoards` deletes the row within the hour; nothing of it survives closing the tab. On the board, «Lövhəni bağla» (asked first) settles the outbox, closes, forgets the bearer and shows «Lövhə bağlandı» with «Yeni kod»; on the phone it sits in the remote's header (in place of the QR button, which a guest board does not need) and on its finished screen. A board closed from the phone learns it from its next poll (401) and opens a new board with a new code. A phone whose board was closed on the wall (or expired) gets a 404 from these routes; the remote then reloads its `guestBoard` prop instead of showing the error.
5. The claimed board shows the claiming teacher's name («Müəllim: …») and «Mənim deyil — yeni kod» on its start and result screens. The code is visible to the whole room, so another registered teacher could claim it first; they would only see their own game on it, and one tap undoes it: the board closes itself and opens a new one with a new code.

**Why.** A QR projected on the classroom wall can be scanned by anyone in the room; if the code were the credential, a parent's or child's phone could control the game, so authority comes from the teacher's login on the phone. Teachers need that login on the phone anyway to share the podium (§6). Many classrooms project from a shared school computer: the guest board keeps the teacher's password and session off it, and the computer keeps nothing once the tab is closed. The teacher board stays for teachers with their own laptop and for play without a phone.

**Rejected.** A code-as-credential remote (no login on the phone): anyone in the room could control the game. Guest boards only: a game without a phone would then still need a phone to start. A second token handed to the guest board after the claim: the secret the board already holds does the job, and it never leaves the tab.

### Channel and authorization

One private channel per game: `private-game.{gameId}` (Echo: `echo.private('game.' + id)`).

```php
// routes/channels.php — the teacher's session or a guest board's bearer
Broadcast::channel('game.{game}', static fn (User|GuestBoard $who, string $game): bool
    => GameChannel::allows($who, $game), ['guards' => ['board', 'web']]);

// Play\Support\GameChannel::allows(): the game exists, is live or paused,
// and Gate::forUser($who)->allows('play', $game) — its owner, or the active
// guest board that shows it (GameSessionPolicy::play).
```

The callback takes the id as a string and loads the game itself: a typed `GameSession $game` would go through the `{game}` route binding, which follows the default (`web`) guard, so during `/broadcasting/auth` a bearer would get 403.

The `board` guard (`Auth::viaRequest('board', new GuestBoardGuard)` in `AppServiceProvider`; `config/auth.php` names the guard) reads the bearer, finds the row, compares the secret's hash with `hash_equals`, refuses a closed or expired board (`isActive()`), and returns the `GuestBoard` (which implements `Authenticatable`, with no password and no "remember me"). The guest board's Echo instance sends the bearer to `/broadcasting/auth`.

**Guard order.** The board routes take `auth:board,web` and the channel `guards: ['board', 'web']`: a bearer sent with a request wins over a session cookie in the same browser. So a guest board's tab on a computer where some teacher is still logged in acts as the board — never with that teacher's wider reach, and still working when that teacher does not own the game. A request without a bearer is the teacher's, as before — and so is one whose bearer no longer works (a closed or expired board authenticates nothing, so the session decides: the board's game is then a 404 unless that teacher owns it); the guest board's own poll is `auth:board` only, answers 401 and makes the tab open a new board. `GuestBoardTest` ("lets the bearer win over a teacher logged in on the same computer…") and `ChannelAuthTest` ("refuses the bearer even when the owner is logged in on the same computer…") pin this.

**Bindings and policies.** On the board routes `Auth::user()` is a `GuestBoard` when the bearer authenticated. `{game}` (`GameSession::resolveRouteBinding`) then finds only the game the board shows (`GuestBoard::showsGame()`: active, claimed, `game_session_id`, and the same owner); a teacher finds their own games (`BelongsToOwner`); anything else is a 404 before controller code. `{board}` finds the board itself for its bearer, or the active boards the current teacher claimed. `Play\Support\Requester::current()` reads the authenticated user through the auth manager's resolver (what `Auth::user()` returns at run time; static analysis types `Auth::user()` by the default guard alone). `GameSessionPolicy` has `view` and `update` for the teacher alone (board page, join code, remote) and `play` — claim, state, sync, status — for the owner or the guest board showing the game; `GuestBoardPolicy` has `view` (poll: the board itself), `show` (next game: the claiming teacher) and `close` (either). The shared Inertia prop `auth.user` is a teacher or null, never a board.

**Status for the bearer.** `PATCH /games/{game}/status` is a board route like claim, state and sync: `board.ts` pauses, resumes and ends through it, and the remote's `client-control` reaches the server through the board, so a guest board needs it for the game it shows.

### How a hit travels

**Decision.** A tap on the phone is a Reverb **client event** (`whisper`) to the board. The board judges it locally, starts the effect in the same frame, acknowledges it to the phone, and queues it for the server. The **board is the only writer of results**: it posts its queue to `POST /games/{game}/sync`; the server judges again, stores and returns points.

```
phone            Reverb             board                          server (PHP)
 │ client-hit ──▶  │ ── client-hit ──▶ │ judge (engine.ts)              │
 │                 │                   │ burst starts (same frame)      │
 │ ◀── client-ack ─┼──────────────────│                                │
 │                 │                   │ outbox ── POST /sync ────────▶ │ RecordBoardEvents:
 │                 │                   │                                │ judge, store, points
 │                 │                   │ ◀── results (+3 ⭐) ───────────│
 │ ◀─ client-state ┼──────────────────│ next round                     │
```

**Why.** The visible path has no PHP, no database and no queue: one hop phone → Reverb, one hop Reverb → board. A direct touch on a touch screen takes the same path inside the board (source `board`), so there is exactly one place that writes results, whatever the input. The server still decides what counts, so a buggy or reloaded board cannot double-count.

**Rejected.** Phone → HTTP → server writes → broadcast → board: the visible path then contains a mobile HTTP request, a PHP request, a transaction and a broadcast hop — roughly twice the latency of client events, and a slow server would freeze the classroom. Two writers (phone and board both post): every hit would need cross-device de-duplication.

#### Latency budget (A7's 300 ms bar)

| Leg                                                                    | Budget   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| phone tap → WebSocket send                                             | < 20 ms  |
| phone → Reverb (school Wi-Fi or 4G, server within ~100 ms RTT of Baku) | ≤ 100 ms |
| Reverb fan-out                                                         | < 5 ms   |
| Reverb → board                                                         | ≤ 100 ms |
| board: judge + first frame of the effect                               | ≤ 20 ms  |

Measured on the phone: `ack_ms` = time from sending `client-hit` to receiving its `client-ack`. The board starts the effect before it sends the ack, so tap→effect ≤ `ack_ms`. Target: p95 `ack_ms` ≤ 300 ms; `?debug=1` on the remote page shows it (count, p50, p95 of the last 500 taps; a retried tap counts from its first send, so a lost first try shows as ≥ 1 s). The server's region is part of O1: a server more than ~100 ms RTT from Baku breaks the budget.

Measured in A7 on localhost (`composer dev`, wss through Herd's certificate, board and remote as two Chrome tabs on one laptop): p50 16 ms, p95 33 ms over 32 taps; with a guest board (A7.3, the board's channel authorized by its bearer): p50 8 ms, p95 23 ms over 15 taps, and p50 9 ms, p95 18 ms over 27 taps. That is the floor of the pipeline, not a classroom figure; a real phone on school Wi-Fi with a deployed server is still to be measured (O1).

#### Client events (protocol v1)

Every payload carries `v: 1`. Both sides parse every event with its zod schema from `contract.ts` (strict: an extra key, such as a name, fails it) and drop what is malformed without an answer; the board answers `stale` to a hit that does not match the current round, and to any hit while it is not playing (waiting for «Başla», paused).

| Event (wire name) | From → to      | Payload                                                                                          | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `client-hello`    | remote → board | `{v, device_id}`                                                                                 | a remote (re)joined; the board answers with `client-state`                                                                                                                                   |
| `client-hit`      | remote → board | `{v, event_id, round_id, item_id, client_at}`                                                    | the teacher tapped an item                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `client-student`  | remote → board | `{v, student_id \| null}`                                                                        | who is at the board (null = whole class, no points)                                                                                                                                          |
| `client-control`  | remote → board | `{v, action: pause \| resume \| skip \| end \| pairing}`                                         |                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `client-ping`     | remote → board | `{v, t}`                                                                                         | every 5 s                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `client-state`    | board → remote | `{v, board_epoch, status, round_id, round_index, student_id, hit_item_ids, misses, last_points}` | snapshot; sent on hello, after every change and every 5 s (`round_id` null: the board is not playing — waiting for «Başla», or over; `last_points`: what `/sync` gave the round solved last) |
| `client-ack`      | board → remote | `{v, event_id, status: correct \| miss \| duplicate \| stale}`                                   |                                                                                                                                                                                              |

Server broadcast on the same channel: `session.status` (`Play\Events\SessionStatusChanged`, `ShouldBroadcastNow`, no queue), payload `{v: 1, status: live | paused | finished | abandoned}` — the channel names the game — when a game is paused, resumed, finished or abandoned through HTTP (`/status`, a `/sync` that solves the last round) or by the scheduler. `BroadcastSessionStatus` sends it when `GameSessionTransitioned` is dispatched, after the commit; nothing is sent for a rolled-back change or a status asked for again. When Reverb is down or unreachable the change still succeeds: the listener catches the failure and reports `Play\Exceptions\SessionStatusNotBroadcast` (the game id, the status and the cause's class — no message, no previous exception), and the board learns the status on its next claim or sync.

Rules:

- **Duplicates.** The same `event_id` is applied once on the board and stored once on the server (unique `event_id`). A hit on the same item from the _other_ source within 800 ms of an accepted one (a child on a touch screen and the teacher on the phone together) is the same touch: the board acks it `duplicate` and does not queue it. In an `any` round a hit on an item that already burst is `stale`. In a `sequence` round tiles are reusable (§2), so a repeated item is judged like any other hit.
- **Retry.** The phone shows a tap as pending until its ack; after 1 s without one it sends the same event again (same `event_id`), once; without an ack 1 s after that, the tap shows "not delivered" and is dropped. The board remembers the last 256 `event_id`s with their ack and answers a repeated one with the same ack, without touching again.
- **Hello.** The remote says hello on every (re)subscription and again when a `client-state` arrives with a higher epoch than the last (a reloaded board, or one opened elsewhere, has not heard it yet). Hello hides the board's pairing card.
- **Fencing.** Each board load calls `ClaimBoard` (`POST /games/{game}/board/claim` → `{game: {status, board_epoch}}`), which increments `game_sessions.board_epoch` under the game row's lock and returns it. A finished or abandoned game is not written to: the claim returns its status and current epoch, and the board shows the result. `/sync` refuses an old epoch (409 `board_replaced`); that board stops and says the game was opened in another window. The phone ignores `client-state` with a lower epoch.
- **Who is at the board** is the board's state: the phone proposes it (`client-student`), the board attaches it to each hit, the server credits the round to the student of the hit that solved it. The phone offers a student grid (avatars, from the page props) and "next" (class order, skipping archived students).
- **Board without a phone.** Pointer events on items call the same `applyHit()` with source `board`; the pairing card can be closed. With no remote connected, the board picks who is at the board itself: React around the canvas shows the same student grid and "next" from the board page's props (`students`: id, display name, avatar `{key, url}`, active only, in class order), starting with the whole class (no one credited). The choice is board state in both cases, attached to each hit and kept in `sessionStorage` across a reload; when a remote says hello, its `client-student` replaces it. Everything else is identical.
- **«Kim oynayır?»** (owner, 2026-11-11). In a class game with at least one active student, «Başla» and «Davam et» first open a dialog with the same grid («Bütün sinif» and the students), the current choice marked: a tap on a choice sets it (`setStudent()`) and starts the game in that same tap, which is the user gesture full screen, the landscape lock and audio need; closing the dialog starts nothing. A home game and a class with no active student start at once, as before (`asksWhoPlays()`, `lib/game-players.ts`). The footer's picker stays for changes during play, the phone's `client-student` still replaces the choice, and `client-state.student_id` carries it to the phone: nothing new is stored or sent.

#### `POST /games/{game}/sync`

Body `{board_epoch, events: BoardEvent[]}` (1–50, in order; `BoardEventsData::rules()`). `Play\Actions\RecordBoardEvents`, in one transaction per event with the game row and then the round row locked (`lockForUpdate`; claims and status changes lock the game row too, so the order is always game → round):

1. an epoch that is not the game's → 409 `{error: "board_replaced", game: {status, board_epoch}}`; events before it in the batch stay stored;
2. an `event_id` already stored → `duplicate`, with the first result's `round_solved` and `points` (the hit that solved a round is the last correct answer of a solved round). Two requests with the same `event_id` at once: the game lock makes the second see the first's answer; where locks cannot meet (the same `event_id` in two games), the unique index refuses the second insert, the violation is caught and the event answers `duplicate`, without reporting anything of the other game;
3. `stale`, nothing written: a finished or abandoned game (`/sync` never writes to a game in a final status, and its answer's `game.status` tells the board), a `round_id` that is not a round of this game (a foreign or made-up id), a round already solved or skipped (for a hit), an item not in the round or already burst (the judge);
4. a `skip` → `skipped` and `skipped_at` set; a skip of a round already skipped → `duplicate` (skips are not stored by event id, so a repeated or doubled skip finds its round skipped); a skip of a solved round → `stale`;
5. a hit → judged with `RoundJudge` against its own round (the server does not require it to be the current one), the answer stored, `progress` / `misses` updated; the hit that solves the round sets `solved_at`, `student_id`, `points` (`PointsRule`), `played_on`, `week_start` (`SchoolCalendar`) and dispatches `RoundSolved` after commit.

Ids in the body are untrusted. A `student_id` that is not an active student of the game's class (another teacher's, another class's, archived, unknown) is stored as null: the hit counts, no one is credited. In a home game (R3) every `student_id` is stored as null and the round is credited to the game's child through `game_rounds.child_id`, which `StartGame` set. Only a malformed body (422: a missing key, a type outside its list, an `event_id` that is not a ULID, more than 50 events) or an old epoch fails the request; every other problem is the result of its own event, because a 422 for the whole batch would block the board's outbox for ever. A **paused** game still takes events: the board stops input while paused, so what arrives was made before the pause reached it. When no open round remains, the game transitions to `finished` inside the same transaction. Every applied event (correct, miss, skipped) touches `last_activity_at`. Answer `{results: AnswerResult[], game: {status, board_epoch}}` (`SyncResponse`), one result per event in order. Throttled per game at 240 **events** a minute (`App\Http\Middleware\ThrottleBoardEvents` counts the events of each body, so bigger batches do not get around it): over the limit, 429 `{error: "too_many_events"}` with `Retry-After`, and the board keeps its outbox.

The other board endpoints: `GET /games/{game}/state` answers `GameState` (`Play\Support\BoardState`: every round snapshot in order and where each stands; no student names); `PATCH /games/{game}/status` takes `{status: live | paused | finished}` (`StatusChangeData`; `abandoned` is the scheduler's alone) and answers `{game: {status, board_epoch}}`, or 409 `{error: "invalid_transition", game}` for a change the lifecycle does not allow.

**Round count** (owner, 2026-11-13). The number of rounds is chosen on the board, under «Başla», not in the setup form: one button per multiple of 5 from `elifba.rounds.min` to `.max` (`BoardProps.roundLimits`, which a guest board's poll carries too; the game's own count is added when it is none of them, `lib/round-steps.ts`), the game's count chosen. It is offered while the board waits for «Başla» and nothing is answered — no round solved, skipped, hit or missed (`board.ts`'s `answered()`, which also turns «Başla» into «Davam et») — and disappears once anything is. A tap sends `PATCH /games/{game}/rounds` with `{board_epoch, rounds}` (`RoundCountData`; a board route like `/status`: the owner's session or the bearer of the guest board showing the game, `play`; `throttle:board-rounds`, 30 a minute per game). `ChangeRoundCount` locks the game row (the lock `/sync`, claims and status changes take) and refuses an old epoch (409 `board_replaced`, the board stops) and a game that has ended or begun — progress, misses, points, a solved or skipped round, or any stored answer — (409 `{error: "rounds_locked", game}`); otherwise it validates the game's setup with the new count (`GameSetupData::inputOf()` and `rules()` for the same `GamePlayers`: 422 with the reason when the learned letters or hidden words no longer allow it), builds every round again with `StartGame::build()` — the pack as the owner plays it now (its version stored), the learned letters and the same seed — and replaces the round rows in the same transaction. The answer is the new `GameState`; the rounds have new ids, the epoch stays. `BoardController.changeRounds()` shows the new rounds and the start screen's «:count raund» from them (a guest board keeps its first props); «Başla» waits while the server builds; a 409 `rounds_locked` for a game that ended shows its result, a failure leaves the rounds as they were with a notice (`rounds_failed`). The phone needs nothing new: it reads `/state` again when the board names a round it does not know.

## 5. Scoring and rankings

**Decision.**

- **Points belong to a solved round**, credited to the student at the board when the round was solved: **3** with no wrong hit, **2** with one, **1** with more (a `pop` round has no wrong item, so it always gives 3). Never negative; nothing for a skipped round. A round solved with nobody at the board (whole class) stores its points but credits no one. The rule lives only in `Play\Support\PointsRule`.
- **Windows.** `config('elifba.timezone')` = `Asia/Baku` (UTC+4, no daylight saving). When a round is solved the server stores `played_on` = the Baku date of `solved_at` and `week_start` = the Monday of that date (ISO week). Rankings filter on these two columns only — no time-zone arithmetic in SQL.
- **Daily ranking** of a class for a date: the sum of `points` per active student over rounds with that `played_on`. **Weekly**: the same over `week_start`. `Rankings\Support\ClassRanking` computes it; nothing is stored.
- **Ties share a place, dense** (10, 10, 8, 7 → 1, 1, 2, 3; `Rankings\Support\DenseRank`). Within a place, names are listed in Azerbaijani alphabetical order (display only). No tie-breaker by time.
- **Who is ranked.** Students with more than 0 points get a place; active students with 0 are listed after them without a place ("hələ xal yoxdur"), in Azerbaijani alphabetical order; archived and deleted students never appear, and neither do rounds the whole class solved.
- **How it is computed.** `ClassRanking::for($class, PeriodData)` (or `day()` / `week()` with a date) runs one `GROUP BY student_id` with `SUM(points)` over the class's rounds with `played_on` = the day, or `week_start` = its Monday, and `student_id` not null. The sum is cast to `int` (engines return it as an int or a numeric string). It is merged in PHP with the class's active students and returned as `Rankings\Data\RankedStudentData` rows (student, points, place). A page receives per student only `id`, `display_name`, `avatar`, `points`, `place` and `place_label`.
- **Which ranking.** `Rankings\Data\PeriodData` reads `?period=day|week&date=YYYY-MM-DD` with its `rules()` (A10's podium reuses them). Both keys are optional: the day (`Rankings\Enums\Period`) and `SchoolCalendar::today()`. Any date selects its Monday–Sunday week. An unknown period, a malformed or impossible date, or a date after today in Baku is invalid. A GET page never answers with a validation error: it redirects to the same query without the invalid key (`PeriodData::valid()`), so its default applies and the URL always says what the page shows. The server also sends a date in the window before and in the window after (null while the window holds today); the page only puts them in links.
- **Labels.** The server formats the window in the interface language — "9 noyabr 2026", the week "9–15 noyabr 2026", "26 oktyabr – 1 noyabr 2026", "28 dekabr 2026 – 3 yanvar 2027" — and the place labels ("1-ci" / "1st"), §3.
- **The page.** `/classes/{class}/rankings` (`RankingController`, page `rankings/show`), linked from the class page: day / week, the window before and after, back to today. It opens for an archived class too, as the class page does; it only reads.
- **Podium** = places 1–3 (each may hold several tied students); empty when nobody has points.
- **Who sees what.** The full ranking (every student with points) is shown only on the teacher's own pages, on the laptop or the phone. The board never shows it: at the end of a game it shows the stars the class earned in that game and today's podium (places 1–3). The podium page is the one meant for the projector and the share card. No page labels a last place.
- The owner confirmed this scoring and visibility on 2026-11-09.

**A child's stars** (R3, R4; ADR 011; owner, 2026-11-11). At home there is no ranking, no place and no podium — only stars, and only the child's own.

- **A star is a point.** A home round gives 3, 2 or 1 by the same `PointsRule`, always to the game's child; nothing is stored but the round's `points`, `played_on` and `week_start`, as for a class.
- **Derived.** `Rankings\Support\ChildStars::of($child)` sums `game_rounds.points` over the child's rounds (`child_id`): `today` (`played_on` = `SchoolCalendar::today()`), `week` (`week_start` = its Monday), `total`, and `days` — the last seven Baku days with their stars and server-made labels — as `Rankings\Data\ChildStarsData`. `ChildStars::today($parent)` gives today's stars of all of a parent's children in one grouped query. Sums are cast to `int`, as in `ClassRanking`.
- **What the parent sees.** On `/children` each child's card shows today's stars, in the parent's own order (`position`), never sorted by stars and never with a total beside another child's. On `/children/{child}`: today, this week, in all, and the seven days. No place, no "best", no comparison with a sibling or with anyone.
- **What the child sees.** On the board's result screen: the stars of this game and the child's total (`BoardProps.child.stars` plus what `/sync` returned), with the child's avatar.
- **In all** means the rounds still stored: games older than `elifba.retention_days` are pruned (§7), and the page says so.
- **Never** for a child: `ClassRanking`, `Podium`, the share card. Their queries filter on `school_class_id`, which a home round does not have, and no route under `/children` leads to them.

**Why.** Points on the round row give one indexed `GROUP BY` per ranking, keep history exact even if the rule or the time zone setting later changes, and need no ledger to keep in sync. Small, never-negative points reward finishing, not speed — the teacher, not the child, controls the order of turns, so speed and order are not fair tie-breakers. Dense ranking lets more children reach the podium, which is the point of a podium for seven-year-olds.

A sibling ranking would turn the home into the classroom's contest between a three-year-old and a nine-year-old, which no rule could make fair; a child's own stars reward the child for playing.

**Rejected.** A stored star counter on the child (a second write, against "derived things are never stored"). A separate points ledger table: a second write to keep consistent for no query that needs it. Computing Baku dates in SQL (`AT TIME ZONE`): differs between SQLite and PostgreSQL. Competition ranking (1, 1, 3): leaves second place empty after a tie. Time bonuses: punish slower readers.

## 6. Share card

**Decision.** The podium page (`/classes/{class}/podium?period=day|week&date=YYYY-MM-DD`) renders a hidden 1080×1920 React node and turns it into a PNG in the browser with `modern-screenshot` (`domToBlob`, after `document.fonts.ready`). The PNG is made as soon as the page opens and shown as the preview, so «Paylaş» can call the Web Share API immediately inside the tap:

1. `navigator.canShare({ files: [file] })` → `navigator.share({ files: [file] })` — on Android and iOS this opens the share sheet, where WhatsApp offers "My status". No `text` is passed (some WhatsApp versions drop the file when text is present).
2. Otherwise (desktop, old browsers): download through an object URL, file name `elifba-{gunun|heftenin}-qalibleri-{date}.png` — never a name in the file name.

**Content** (and nothing else):

- title — "Günün qalibləri" or "Həftənin qalibləri" (in the teacher's interface language);
- the date or the week range, formatted by the server ("9 noyabr 2026", "9–15 noyabr 2026"; `PeriodData::label()`, §5);
- places 1–3: avatar, `display_name`, place label from the server (1-ci, 2-ci, 3-cü), points;
- a festive decoration from CC0 or own art (confetti from `canvas-confetti` on the live page; static shapes on the card);
- the Elifba wordmark and the domain.

Layout: content inside the WhatsApp status safe area (nothing important in the top 250 px or the bottom 300 px); fonts and pictures are same-origin files, so the capture never needs a third-party request.

**As built (A10).**

- **Server.** `Rankings\Support\Podium` takes places 1–3 from `ClassRanking` (no query of its own) as `Rankings\Data\PodiumPlaceData` (place, points, the tied students in Azerbaijani order). `PodiumController` (page `podium`, `routes/rankings.php`) reads the query as `RankingController` does and sends the podium for the teacher's page (ids, names, avatar keys) and, apart from it, `card` from `Rankings\Data\ShareCardData`: `title`, `label`, `places[] {place, place_label, points_label, students[] {display_name, avatar_url}}`, `wordmark` (`app.name`), `domain` (the host of `app.url`), `file_name` — or null when nobody has points. Every text is final ("6 xal", "1st"; plurals by `Language\Support\Plural`, the server twin of `useTranslation()`'s `{one}|{other}`). `avatar_url` is a path from the root (`/content/avatars/…`, `ContentAssets::path()`), so the picture loads from the page's own origin even when the server saw another host or scheme (a tunnel, a proxy); null draws a circle with the initial. The file name's date is the day, or the week's Monday. `PodiumPageTest` pins the card's exact recursive key set and that no id, class or teacher name, link, time or student off the podium is in it.
- **Browser** (`components/share`). `ShareCard` receives only `card` and draws it in one fixed light look (Andika, the medal colours of `place-colors.ts`, `CardDecoration`'s static shapes in the covered bands and margins). `SharePanel` renders it off-screen (`position: fixed` far left, never `display: none`, which leaves nothing to measure or capture), and on open, and again for another day or week, `makeShareImage` awaits `document.fonts.load()` of Andika 400/700 (it fails rather than capture a fallback face) and every `<img>`'s `decode()`, fits the places (`fitShareCard`: the first of nine densities, from one big row per child to six columns, that fits as it is; when none does — long names that wrap, a very large tie — the density whose names stay largest once scaled down; names are never clamped, so a tied class and 40-character names are always whole and inside the safe area), then `domToBlob` at 1080×1920, scale 1. The PNG becomes the preview's object URL, revoked when it is replaced or the page closes; the download reuses it. «Paylaş» calls `navigator.share({ files })` synchronously in the tap when `canShare({ files })` holds; closing the sheet (`AbortError`) is a cancel, any other refusal downloads. Without file sharing the button says «Şəkli yüklə» and downloads.
- **Live page.** `canvas-confetti` once per visit when the podium is not empty, with `disableForReducedMotion`. An empty podium shows an empty state, no card and no button. Linked from the class page and from the rankings page (same period and date).

**Why.** Rendering in the browser needs no image service, no queue and no server CPU, and the fonts and avatars are already on the page. The Web Share API with files is the only way a web page can hand an image to WhatsApp status.

**Rejected.** Server-side rendering (headless Chrome / Browsershot, or GD/Imagick): a browser binary in the production image, or hand-drawn layout with poor text shaping. Sharing a link instead of an image: the status would show a link preview, and the page would have to be public — a child's name behind a public URL.

### Agreements

R2, ADR 013. An account agrees to three separate things, each at the moment it applies, and each moment is kept:

| When                                                      | The sentence (az / en)                                                                                                        | Stored in                 |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Registration (the form, or the Google completion page)    | «[Məxfilik qeydini] oxumuşam.» / "I have read the [privacy note]."                                                            | `users.terms_accepted_at` |
| Taking the teacher role (`/role`, or Settings → «Rollar») | «Elifba-dan məktəbimin icazəsi ilə istifadə edirəm.» / "I use Elifba with my school's permission."                            | `users.teacher_agreed_at` |
| Taking the parent role (the same pages)                   | «Əlavə etdiyim uşaqların valideyni və ya qanuni nümayəndəsiyəm.» / "I am the parent or legal guardian of the children I add." | `users.parent_agreed_at`  |

`CreateNewUser` and `CreateUserFromGoogle` keep requiring `terms`; only its text changes. `GrantRole` refuses a role whose sentence is not accepted (`RoleChoiceData`: `agreement` accepted) and stamps the column with `forceFill`, as registration does. **Why.** A teacher acts for a school and a parent for their own child: one sentence cannot be true for both, and an account that takes its second role months later must accept that role's sentence then.

### Google sign-in

Teachers sign in in two ways: an e-mail address and a password (Fortify, with optional two-factor authentication), or Google. Every account has a password or a linked Google account: registration requires a password, Google sign-up stores `google_id`, linking clears the password only while it sets `google_id`, and a teacher without a password cannot change the address (which unlinks Google). The starter kit's passkeys were removed on 2026-11-10, as a third button made the sign-in page harder to read; `2026_11_15_000001_drop_passkeys_table` drops their table, and no account lost its only way in.

ADR 008. Off unless `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` and `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` are both set (`config/services.php` `google`, read by `App\Support\GoogleSignIn::enabled()`): the routes in `routes/auth.php` always exist, so Wayfinder generates them everywhere, but `EnsureGoogleSignInIsEnabled` answers 404 and the pages hide the button. Laravel Socialite's Google driver runs on the server only; the button is a link to our own route with an inline G mark (§8).

| Route                                         | Who     | What                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /auth/google/redirect`                   | guest   | to Google with `openid email profile`, `prompt=select_account` and PKCE; Socialite keeps the OAuth `state` in the session |
| `GET /auth/google/confirm`                    | teacher | the same, to confirm a teacher who is linked to Google and has no password                                                |
| `GET /auth/google/callback`                   | either  | the answer; the redirect URI is `services.google.redirect` (`/auth/google/callback`) on the host the teacher used         |
| `GET`, `POST`, `DELETE /auth/google/register` | guest   | the completion page of a new teacher: show, create the account, cancel                                                    |

All are throttled (`google`: 30 a minute per IP). The callback works only with the intent the redirect left in the session (`google.intent`, pulled once), and with Socialite's `state`:

1. **Sign in.** Google must vouch for the address (`GoogleProfile::vouchesForAddress()`): verified (`email_verified` is JSON `true`) and either Gmail or a Google Workspace account (`hd`). Any other address Google verified once, when the Google account was made, and it may have changed hands since (Google's own guidance), so it is refused. The teacher is found by `google_id`, else by e-mail (case-insensitive, an exact match first; several matches are refused) and linked by `App\Actions\Fortify\LinkGoogleAccount`. An account already linked to another Google account is refused, and so is an account whose teacher changed to this address and has not confirmed it (`email_changed_at`): it is theirs, whoever holds the address. An account whose registration address was never confirmed may have been registered by someone else with this address, so linking marks it confirmed and drops every credential set before: password (null), two-factor secret and recovery codes, the remember token, and the account's other sessions (rows of the `database` session driver). Nothing else from Google is stored: no name, picture or token.
2. **Log in.** A teacher with confirmed two-factor authentication gets Fortify's challenge (`login.id`, `login.remember` = false), never a session. Everyone else is logged in without "remember me", with a regenerated session, and sent to the intended page or `/classes` (from R2: `/start`, which lands by role, §1 "Accounts and roles").
3. **A new teacher** has the profile (`id`, `email`, `name`, `email_verified`, and when it expires) kept in the session (`google.pending`), honoured for 10 minutes and forgotten when the account is created or the teacher cancels (otherwise it ends with the server-side session), and sees `auth/google-register`: the address (read only), the name from Google (editable) and the privacy-note checkbox. `App\Actions\Fortify\CreateUserFromGoogle` validates name, address (unique), `google_id` (unique) and `terms`, and creates the teacher with `terms_accepted_at`, `email_verified_at`, `locale` and `google_id`, without a password. From R2 the account has no role yet, like one made by the form: `role.chosen` sends it to `/role` next, and the page's tick is the privacy note alone ("Agreements" above). Without a pending profile the page sends the teacher to `/login`; so does an address or Google account taken between the check and the insert, caught as a refusal so that the database error, which holds the name and address, is never logged.
4. **Confirm.** Only a teacher linked to Google who has no password confirms with Google (`GoogleSignIn::canConfirm()`); with a password, the password stays the proof, since a Google session left open on a school computer must not stand in for it. With the `confirm` intent the callback marks the password confirmed (`auth.password_confirmed_at`) only when the Google account is the one linked to the logged-in teacher, and returns to the intended page or the profile.

**Changing the address** in the profile marks it unconfirmed (as the starter kit does), stamps `email_changed_at` and unlinks Google (`google_id` = null): the Google account vouched for the old address only. A teacher without a password sets one first; the profile refuses the new address until then.

**Teachers without a password.** The password login fails for them with the usual "credentials do not match". Fortify's confirm page (title «Hesabınızı təsdiqləyin») offers «Google ilə təsdiqlə»; it has no password field. On the security page they set a password without a current one, and deleting the account asks for a fresh confirmation (within `auth.password_timeout`) instead of the password; both check it on the server. A password reset mail still works for them.

**Logs** carry account ids and exception class names, never an e-mail address or a name from Google.

### Pasting a list

The teacher pastes a list of names (one per line) and sees a preview before anything is stored. `Classroom\Support\NameListParser` builds it; it writes nothing, and the pasted text (which holds surnames) is returned only to the teacher who pasted it, never stored or logged. The preview is `POST /classes/{class}/students/preview` answered with JSON — a validation error included — so the text is never in a URL and no redirect flashes it into the session (`text`, `names` and `display_name` are in the exception handler's `dontFlash`); the parser marks it `#[SensitiveParameter]` so a stack trace shows none of it; the page keeps it only in component state. Only the confirmed display names are posted to `AddStudents`.

**A display name** (`Classroom\Support\DisplayName`, the rule in `StudentData` and `AddStudentsData`) is words of the 32 letters separated by single spaces or hyphens, optionally ending in a surname initial with a dot ("Aysel M."), at most 40 characters, checked after `Az::normalize()`. Foreign names are written in the Azerbaijani alphabet ("Vilyam"), as Azerbaijani spelling does. It keeps no surname: a name with a sure surname beside the first name (score 2 below) or a patronymic — «Nihat Əliyev», «Əliyeva Aysel», «Nihat Rəşid oğlu» — is refused with its own message (`students.errors.surname`, a child's `children.errors.surname`), which names no one; a first name of two words («Məhəmməd Əli») and a lone first name that ends like a surname («Xosrov») pass (`Language\Support\PersonName::hasSurname()`, since 2026-11-13). Rows saved before keep their name until edited, and the board's voice reads the first name from them (§3 "Voice").

**Per line**, in this order:

1. **Columns.** A line with tabs (pasted from Excel or Word) keeps only the cells that contain letters and no digits, so number, date and ID columns fall away and "Məmmədov ⇥ Əli ⇥ Rəşid oğlu" becomes one name.
2. **Normalize** (`Az::normalize()`, §3), then **strip numbering and bullets** at the start: `1.`, `1)`, `(1)`, `№1`, `#1`, `1 -`, `-`, `–`, `•`, `*` and similar.
3. **Skip** a line with no letter left (blank, numbering only, a separator). It is not reported.
4. **Words.** A word with a digit is dropped (a date, an ID); punctuation around a word is trimmed; apostrophes of the old spelling are removed ("Mə'sumə" → "Məsumə"). A word with another character inside, or a letter outside the alphabet ("William", Cyrillic), makes the line **invalid**. A one-letter word is an initial.
5. **Patronymic.** One patronymic is removed: a father's name followed by `oğlu` / `qızı` (also typed `oglu`, `qizi`, `ogly`, `kızı`, `kyzy`), or one word ending in `-ovna`, `-evna`, `-oviç`, `-eviç`, `-ovich`, `-evich`. Whether it ended the line is remembered.
6. **Order.** With two or more names left, the first and the last are scored as surnames (`Language\Support\PersonName::surnameScore()`, shared with the display name rule and the board's voice): **2** for `-ov`, `-ova`, `-ev`, `-eva` (so `-yev`, `-yeva`), `-oğlu` / `-oglu` (at least 5 letters); **1** for `-zadə` / `-zade`, and for `-lı`, `-li`, `-lu`, `-lü` after a consonant (at least 6 letters: "Kərimli", but not "Əli", "Vəli", "Nazlı", "Məmmədəli"); **0** otherwise. The higher score is the surname. On a tie a patronymic decides (at the end of the line → surname first, as registers write "Məmmədov Əli Rəşid oğlu"; in the middle → first name first). Lines still undecided follow the majority of the decided lines of the same paste, and first name first when there is none.
7. **First name** = the second name when read surname first, otherwise the first; a third name is ignored. **Surname** = the other end, or a lone initial ("Aysel M."). A line with no name left, or only a surname (score 2) with initials, is **invalid**; so is a first name longer than 37 characters (no room for an initial).
8. **Case.** The first name is title-cased (`Az::title()`): "QULİYEV İLKİN" → "İlkin".

**Across the list**, against the class's **active** students:

- **Duplicate**: the same full name (case-insensitive, after the steps above) as an earlier line. It carries the earlier line's suggestion and is not added.
- **Surname initial**: added ("Aysel M.", "Aysel H.") only when another distinct pasted line or an active student has the same first name, compared with `Az::lower()`; never otherwise.
- **Taken**: the suggestion (case-insensitively) equals another line's suggestion or an active student's name — two "Aysel M.", or a pasted "Əli" without a surname when the class has an Əli. The teacher edits it.

**Output**, one `Classroom\Data\PastedNameData` per reported line: `line` (1-based in the pasted text), `text` (the line as understood), `display_name` (the suggestion; null when invalid) and `flags` (`Classroom\Enums\PastedNameFlag`, in this order: `invalid`, `duplicate`, `surname_first`, `initial_added`, `in_class` (an active student has this first name — it may be the same child), `taken`). `addable` is true unless the line is `invalid`, `duplicate` or `taken`. The teacher edits and confirms; `AddStudents` stores only the confirmed display names and checks them again. Limits: a paste of at most 10,000 characters (`NameListData`), at most 50 names per `AddStudents` call (`AddStudentsData::MAX_NAMES`).

**Why.** Teachers copy names from the school register (surname first, with a patronymic), from Excel or from a chat. Suffix scores read both orders without a dictionary of names; the majority rule settles the lines suffixes cannot. Keeping only the first name is the minimum-data rule; the initial appears only where a class needs it.

**Rejected.** A dictionary of Azerbaijani first names: a data set to maintain and license, still wrong for rare names. Storing the full name and showing the first: the surname would be stored. Asking the teacher to pick the order for the whole list: one more step, and lists are often mixed.

- **National symbols.** Only cultural symbols and the State Flag are used — never the State Emblem or the anthem. The flag is drawn to the official proportions and colours (three equal horizontal bands blue, red, green; white crescent and eight-pointed star in the red band; 1:2) and is never burst, distorted, cut or shown upside down. `symbols/bayraq.svg` follows the flag's construction sheet for the law of 5 February 1991 (as published on Wikimedia Commons; with h the flag height: the crescent's outer circle 3/10 h across, its inner circle 1/4 h across and 1/30 h to the right, the star 1/6 h across with an inner circle of 1/12 h, crescent and star spanning 2/5 h, centred) and the colours of the 2013 Rules for the Use of the State Flag (item 9.2: Pantone 306 C `#00B5E2`, Red 032 C `#EF3340`, 362 C `#509E2F`, also the `symbols` palette); on ADR 014's 32 grid the field is 28 × 14 at (2, 9), every measure scaled from h = 14, in flat fills with no gradient, highlight or blur; its frame (`#33293D`, the darkest neutral of ADR 014's palette) lies outside the field and the crescent and star are not outlined, so nothing changes their proportions. The file's comment names these sources. The `symbols` pack therefore uses the gentle `sparkle` effect for every item, and its hit sound is the chime, not the pop.
- **Elifba's mark.** The logo is our own art under MIT: a balloon from the `find` game with «ə» cut out, drawn only as SVG paths (no `<text>`, no font) so it looks the same at 16 px and 512 px. The masters are `resources/images/brand/mark.svg` (one colour, `currentColor`) and `tile.svg` (white on the logo's green `#15803D`); `AppLogoIcon` draws `mark.svg`'s paths, `public/favicon.svg` is `tile.svg`, `public/images/og.png` shows the tile, and the app icons (`public/icons/`, `public/apple-touch-icon.png`, §10 "Installing Elifba") are drawn from `tile.svg` by `scripts/app-icons/generate.php`. It is not a content asset: it lives outside `content/assets/`, so it has no entry in `content/credits.json` (which lists only `content/assets/` and the fonts), and it is the one mark on the share card (§6).
- **Brand marks.** The «G» on the Google sign-in button (§7 "Google sign-in") is Google's own brand mark, drawn unchanged in Google's colours as an inline SVG in `resources/js/components/google-sign-in.tsx`, only to label that button, as Google's sign-in branding guidelines ask. It is not an asset: not under `content/`, not in `content/credits.json`, never on the board or the share card, and loaded from no Google server.
- **The GitHub mark** on the welcome page's link to the source code (frontend.md rule 10) is GitHub's own mark, drawn unchanged in one colour as an inline SVG in `resources/js/components/github-link.tsx`. It only labels that link, which goes to `elifba.source_url`. Like the «G», it is not an asset, never appears on the board or the share card, and nothing on the page loads from GitHub: no `buttons.js`, badge, iframe or star count.
- Nothing licensed NC (non-commercial), ND (no derivatives) or with an unknown license enters the repository. Downloads are unpacked outside the repository and only the files used are copied in.

**Why.** CC0 assets carry no obligations anywhere, which matters for an image that leaves the app. Keeping BY-SA files in their own folder makes the boundary visible to contributors. One picture style per pack keeps a round readable: a child compares words, not drawing styles. Fluent's Color style was the owner's choice on a side-by-side sample sheet for its light and depth on a projector, with an MIT licence that asks nothing of the screen (ADR 014). Andika was designed for early literacy (clear a, g, I/l) and covers Ə ə Ğ ğ İ ı.

**Rejected.** An emoji set for avatars: every share card would need a licence notice. Kenney's object packs for word pictures: they cover few of the words, and mixing them with the emoji set would put two looks in one pack. Keeping OpenMoji, Fluent Flat, Twemoji, Noto 2D and Fluent 3D for the word pictures: ADR 014. Hot-linking assets from CDNs: a third-party request from a children's app, and broken boards when the CDN is blocked by a school network. OGG sounds: older Safari versions, still on school iPads and Macs, do not play them.

## 9. Internationalization

**Decision.**

- `APP_LOCALE=az`, `APP_FALLBACK_LOCALE=en`. Locales: `az` (default) and `en`, listed in `config/elifba.php` (`locales`).
- Interface strings live in `lang/az.json` and `lang/en.json`, keyed by dotted keys (`classes.create.title`) and shared with React as in the precedent (`App\Support\Translations`, a `translations` prop, `useTranslation()`); plurals use `{one}…|{other}…` (Azerbaijani keeps the noun singular after numbers, so its two forms are usually equal).
- Framework messages (validation, auth, passwords, pagination) are PHP files `lang/{az,en}/*.php`, copied once from `laravel-lang/lang` (MIT) and committed; the package is not a dependency.
- **Parity test** (`tests/Unit/TranslationsTest.php`, reused from the precedent with `az` as the base): every JSON file has exactly the keys of `az.json`, no empty values, identical `:placeholders`; the PHP files have the same keys in both languages; the offered locales equal the shipped files.
- **Locale resolution** (`SetLocale` middleware): the teacher's `users.locale` → the session's choice (switcher in the footer for guests) → `az`. Not `Accept-Language`: the product is Azerbaijani first. Carbon's locale follows.
- **Content is not interface.** Words, sentences and prompts are Azerbaijani in every locale (§3); pack and level titles carry their own `az`/`en` and are checked by `ContentValidator`, not by the parity test.
- Names are sorted by `Language\Support\AzCollator` (the 32-letter order) on the server; lists reach React already sorted.
- **Roles and the parent area** (R2–R4) are interface: the role names («Müəllim», «Valideyn»), the three agreement sentences (§7 "Agreements"), `/role`, Settings → «Rollar», the area switch and the parent pages get keys in both files (`roles.*`, `children.*`, `nav.children`; the agreements under `auth.register.terms`, `roles.agreement.teacher`, `roles.agreement.parent`). The role's value (`teacher`, `parent`) is never shown; the page translates it. Where an existing string says «müəllim» for what is now any account (the guest board's «Müəllim: …», the Google paragraph of `/privacy`), it is reworded.
- **The picture and number games** (R5, R6) follow "content is not interface": the prompts («pişik» hansıdır?, Neçə «alma» var?), the words and the digits are Azerbaijani in every locale; the titles of the new levels and packs carry their own `az`/`en` in the content. The setup form's group names («Şəkilli oyunlar», «Oxu oyunları») and the mechanic names («De və vur», «Say») are interface keys. Digits are written 1–10 in both locales.

**Why.** It is the owner's working pattern from the precedent, with az as the base so a missing Azerbaijani string fails CI first.

**Rejected.** PHP array files for all interface strings: harder to share with React. i18next or another client library: a second source of truth; the precedent's hook already does the job.

## 10. Hosting

**Decision.**

**Development.** Laravel Herd serves `elifba.test` (PHP 8.4; `herd secure` for HTTPS); `composer dev` runs Vite, Reverb (`php artisan reverb:start`, which `laravel/reverb` registers in `php artisan dev` itself) and the log tail. `composer setup` runs `reverb:install`, which appends a random `REVERB_APP_ID`, `REVERB_APP_KEY` and `REVERB_APP_SECRET` to `.env` (`.env.example` leaves them out on purpose: the command only adds keys that are missing). Until then the `reverb` broadcaster cannot be built, and `routes/channels.php` builds it on every boot, so `composer setup` runs `key:generate` and `reverb:install` with `BROADCAST_CONNECTION=null` (`@putenv`) and clears it before `migrate`. Database: SQLite (`database/database.sqlite`). Testing with a real phone needs the laptop's LAN address for both the app and Reverb — phones cannot resolve `elifba.test` — so `composer dev:lan` (`scripts/dev-lan.php`) finds the LAN IPv4 (or takes it as `composer dev:lan -- 192.168.1.20` or from `ELIFBA_LAN_IP`) and runs `artisan serve --no-reload` on `0.0.0.0:8000`, Reverb on `0.0.0.0:8080`, Vite (listening on every interface, HMR from the LAN host: `ELIFBA_LAN_HOST` in `vite.config.ts`) and the log tail under `concurrently`, with `APP_URL`, `REVERB_HOST` and `VITE_REVERB_HOST` set to the LAN address in those processes only. `.env` is never edited: Laravel's `.env` loader does not overwrite variables already set, and `--no-reload` keeps `artisan serve` from dropping them. Phones open `http://{ip}:8000/pult`. In LAN mode Vite does not look for a certificate (`detectTls: false`), Reverb is plain `ws` on the port `.env` gives it (`REVERB_SERVER_PORT`, else `REVERB_PORT`, else 8080), and nothing else changes.

**Local HTTPS** (A10: the Web Share API and `canShare({ files })` exist only in a secure context). `herd secure elifba` makes `https://elifba.test` (plain http redirects to it) and a certificate for that hostname. `.env` then has `APP_URL=https://elifba.test`, `REVERB_HOST=elifba.test`, `REVERB_SCHEME=https` and `REVERB_CA_BUNDLE` = Herd's CA (`~/Library/Application Support/Herd/config/valet/CA/LaravelValetCASelfSigned.pem`), with `VITE_REVERB_*` following `REVERB_*` (`.env.example` has the recipe). Nothing else changes in how the stack starts: `composer dev`'s `reverb:start` takes its hostname from `REVERB_HOST`, finds Herd's certificate for it and serves `wss`; the Vite plugin finds the same certificate and serves https on `elifba.test`; the app trusts Herd's CA when it broadcasts (`client_options.verify` in `config/broadcasting.php`, PHP's own bundle does not hold it); the allowed origin is still the host of `APP_URL`. A machine where another app already holds port 8080 sets `REVERB_SERVER_PORT` and `REVERB_PORT` to a free port. Phones test the share card on the HTTPS URL of `herd share`, with `composer dev` running: Expose finds the Vite dev server and tunnels it too (it rewrites `public/hot` while it shares), and rewrites only `Location` headers, never a page. URLs the server writes absolute (`asset()`, e.g. the page's own avatars) still name `elifba.test` there unless the app trusts the tunnel's `X-Forwarded-*` headers; the share card's pictures are paths from the root, so the card is unaffected.

**Production** (`docker-compose.yml`, `Dockerfile`, A11): one image built from the repository on `serversideup/php` (`8.4-fpm-nginx-bookworm`, a pinned `v5.x` tag; nginx + PHP-FPM, nginx on port 8080) with `intl` added (`pdo_pgsql` is in the image), the app without dev packages, and the assets built in a stage with Node 22 (`vp build` runs Wayfinder, which calls `php artisan wayfinder:generate`, so that stage has PHP, `vendor/` and Node; only `public/build` leaves it). `.dockerignore` is the only filter (the folder need not be a git repository): `.env`, `node_modules`, `vendor`, `public/{build,hot,content,storage}`, the dev fonts manifest, the generated Wayfinder files, `storage` and `bootstrap/cache` never enter the image. Only `app` runs the image's Laravel automations (`AUTORUN_ENABLED=true`): `storage:link`, `migrate --force` and `optimize`, which includes `content:cache` (§3). `reverb` and `scheduler` run with them off and start once `app` is healthy. Every image tag is pinned; Dependabot watches `Dockerfile` (`docker`) and `docker-compose.yml` (`docker-compose`).

| Service     | Image                      | Runs                                                                                                                                                          | Health check                                   |
| ----------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| `proxy`     | `caddy:2.x-alpine`         | `docker/caddy/Caddyfile`: the site; WebSockets on `/app/*` → `reverb:8080`; `/apps`, `/apps/*` → 404; everything else → `app:8080`; the only published ports  | Caddy's admin API on `127.0.0.1:2019` (inside) |
| `app`       | the image (`elifba:local`) | nginx + PHP-FPM; the automations on start                                                                                                                     | the image's own: `/up` (`HEALTHCHECK_PATH`)    |

| `db`        | `postgres:17.x`            | PostgreSQL 17, volume `db-data`; no published port                                                                                                            | `pg_isready`                                   |
| `backup`    | `postgres:17.x`            | `docker/backup/elifba-backup.sh nightly`: `pg_dump --format=custom` at `BACKUP_AT` (03:00) Baku time into volume `backups`, `BACKUP_KEEP_DAYS` (14) days kept | `pg_isready` against `db`                      |

The workers set `stop_signal: SIGTERM` (the image's `SIGQUIT` is for PHP-FPM). `docker compose exec backup elifba-backup now` makes a dump at once; the script's header has the `pg_restore` line.

**Configuration.** One `.env` in the repository's root, copied from `.env.production.example`, is read twice: by Compose for its `${…}` values and as the PHP containers' `env_file`. `scripts/docker-env.sh <domain> [https|http] [port]` writes it with fresh secrets from `openssl` (`APP_KEY`, `REVERB_APP_ID`/`KEY`/`SECRET`, `DB_PASSWORD`; no PHP on the host) and never overwrites one. The address is `ELIFBA_DOMAIN`, `ELIFBA_SCHEME` and `ELIFBA_PORT`: Caddy's site address is `{scheme}://{domain}:{port}`, `APP_URL` is the same, Reverb's allowed origin is its host, and the browser's socket uses all three (`VITE_REVERB_HOST/PORT/SCHEME`, with `VITE_REVERB_APP_KEY` = `REVERB_APP_KEY`). Those `VITE_*` are build arguments, baked into the assets (`socket.ts` is unchanged), so changing them needs `docker compose up -d --build`; the build fails when the key or host is empty, and Compose refuses to start without the domain, port, scheme or secrets. With `https` (port 443) Caddy gets and renews a Let's Encrypt certificate itself and redirects `ELIFBA_REDIRECT_PORT` (80) to it. With `http` it is a try-out without certificates, which leaves port 80 alone (`docker-env.sh` puts the unused redirect port on `ELIFBA_PORT` + 1): `localhost` on this machine, or the machine's LAN address so phones on the school Wi-Fi can open `/pult` (no Web Share there, §6). What follows from the services is set in `docker-compose.yml` and wins over `.env`: `APP_ENV=production`, `LOG_CHANNEL=stderr`, `DB_*` → `db`, `REVERB_HOST=reverb`, `REVERB_PORT=8080`, `REVERB_SCHEME=http`, `QUEUE_CONNECTION=sync`, database sessions and cache. Mail is `log` (the mails land in `docker compose logs app`; the log transport writes at `debug`, so the example keeps `LOG_LEVEL=debug`) until the operator sets the SMTP lines, which teachers need to confirm their address — or Google sign-in, below, whose verified address confirms it.

**Google sign-in** (optional, ADR 008, §7). The operator creates an OAuth client of type "Web application" in Google Cloud Console with the authorised redirect URI `{scheme}://{domain}:{port}/auth/google/callback` (no `:{port}` for 443 or 80) for every address teachers open (no JavaScript origin: no Google script runs on our pages), and puts its id and secret into `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` and `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` (empty in `.env.example` and `.env.production.example`: off). The redirect URI is built from the request's own host, so it follows `X-Forwarded-Host` behind Caddy. Google accepts plain `http` only for `localhost` and refuses raw IP addresses and non-public domains, so the `http` try-out on a LAN address, `composer dev:lan` and `elifba.test` cannot use it; to try it locally, run `php artisan serve` and register `http://localhost:8000/auth/google/callback`. The consent screen asks only for `openid`, `email` and `profile`. Changing the two values needs `docker compose up -d` (the config is cached at start), not a new build.

**Behind the proxy.** The app sees plain http from Caddy. `bootstrap/app.php` trusts `X-Forwarded-For`, `-Host`, `-Port` and `-Proto` from the proxies in `config/trustedproxy.php` (`TRUSTED_PROXIES`), which is empty by default, so Herd, `composer dev`, `composer dev:lan` and `herd share` behave as before; `docker-compose.yml` sets the private ranges, since Caddy is the only peer that reaches `app` and it replaces any `X-Forwarded-*` a browser sends. Then `asset()`, redirects and the session cookie follow the browser's scheme: `SESSION_SECURE_COOKIE` stays unset, and Laravel marks the session and XSRF cookies `Secure` on https requests. nginx in the image keeps `REMOTE_ADDR` as the peer (`TRUSTED_PROXY=off`; the image's default `cloudflare` would take it from a `CF-Connecting-IP` header that any browser can send through Caddy).

The browser reaches Reverb through the proxy (`VITE_REVERB_HOST` = the public domain, port 443, `https`); the `app` and `scheduler` containers broadcast to `reverb:8080` on the internal network (`REVERB_HOST=reverb`, `REVERB_SCHEME=http`), so Reverb's HTTP API is never public.

**Smoke test.** `scripts/docker-smoke.sh` (the `docker` job in CI, §11) writes an http `.env` for `localhost:8088` when there is none, runs `docker compose up -d --build --wait` and checks: every service healthy; `/up` and the login page's built assets through Caddy, with no Vite dev server; a WebSocket handshake to `/app/{key}` from the app's origin (101); `/apps/*` answering 404 from outside while `app` reaches Reverb inside; the scheduler's four tasks; a backup dump that `pg_restore --list` reads; a row surviving `down` and `up`.

No queue worker and no Redis in v1: broadcasts are `ShouldBroadcastNow`, mail is sent synchronously, the share card is made in the browser. `QUEUE_CONNECTION=sync` in every environment, so anything a package queues still runs instead of waiting for a worker that does not exist. The starter kit's `composer dev` entry for `queue:listen` is removed (A2, `DevCommands::except('queue')`); Reverb is in `composer dev` since A7. Add a `queue` service the day something needs one; add Redis only when Reverb has to scale across servers.

**Installing Elifba.** A teacher can install Elifba from Chrome as an app with its own window and icon (desktop, Android). Nothing in it is offline: it is the same site in a window.

- **Manifest.** `GET /manifest.webmanifest` (`ManifestController`, `App\Support\WebApp::manifest()`): `id` `/` (the app keeps its identity if the start page moves), `name` and `short_name` `app.name`, `description` `welcome.meta.description`, `lang` APP_LOCALE (az), `start_url` `/start` (where an account lands by role, §1 "Accounts and roles"; a guest is sent on to the login), `scope` `/`, `display` `standalone`, `theme_color` the logo's green `#15803D`, `background_color` `#FFFFFF` (the light theme's, behind the icon while the app starts), `categories` `education`, and the icons. Chrome fetches the manifest without cookies, so its text is in APP_LOCALE and the route runs outside the `web` middleware group: no session (one per fetch would only fill the sessions table), no cookie, no CSRF. It answers `application/manifest+json` with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=86400` and an ETag.
- **Head.** Every page (`app.blade.php`) links the manifest, `apple-touch-icon.png`, `<meta name="theme-color">` (the same green) and `apple-mobile-web-app-title` (the app's name, not the page title, under the icon on an iPhone), and carries the inline script that catches Chrome's install offer for the banner (frontend.md rule 11).
- **Icons.** `public/icons/elifba-192.png` and `elifba-512.png` (`purpose: any`: `tile.svg` as it is, the rounded tile), `public/icons/elifba-maskable-512.png` (`maskable`: full-bleed green, the mark at 80 % so a launcher's circle never cuts it) and `public/apple-touch-icon.png` (180×180, full-bleed: iOS rounds it, and asks for this path even when a page names none). `php scripts/app-icons/generate.php` draws them from `resources/images/brand/tile.svg` with headless Chrome (no dependencies; `CHROME` names the binary); run it again whenever the mark changes. `WebAppTest` checks the head, the manifest and that every icon is a PNG of the size it states.
- **No service worker.** Chrome no longer asks for one: installing from the menu stopped needing a service worker with a `fetch` handler in Chrome 108 (Android) and 112 (desktop), Chromium's installability check (`InstallableParams`) has no service-worker criterion any more, and Chrome 157 fires `beforeinstallprompt` for Elifba with the manifest alone (checked 2026-11-10 over the DevTools protocol: `Page.getInstallabilityErrors` empty). So Elifba registers none: nothing sits between the page and the network, nothing is cached on a shared school computer, a deploy is never hidden behind a stale copy, and Vite's HMR is untouched. If one is ever needed (offline play, push), it is hand-written (no Workbox, no CDN), network-only unless this section says otherwise, and never caches HTML, Inertia JSON or anything with class or student data (§7); that change starts here.
- Safari and Firefox have no `beforeinstallprompt`; their own menus add Elifba (Safari's Add to Home Screen or Add to Dock reads the manifest and the touch icon).

**Database.** PostgreSQL 17 in production; SQLite in development and tests. Queries stay on the query builder with no engine-specific SQL (dates are computed in PHP, §5; names are sorted in PHP, §9). CI runs the Pest suite on both (§11). A self-hosting school may run SQLite in production — the same tests cover it.

**Why.** One domain for the app and Reverb removes CORS and mixed-content problems and lets phones reach the socket through port 443. PostgreSQL is the safer engine for a public instance with many classrooms writing at once (row locks, no single-writer file lock, `pg_dump`, a path to more servers). SQLite keeps `composer setup` free of services for contributors and makes tests fast.

**Rejected.** SQLite in production for the public instance: a single writer and a single host; moving data off it later is the hard migration this avoids. MySQL: no advantage here over PostgreSQL. A managed platform (Forge/Vapor/Cloud): the repository must stay self-hostable with plain `docker compose up`.

### Rules the `CLAUDE.md` will give later agents (A2 writes it)

- **Read first:** `docs/architecture.md` (this contract), `docs/glossary.md` (names), `docs/frontend.md` (UI rules). Change the contract first when the model changes.
- **Domains** `Language → Content → Classroom → Play → Rankings`; a domain uses only the ones before it. (`Family` joins before `Play` in R3; `CLAUDE.md` is the living copy of these rules and carries the lines R2–R6 change.)
- **Only Actions write.** Controllers, commands and channel callbacks validate, call one action and shape the response. Validation lives in `*Data::rules()` and is reused by every entry point.
- **Statuses** change only through `TransitionGameSession` (`GameSessionStatus::allowedTransitions()`); derived things (the current round, rankings, places) are never stored.
- **Facts are events after commit** (`GameSessionTransitioned`, `RoundSolved`); listeners never run inside the writing transaction.
- **The board is the only writer of results.** The phone never posts hits; it sends client events. Every result goes through `RecordBoardEvents`, idempotent by `event_id` and fenced by `board_epoch`.
- **Two board modes, one protocol.** A teacher board uses the teacher's session; a guest board uses its bearer, which reaches only the game it shows — never classes, rankings or other games. Codes come only from `PairingCode` and never authorize anything.
- **One judge, one points rule.** `RoundJudge` (mirrored by `engine.ts` against the shared fixtures) and `PointsRule` (PHP only). The round contract changes in the PHP DTOs, the zod schemas in `contract.ts` and the fixtures together; TypeScript types are inferred from the schemas, never written by hand.
- **Content is data.** Never hard-code words, letters, prompts or theme looks in PHP or TypeScript; add them to `content/` and run the validator. Text is cased, syllabified and ordinal-suffixed on the server (`Language`), never in TypeScript. Teachers' own and hidden words are rows (`custom_words`, `hidden_words`); Play reads a pack only through `TeacherPacks` wherever it builds or checks rounds, and a word rule lives once, in `WordRules`.
- **Children's privacy.** A student is a display name and an avatar. Do not add fields about children. Never put a student's name in a log, an exception, a broadcast or a URL, nor a teacher word in a log, an exception or a URL. New routes with `{class}`, `{student}` or `{game}` go into the isolation test. Nothing on the share card but §6's list.
- **No third-party requests** from pages: no CDNs, external fonts, analytics or trackers.
- **Assets** enter only with an entry in `content/credits.json` and an allowed license; Fluent Emoji files stay in `content/assets/fluent/` and never on the share card; state symbols follow §8.
- **Two languages.** Every interface string goes into `lang/az.json` and `lang/en.json`; the parity test fails otherwise. Content stays Azerbaijani.
- **Database portability.** Query builder only; no engine-specific SQL; Baku dates come from `SchoolCalendar`. Tests must pass on SQLite and PostgreSQL.
- **Tests** use Pest; fake time with `travelTo` around midnight and Monday boundaries; never call a real Reverb server in a test: tests run the `reverb` broadcaster with test keys and `tests/Support/FakeReverb` (`$this->reverb->sent`, `->down()`) in place of its HTTP client; or fake the event.
- **Gate:** run `composer ci:check` before calling work done.
